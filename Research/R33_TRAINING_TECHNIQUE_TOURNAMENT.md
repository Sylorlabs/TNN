# R33 training-technique tournament

Status: prospective, after sensory/telemetry and initial capacity/preservation
work. The trainer is human; an automated curriculum aid is an attributed tool.

## Controlled comparisons

Freeze the learner architecture, parent state, target competency, allowable
experiences and actions. Compare random experience, balanced diverse experience,
human demonstrations, contrasts/counterexamples, prompts, questions-only
teaching, learner-requested observations, learner-selected rehearsal, attributed
sibling information, and mixtures. Start with single-factor contrasts rather
than a large confounded recipe tournament.

Report separate equal-exposure, equal-compute and equal-human-effort views.
Adaptive teaching may select from an allowed training pool but cannot see sealed
assessment outcomes or hidden evaluator categories. Log every item offered,
selected, refused or replayed and why the selecting mechanism could know it.
An automated teacher's extra model/data/tool budget is part of the treatment.

## Evidence design

Predefine changed-example, changed-context and delayed-retention probes per
competency. Track learning curves against unique experiences, presentations,
elapsed compute and human interventions. Compare transfer from prior learning
with a branch in which the relevant abstraction is unavailable, without deleting
canonical knowledge or relabeling a newborn reset as a continuation.

A plateau requires enough dose to distinguish continuing improvement from
stagnation. Test dose before claiming architectural failure, and verify the
relevant information reached the learner first. Extra demonstrations are not
proof of learner independence; remove the aid for qualification.

## Gates and failure reports

Use [withdrawal criteria](R33_HAND_HOLDING_WITHDRAWAL_PLAN.md), mistake taxonomy,
retrieval outcomes, internal deltas, independent competence, forgetting and
interference from the telemetry contract. Preserve learner requests for help,
including justified uncertainty rather than treating every request as failure.

Falsify a technique with memorized repeats but failed transfer, a benefit that
vanishes after assistance withdrawal, increased teacher effort at equal outcome,
damage to prior competencies, or hidden curriculum/evaluator leakage. Do not
conflate failure of one teacher with failure of the learner architecture.

A winning technique must survive fresh populations and changes in human/aid
behavior. It becomes a trainer option, not a fixed universal curriculum imposed
by the architecture team. Freeze a successor preregistration before any run.
