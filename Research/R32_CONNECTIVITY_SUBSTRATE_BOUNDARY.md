# R32 Connectivity Substrate Boundary — Introspectable Self-Modifying Cognition

Date: 2026-08-30
Status: `RESEARCH BOUNDARY — NO SUBSTRATE PRESELECTED`
Canonical: R27 step 60,423

## Design target

TNN is not defined as a graph. The target is a machine cognition substrate whose active internal connections and mutable routing parameters are machine-visible to TNN itself and can be modified online under delayed grounded utility, regret, resource cost, and causal provenance.

This is an engineering operationalization of global connection introspection and self-modification. It is not evidence by itself of subjective consciousness.

## Why graph cognition is not assumed

Prior graph-like research can improve focus/selectivity while creating switching friction, lock-in, and poorer regime adaptation. Therefore focus alone is not a success criterion. Any substrate that becomes more selective but cannot rapidly revise, reverse, or reroute itself fails the intended intelligence target.

Explicit graph topology is one contestant, not the default winner.

## Candidate substrate families

The next connectivity experiment, only after E53 policy stabilization is qualified, should compare matched implementations of:

1. **Fixed topology + plastic weights** — connectivity is fixed; strength/sign/gating are learner-owned.
2. **Dense/recurrent weighted field** — broad recurrent interaction with learner-owned weights and gates, without explicit edge objects as the cognitive primitive.
3. **Learned routing/gating matrix** — modules/representations remain broad while TNN learns which routes become active by context.
4. **Sparse dynamic connectivity** — learner can create, remove, strengthen, weaken, gate, and reroute explicit links.
5. **Graph-like adaptive topology** — explicit nodes/edges with learner-owned structural mutation.
6. **Hybrid substrate** — stable weighted substrate plus reversible sparse dynamic overlays for rapid specialization and switching.

The protected runtime may provide generic storage/allocation/tracing primitives. It may not choose task-specific topology for TNN.

## Introspection contract

Whatever representation wins, TNN must be able to inspect the mutable cognitive relationships that actually affect its decisions. For each realized relationship or equivalent routing parameter, the substrate should expose machine-readable state sufficient to recover:

- source and destination/routing coordinates or equivalent influence domain;
- sign and current strength;
- activation/gating state;
- learned utility contribution estimate;
- causal/provenance trace;
- uncertainty/support mass;
- age and modification history;
- context specialization;
- resource/compute cost;
- rollback/consolidation state.

A representation may compress many equivalent links into shared factors, programs, templates, or low-rank structures, provided TNN can still inspect and modify the causal influence represented by that compression.

## Learner-owned modification actions

Permitted generic structural/parametric actions include:

- GROW / instantiate influence;
- STRENGTHEN;
- WEAKEN;
- change sign when learned evidence warrants it;
- GATE / ungate;
- REROUTE;
- SPLIT;
- MERGE;
- PRUNE;
- CONSOLIDATE;
- DECONSOLIDATE / rollback.

The learner chooses these from its own state and delayed consequences. No evaluator mode, answer label, task-family identifier, or handcrafted semantic rule may select them.

## Reversibility before consolidation

Fast online self-modification must not become irreversible lock-in. New structural changes should normally begin as reversible shadow/plastic changes. Consolidation requires repeated delayed evidence that the change improves grounded utility after resource/interference cost. Later regret must be able to weaken, reroute, split, or remove previously useful structure.

This is the key test against the historical failure mode where stronger focus came with worse switching.

## Required comparison metrics

All candidate substrates must be scored on the same worlds and utility contract. Required metrics include:

- grounded task utility;
- known-correct and known-wrong commitments;
- no-unique safety;
- focus/selectivity;
- context-switch latency;
- policy/topology lock-in;
- reversal/restoration success;
- adaptation after regime change;
- catastrophic interference;
- transfer to new contexts;
- delayed-credit quality;
- connection/parameter churn;
- compute per correct decision;
- memory per retained capability;
- resource shadow price;
- introspection completeness;
- deterministic replay/provenance.

A focus gain does **not** count as improvement when switching latency, interference, safety, or net utility regresses.

## Scaling rule

Do not equate intelligence with allocating a dense explicit graph. Potential connectivity may be enormous while realized computation remains sparse or compressed. Candidate representations may use factors, shared routing programs, reversible overlays, local recurrent fields, templates, or sparse materialized links when these preserve introspection and learner control.

The system should pay for realized memory, execution, and interference. TNN may learn that some domains benefit from sparse explicit structure while others benefit from distributed weights or hybrid routing.

## Promotion boundary

No connectivity substrate is promotable merely because it is more expressive or biologically suggestive. It must beat matched alternatives on net utility, safety, switching/adaptation, transfer, compute, and memory while preserving the accepted R27 capability contract.

R27 remains the immutable canonical control until that evidence exists.
