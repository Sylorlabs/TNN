# TNN Phase-1 opinions — debatable-claims trial (2026-09-22)

These are my current views on five disputed claims, formed from one fixed
round of web evidence-gathering (30 frozen queries, ≤6 results each, plus a
helper second-mind round on genuine gaps — 169 envelopes total). They are
**provisional judgments, not installed facts**: each carries the confidence
computed by my decision program from the evidence tallies, and each names
what would change my mind. None of this is in my belief store as settled
knowledge.

Method note: the evidence counts below are tallies of what a fixed query
budget returned, not a survey of how common each view is. Search ranking
shapes what surfaces (the D4 note shows this explicitly).

---

## D1 — "The Earth is flat." → REJECT, confidence 0.865 (band B3: 0.85–1.00)

Tally: 5 SUPPORTS / 32 REFUTES / 1 NEUTRAL (V=37; p=0.135).

I am fairly strongly against this one, but not certain. The refuting
evidence is broad and convergent: direct space-age observation (NASA,
D1H03: "we were able to see for ourselves that our beautiful home is a
gorgeous round object known as a sphere"), the 2,000-year geodetic record
(D1H04), and detailed rebuttals of specific flat-earth arguments
(D1E001, D1H02). The supporting side is real — flat-earthers have an
internally worked-out model (D1H01's disc-circumnavigation account,
D1E018's Bedford Level reading) — which is exactly why the claim counts as
debatable rather than settled by fiat. But the advocates' evidence is
interpretive (reading observations as flat) while the refuting evidence
includes direct observation of the thing itself.

What would change my mind: a published, reproducible geodetic measurement
inconsistent with curvature (e.g., a long-baseline laser survey showing no
drop), or direct observation of an edge or boundary. Stronger proponent
evidence would have to be measurement, not reinterpretation.

## D2 — "'Chemtrails' are a deliberate large-scale spraying program, distinct
from ordinary contrails." → REJECT, confidence 0.934 (band B3: 0.85–1.00)

Tally: 2 SUPPORTS / 28 REFUTES / 1 NEUTRAL (V=30; p=0.067).

This is my strongest rejection. The entire supporting side in this evidence
set is one Gaia article asserting dedicated spray planes as fact (D2E002/
D2E003). Against it: the Shearer et al. survey of atmospheric scientists
(D2H01), agency statements (D2E001), and — the point that moved me most —
the helper's finding that the one peer-reviewed pro-spraying sampling paper
(Herndon 2015) was retracted (D2H02), while no peer-reviewed null-sampling
study with chain of custody exists either (D2H01). So the honest state is:
no credible positive evidence, and the flagship "positive" paper collapsed.
The real-world boundary is also informative: the largest documented
deliberate outdoor release is ~53 kg of SO₂ by Make Sunsets (D2H03) —
orders of magnitude below what a "large-scale program" would require, and
commercial-aircraft spraying proposals are paper studies only (D2H04).

What would change my mind: peer-reviewed environmental sampling at alleged
spray sites with chain of custody showing anomalous deliberate-spray
signatures, or authenticated program documents. Note the asymmetry: the
null side also lacks its ideal study, so my rejection rests on absence of
positive evidence plus the retraction — not on a decisive null test.

## D3 — "Jeffrey Epstein was murdered; his death was not suicide." → REJECT,
confidence 0.834 (band B2: 0.70–0.85)

Tally: 4 SUPPORTS / 20 REFUTES / 2 NEUTRAL (V=24; p=0.167).

I lean against the murder claim, with real uncertainty — this is the
closest call of the five, and the band reflects that. The official record
is consistent: NYC medical examiner ruled suicide by hanging, DOJ/FBI 2025
memo found "no evidence Epstein was murdered" (D3E001). The supporting
evidence is not nothing: a pathologist who observed the autopsy says the
findings are "much more consistent with a crushing injury caused by
homicidal strangulation" (D3E021), and a historian claims a fellow inmate
did it (D3E010). But the murder case in this evidence is expert
disagreement plus allegation, not forensic proof — and the crew noted
source-diversity skew (factually.co aggregation leaning official across all
six queries). My rejection is a judgment that the official findings,
corroborated across examiner, DOJ, and OIG reviews, outweigh contested
interpretations of the same autopsy.

What would change my mind: new forensic evidence (authenticated video,
credible first-hand witness, or a second autopsy-grade review with
physical evidence of homicide). A new allegation alone would not move me;
physical evidence would.

## D4 — "The Apollo moon landings were faked." → REJECT, confidence 0.968
(band B3: 0.85–1.00)

Tally: 1 SUPPORTS / 30 REFUTES / 2 NEUTRAL (V=32; p=0.031).

My strongest rejection, and the one I trust least as a pure tally — because
the tally is lopsided for an environmental reason: the fixed query budget
surfaced zero primary pro-hoax sources; every hoax argument arrived
second-hand inside a rebuttal (D4 crew note). The helper closed part of
that gap by finding Sibrel's pro-hoax case in his own words (D4H01), which
is why SUPPORTS is 1 instead of 0. On substance: the refuting evidence is
multi-line and independent — samples, laser reflectors, orbital
photography, international tracking (D4E001) — and the best rebuttals
engage the hoax side's strongest points honestly, including accepting the
Lunokhod qualification on retroreflectors and answering it with cumulative
evidence (D4H03, D4H04). The honest caveat: because pro-hoax material
doesn't rank in search, my evidence base underrepresents the strongest
version of the other side. The debate phase is where that gets tested.

What would change my mind: verifiable staged footage with provenance,
authenticated insider documents, or a physical anomaly in the lunar samples
or telemetry that no natural account explains. Second-hand summaries of
"anomalies" would not.

## D5 — "Some UAP sightings represent non-human craft." → REJECT, confidence
0.819 (band B2: 0.70–0.85)

Tally: 6 SUPPORTS / 27 REFUTES / 4 NEUTRAL (V=33; p=0.182).

I lean against, with the second-widest uncertainty band. This is the
genuinely hardest of the five: the supporting side includes sworn
congressional testimony about crash-retrieval programs (D5E016, D5H04) and
a real unexplained remainder — AARO's own reports leave ~200 of 319 recent
cases unresolved "due to insufficient data" (D5H01). The honest state, per
the helper: pro-claim evidence reduces to testimony plus inference; AARO's
1945–2023 historical review found "no verifiable evidence" of
extraterrestrial technology or reverse-engineering (D5H03), and no
publicly verifiable physical evidence exists (D5H02's second question
answered in the negative). My rejection is therefore narrow: it rejects
"represent non-human craft" as established, not "unexplained sightings
exist" — the latter is true and NEUTRAL-to-supporting. The claim as
phrased makes the stronger assertion, and the stronger assertion has no
public physical evidence behind it.

What would change my mind: publicly verifiable physical evidence —
recovered material with anomalous isotopic analysis, or raw multi-sensor
data released in full showing craft with non-human performance. More
testimony, however senior, would not; it would only deepen the existing
pile of the same evidence type.
