## §2. Front (a) — Attack-aware contracts: enumerate the enemy, or check the property?

**Team FOR (steelman).** R2-2's mechanism was load-bearing — B4 PASSED: 49.7% of decisions changed, false installs cut from 2022 to 836. An enumerated adversary turns KB4 from an abstract property dispute into a concrete engineering duel: each spoof family gets a named defense, each defense a measured margin, and the false bank grows with every caught attack across rounds — deliberate learning from mistakes, which is exactly what the program claims TNN should do. Generic property-checking is what produced G2's "predictable ≠ right" disaster: a beautiful property with no model of the enemy. Micah's law is "tests decide," and an explicit adversary model *is* the tests, versioned and frozen per prereg. The "always one attack behind" regress is managed the way science manages incompleteness — the battery is the current best adversary, provisional, never final, and it is honest about being so.

**Team AGAINST (steelman).** R2-2 *is* the experiment on this front, and it measured the regress: 425 novel-family false installs, 4.0% targeted vs the 1% bar, 14.4% overall vs the 2% bar. The verdict's diagnosis is structural, not feature-level: "the margin cannot distinguish 'far from bank because safe' from 'far from bank because novel attack.'" No bank update fixes that ambiguity — it is the *shape* of enumerating the enemy: every defense is indexed by yesterday's attack, and the adversary reads the prereg. Debate A already ruled this as P8: adversarial robustness DIES as the checked property and SURVIVES as the measurement instrument — the referee, not a player. An attack-aware *contract* confuses the map (the frozen test) with the territory (the live adversary) and pays Goodhart's price: the gate optimizes against the enumerated list, and the novel family walks through the gap.

**Rebuttals.** FOR: the regress is bounded by upgrading from bank-distance to challenge-enumeration (R2-7's form): each enumerated family doesn't just get a bank entry, it gets a *discriminative challenge* the family fails by construction, not by distance. The bank was the weak form of attack-awareness; challenge-enumeration is the strong form. AGAINST: a discriminative challenge per family is still per family — the novel-family killer is *novelty*, and no per-family instrument measures novelty. The only thing that ever covered novel families was a property independent of the enumeration: H2's out-of-span requirement scored 0/10,000 with no attack model at all. Enumeration buys sharpness on knowns at the price of nothing on unknowns — and KB4 is graded on unknowns.

**RULING: AGAINST wins.** R2-2's 425 novel-family false installs are the measured regress, and the verdict's safe-vs-novel ambiguity is structural. Attack-enumeration DIES as the checked property — P8's ruling stands; this front was P8 in another costume. Attack-awareness SURVIVES as (i) the battery/referee layer — enumerated, frozen, grown per round — and (ii) the *design input* for R2-7-style challenge registries (each family must motivate a discriminative challenge). The check that installs is the challenge outcome, never family membership. Sol concurs (§9): "Use enumerated families to challenge a gate, not to certify it."

**PREREG-READY fork spec — FS-A "Attack-Informed Discriminative Challenge."**
- *Hypothesis:* a gate designed against an enumerated adversary is safe iff each enumerated family is answered by a discriminative challenge (claim vs strongest alternative predict different outcomes on disjoint evidence) rather than by a bank-distance check; the check that installs is the challenge outcome, never family membership.
- *Mechanism sketch:* take the frozen R2A 18-family battery; for each family preregister a challenge in the R2-7 registry form (named alternative, differing predicted outcomes, disjoint evidence path); INSTALL only on challenge PASS; UNRESOLVED/non-discriminating → WITHHOLD.
- *KILL BAR:* (1) false installs ≤1% overall and ≤2% per enumerated family on 10,000 adversarial trials (R2-7's frozen KB4 bars — justified as the program's current strictest install bar); (2) NOVEL-FAMILY HOLDOUT: ≥6 families frozen OUT of the design (fork authors never see them; frozen in the prereg); false installs ≤2% per holdout family, pooled ≤1% — any exceedance kills; (3) ablation: replace each challenge with an R2-2-form bank-distance check → false installs must rise ≥2× on enumerated families AND the holdout gap must widen — proves the challenge, not the enumeration, carries the load. Dies on any bar.

## §4. Front (c) — Independent cross-modal warrants

**Team FOR (steelman).** Cross-modal agreement is the most concrete "different measurement path" available to the R2-7 registry — different sensors, different noise, different failure modes. H2's rule 4 used cross-modal agreement as the gold-standard install condition, and H2 is the only fork with 0/10,000 false installs. The adversary's cost is strictly raised: fooling audio and vision jointly is harder than fooling one channel. Debate A P6 survived-in-composite. Where a percept is genuinely multimodal, requiring cross-modal agreement is the cheapest independent warrant on the table.

**Team AGAINST (steelman).** H1 *had* cross-modal corroboration in its contract (rule 5) and died at 44.5% — the adversarial battery fooled both modalities together (the 30%-misleading set was audio-visual by design). A competent adversary controls the scene, not the sensor; correlated fooling is the norm, not the exception. Two structural defects: (1) unimodal percepts (a pure tone, a still photo) are legitimate and common — a *required* warrant that cannot be checked half the time is not a contract, it is a sometimes-bonus; (2) R2-1's lesson transfers across the boundary: disjointness in the declared dimension (there: temporal spans) collapses when the front end shares invariants across it — if the audio and visual front-ends share an encoder or timing model, "different modality" is two costumes on one measurement. Debate A judged P6 booster-only; nothing since has promoted it.

**Rebuttals.** FOR: the R2-3 admission law answers both defects — disjointness is *declared and audited*, never assumed: the prereg declares which modality pairs count as independent per task, the battery carries correlated-fooling families, and unimodal percepts use the temporal/spatial disjoint options instead (P6 was never "required"). AGAINST: accepted as the composite — but that concedes the front: cross-modal is one admissible disjoint-evidence class inside R2-3, not a warrant in its own right. As a REQUIRED install warrant it dies on unimodality alone.

**RULING: AGAINST as a required install warrant; FOR as a booster/composite.** Debate A's P6 verdict is confirmed and extended: cross-modal corroboration SURVIVES as one admissible disjoint-evidence class inside the R2-3 admission law — never required, never sufficient; correlated fooling must be a frozen battery family. Sol concurs (§9): "Modality difference is not independence by itself… Do not silently substitute same-modality evidence and call it cross-modal."

**PREREG-READY fork spec — FS-C "Cross-Modal Booster Marginal-Gain fork."**
- *Hypothesis:* requiring cross-modal agreement (on multimodal tasks) reduces false installs over the R2-7 challenge baseline by a measurable, ablation-proven margin.
- *Mechanism sketch:* R2-7 challenge runner + booster rule: on multimodal tasks, INSTALL additionally requires the challenge to succeed on evidence from a second modality (declared disjoint per R2-3); unimodal tasks run the R2-7 baseline unchanged.
- *KILL BAR:* (1) R2-7's bars (≤1% overall, ≤2%/family, recall ≥80%); (2) on the multimodal task subset, false-install rate with booster vs without (ablation) — with-booster must be ≥5pp lower in absolute terms, AND the frozen correlated-fooling family (audio-visual joint spoof) must hold ≤2%; (3) no recall loss: multimodal-task recall with booster ≥ recall without − 5pp. Dies if the marginal gain is <5pp or recall drops — the booster must earn its keep or die. Justification: the 5pp margin bar forces the booster to prove it is load-bearing rather than decorative; the correlated-fooling family is H1's 44.5% death made into a test.

## §5. Front (d) — Temporal warrants: what distinguishes a working one?

**Team FOR (steelman).** Time is the one evidence dimension the adversary cannot compress into a single instant — a spoof must hold across windows. H3's mechanism was the only *active memory hygiene* in round 1: broken-prediction quarantine, downgrade, revocation — no other fork retired bad memories. The passage of sensory time is free evidence, and persistence is the cheapest out-of-span source available to a streaming PAM.

**Team AGAINST (steelman).** H3 is the experiment: 22.95% false-install vs the 4% bar — its predictions re-ran the same fooled measurements, so time added delay, not independence ("time is a dimension, not a witness" — debate A P5, unanimous). Then sticky quarantine produced amnesia: 0% recall on colordisc and colorconst, KB4 regret +89% vs A. And R2-1 tested the repaired form — temporal disjoint spans (formation frames 1–60, gate frames 61–120) — and died worse: 15.62% vs the 2% bar, ablation 1.37× vs the 5× bar: "when the front end is fooled on F, it is typically also fooled on G." The post-freeze experiment is the clincher: removing stickiness re-admitted the lies (KB1 35.2%, KB2 57.6%). Temporal separation without informational independence is slow self-confirmation.

**Rebuttals.** FOR: the failure is specified precisely — the predictions must be DISCRIMINATIVE (P10) on the later window, not mere re-measurement; H3's and R2-1's checks confirmed rather than discriminated. A temporal warrant that runs a claim-vs-alternative challenge on the later window is R2-7 with temporal evidence — untested as such. AGAINST: accepted — but that is R2-7, not a temporal warrant. The "temporal" label adds nothing once the requirements are independence + discrimination; a temporal warrant that works is a discriminative challenge that happens to use later evidence. Judge the composite; kill the standalone.

**RULING: AGAINST as standalone.** H3's 22.95% and R2-1's 15.62% are two independent epitaphs; debate A's P5 death stands. Temporal evidence SURVIVES only as one disjoint-evidence class inside the R2-3 admission law, and only when the check run on it is discriminative (P10). What distinguishes a working temporal warrant: the later window must carry a challenge whose outcomes *differ* under the claim vs the strongest alternative — confirmatory re-measurement dies.

**PREREG-READY fork spec — FS-D "Discriminative Temporal Challenge fork."**
- *Hypothesis:* a temporal warrant works iff the later-window check is discriminative (claim vs strongest alternative predict different outcomes), not confirmatory.
- *Mechanism sketch:* R2-7 challenge registry restricted to temporal-disjoint evidence per the R2-3 declarations (e.g., colordisc gate evidence = frames 61–120 vs formation 1–60); INSTALL only on discriminating-challenge PASS.
- *KILL BAR:* (1) R2-7's bars (≤1% overall, ≤2%/family on 10,000 adversarial; recall ≥80%); (2) the R2-1-killer ablation: replace the discriminative challenge with a confirmatory re-measurement on the same later window → false installs must rise ≥5× (a 1.37×-shaped ratio means the temporal leg is decorative and the fork dies); (3) R2-3 admission: ≥90% withhold on the R2P temporal pairs. Dies on any bar. Justification: the 5× ablation bar is R2-1's own kill-3 bar hardened — it directly separates working temporal warrants from H3/R2-1 self-confirmation.

## §6. Front (e) — Action-consequence warrants: cost vs safety

**Team FOR (steelman).** Grok's P11: intervention is the strongest form of the out-of-span check because it breaks confounds observation cannot. R2-8 (Grok's hypothesis) is built; the 5× percept cost (X, S, P1, P2, P3) is linear and honest. Where action exists, observation-only checks are the weak form — debate A said so, and the crew accepted the correction.

**Team AGAINST (steelman).** Cost and scope. 5× per install prices intervention out of streaming PAMs — efficiency bars (B3) exist for a reason. Worse, scope: most PAM percepts are passive (a still photo, a field recording) — there is no intervention available, and inventing one (re-rendering, re-querying) is observation in a costume. And the action channel is itself attackable: an adversary that controls the environment predicts the intervention's consequence as easily as the percept does — the do-operator is only as clean as the actuator. No datum yet: R2-8 is PENDING on B4/B5/KB2/recall/leg-(ii); judging the principle before the verdict is premature.

**Rebuttals.** FOR: scope is handled by composition — intervention is the strongest form *where available* (P11's debate-A ruling: SURVIVES-IN-COMPOSITE), observational challenge elsewhere; the cost objection is answered empirically by R2-8's leg-(ii) ablation (≥0.2pp reduction vs leg-i-only), which measures whether the 5× buys anything. AGAINST: agreed — the principle is conditional on R2-8's verdict. If the interventional leg's ablation fails, intervention dies as load-bearing regardless of its philosophical appeal.

**RULING: FOR as the strongest form, AGAINST as a requirement.** P11's composite verdict stands: interventional warrants are the strongest admissible form of the out-of-span check where an action channel exists; they are never required for passive percepts. No new fork until R2-8's verdict lands — the pending evaluation IS the experiment (B5 <0.5%, KB2 ≥95% self-flag, recall ≥70%, leg-(ii) ablation ≥0.2pp).

## §7. Front (f) — G3+attack features: closing the last 3.36 points, or the ceiling?

**Team FOR (steelman).** G3's *mechanism* passed every bar it faced — B4 84% disposition change (12 vs 102 false installs), 97.0% retrieval correctness (745/768), B6 byte-identical determinism, cheaper than Approach A (36 fixed bytes; 8k–204k ops vs A's 8k–4.5M). The ONLY failure was feature coarseness: 4-bit harmonic bins, area/radius ratios, coarse displacement cells — the attacks moved the class without moving the bins. That is front-end engineering, and 4.36% → 1% is 3.36pp of feature work. Attack-aware features (finer spectral structure, phase, contour detail) close the collision space the adversary exploited. Nearest miss in the program — finish the job.

**Team AGAINST (steelman).** R2-2 ran this exact composite — two-sided reference, frozen false bank, the program's best mechanism — and died at 4.0% targeted (bar 1%) and 14.4% overall (bar 2%) with 425 novel-family false installs. The verdict's diagnosis is not "features too coarse": "the margin cannot distinguish 'far from bank because safe' from 'far from bank because novel attack'" — that ambiguity is structural to the relational-signature property. Every signature is lossy; the adversary lives in the lost bits; finer features move the collision boundary but the bank is still always one attack behind. The 3.36pp gap is not feature work — it is the property's ceiling, and R2-2 measured it.

**Rebuttals.** FOR: R2-2's features were G3's frozen coarse features — it tested the two-sided *reference*, not attack-aware *features*; the ceiling claim is untested until the features are actually rebuilt against the collision families. AGAINST: the ceiling argument doesn't depend on feature fineness — it depends on novelty: any finite feature set has a collision class the adversary finds next. The holdout test (novel families the feature designers never saw) is the falsifier, and R2-2's 425 novel installs show the shape of the failure. If attack-aware features close the gap on the holdout, the ceiling claim dies — so run the holdout.

**RULING: AGAINST — the property is the ceiling.** R2-2's verdict stands: the margin's ambiguity under novel attack is structural, and no feature refinement removes novelty. Attack-aware features SURVIVE as front-end work (better features help every fork's front end) but DIE as the contract's closing argument for the last 3.36pp — the contract cannot be saved by features alone.

## §9. Second opinion — gpt-5.6-sol via UnoRouter, 2026-09-23 (verbatim)

[BEGIN VERBATIM — Sol / gpt-5.6-sol]
### (a) Attack-aware contracts

**Steelman for enumeration:** A frozen adversary list makes the claim testable: the gate can be evaluated against concrete spoof families, rather than relying on a vague property that may not distinguish real from false evidence. It also supports targeted defenses and clear failure analysis.

**Verdict:** Enumeration does not beat property-checking as the admission law. It can make testing sharper, but it does not establish that evidence is independent or discriminative. R2-2 is direct evidence of the coverage failure: despite a frozen bank, novel-family false installs were 425, and both targeted and overall rates exceeded their bars. A list of attacks can become one attack behind. Use enumerated families to challenge a gate, not to certify it. The durable requirement should remain a property of the warrant, with explicit tests for known and novel attacks.

### (c) Independent cross-modal warrants

**Steelman for cross-modal confirmation:** A second modality can supply evidence with different sensors, noise, and failure modes. That may prevent a spoof that fools one perceptual pipeline from automatically validating its own output. It is a concrete route to independence, not merely a second view of the same signal.

**Verdict:** Modality difference is not independence by itself. If the adversary controls both channels, or the channels share preprocessing, timing, or invariants, they can be fooled together; R2-1 shows why disjoint spans alone do not solve shared-front-end failures. Cross-modal evidence is useful only when its formation path and attack exposure are demonstrably independent and it discriminates the relevant false hypothesis. A unimodal percept is a real scope limitation: it cannot meet a mandatory cross-modal admission rule without rejecting otherwise valid installs. That is not fatal if the system explicitly abstains or defers those installs; it is fatal to a claim that the rule supports general installation across unimodal cases. Do not silently substitute same-modality evidence and call it cross-modal.

### Preregistered kill bar: attack-aware contracts

Freeze the adversary families, sample-generation rules, thresholds, and evaluation code before testing. Use deterministic decisions and disjoint train, tuning, and test material. Report counts and denominators, not just percentages.

- **False-install bar:** On held-out attacks from every enumerated family, at most 1% targeted false installs and at most 2% overall, each with at least 10,000 attempted installs. Require the upper 95% confidence bound to meet each threshold; a zero count in 10,000 is not by itself proof of a zero rate.
- **Recall bar:** At least 90% of valid installs accepted, with at least 10,000 valid attempts spanning the declared operating conditions. Apply the same confidence-bound rule.
- **Novel-family bar:** Freeze a separate set of at least 10 previously undisclosed spoof families, with at least 1,000 attempts per family. Require no family above 2% false installs and a pooled rate at most 1%. Any post-freeze family disclosure invalidates that family’s novel status.
- **Ablation bar:** Compare the full contract against a preregistered ablation removing its claimed independent, discriminative warrant while holding the rest fixed. Require the full contract’s false-install rate to be at least 50% lower on held-out attacks, without reducing valid-install recall by more than 5 percentage points. If the ablation is not worse, the claimed mechanism has not earned credit.

Failure of any bar kills the contract as specified; passing these tests supports only the frozen scope, not protection against arbitrary adversaries.
