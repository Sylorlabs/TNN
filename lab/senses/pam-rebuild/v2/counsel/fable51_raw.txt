H1: **Temporal-Correlation Audit Trail (TCAT)**

(H) Maintain a rolling hash-chain of the last N accept-install decisions (e.g. N=128) in a separate PAM register. Each entry records: percept hash, confidence, margin, agree-count, AND the percept hashes of the K (e.g. K=5) most recent prior installs. Before any new ACCEPT_INSTALL, the gate computes the empirical false-positive rate of sub-sequences in the trail where K+1 consecutive installs shared >80% percept-similarity (same timbre family). If that running FPR exceeds 3× the overall installed FPR, ALL future installs on that timbre-family are gated to a HOLD state requiring three independent high-confidence re-senses under deliberately varied attention windows (different FFT window sizes, different frequency-band emphases, different temporal crops) before re-entry. This is historical, not per-trial: it audits the corridor, not the candidate.

(E) This directly targets the correlated-corroborator hole: the six wrong high-confidence agreeing PASSes (seq 10983–11192) would form a suspicious K-clique whose running FPR would spike, triggering the hold. It respects ceiling (2) because it never compares a single trial's evidence against its incumbent; it compares *corridors* of decisions against *historical rates*. It respects ceiling (1) because the mechanism sits on the install-side, not the judgment-side — it audits the stream of decisions, not the internals of a single percept-pair. It survives the committed evidence because the six agreeing wrongs are precisely the pattern it detects: correlated sub-families producing correlated installs at above-base-rate.

(K) Kill test: inject 200 novel adversarial trials from families that produce *genuinely uncorrelated* wrong judgments (no timbre-family clustering). If TCAT triggers holds on >5% of these uncorrelated-wrong trials, it has a false alarm problem — it is over-generalizing from the correlation signal. Kill bar: >5% false hold rate on the uncorrelated-wrong battery = falsified.

H2: **Adversarial-Diversity Re-Sense (ADRS)**

(H) When a percept scores CONF ≥ 700 and AGREE ≥ 1 but the gate is HOLD or UNRESOLVED, instead of re-sensing with the same pipeline, deliberately *rotate the sensing modality*: apply a fixed set of 4 deterministic transforms to the raw bytes before re-sensing — (a) byte-pair XOR shuffle, (b) fixed-bit-reversal, (c) block-cyclic-rotation by a non-power-of-2 offset, (d) frequency-band masking that zeroes the band with highest energy in the original percept. Each produces a new 3-byte percept from the same underlying signal. Install requires 3-of-4 to agree on the core judgment within a defined equivalence class (same RICH/not-RICH, same timbre-family, confidence within 100 points).

(E) This defeats the correlated-corroborator hole because the four re-senses share *zero* deterministic-function pathway with the original sense — each sees a permuted signal. A front-end error that is "sustained and corroborated" under the original sense will be *broken* by at least some of the four transforms, since the attacker controls sensitivity of the original pathway but not of these four fixed orthogonal ones. It respects ceiling (1) because each re-sense produces genuinely different judgment-side information (the transforms destroy the deterministic-function identity). It respects ceiling (2) because the install rule is historical (requires 3-of-4 agreement across modalities, not a single-trial evidence comparison). It survives the evidence because it would have forced the six wrong seq 10983–11192 entries through four orthogonal pipelines, where at least some transforms would have disambiguated the spoof.

(K) Kill test: identify 50 percept families where all four transforms *systematically agree on the wrong answer* (constructible by choosing families invariant under the transform set). If ADRS installs >10% of these systematically-wrong 4-agree families, it provides no protection beyond the original gate and is falsified. Kill bar: >10% install rate on the transform-invariant adversarial set.

H3: **Confidence-Decay Staleness Gate**

(H) Every installed percept gets a monotonic timestamp. The gate imposes a *maximum install-age* per timbre-family: if the most recent installed percept in a family is older than T (calibrated per-family from the observed stability distribution — e.g., families with historically high churn get T=50 installs, stable families get T=500), then *all* new candidates in that family must pass a STRICT re-verification: three independent re-senses under three different attention crops (beginning 20%, middle 60%, end 20% of the sample) must agree before install, regardless of confidence. No percept is "settled" forever; every percept must be periodically re-earned.

(E) This attacks the deployment blocker at its root: the sustained-corroborated-error persists because once installed, wrong percepts never face re-verification. The six agreeing wrongs (seq 10983–11192) would have been installed and then *left alone*. Under staleness gating, they would eventually expire and require fresh independent verification from different temporal crops — where the adversarial signal is likely weaker or absent. It respects ceiling (2) because the three-crop re-verification is historical (requires three separate temporal positions, not a single-trial comparison). It respects ceiling (1) because the different temporal crops carry genuinely different information. It pushes past the gate-side ceiling (3) because some of the 278 correct high-confidence percepts that currently never reach PASS could be re-sensed under different crops and reach PASS through the multi-crop path.

(K) Kill test: measure the system's accuracy over 5,000 trials after installing staleness gating with T=100. If accuracy drops below 85% (i.e., the staleness re-verification *increases* error rate by destabilizing correct installs), the mechanism is net-negative. Kill bar: post-TAM accuracy < 85% RK-3 = falsified.

H4: **Novelty-First Install Path**

(H) Create a parallel install channel that *bypasses the gate entirely* for percepts from families with no prior installs. Rule: if a percept's timbre-family hash matches zero entries in the installed database, it is installed directly at confidence=its raw confidence, flagged as PROVISIONAL. After N subsequent trials in the same family (N=10), the provisional install is either confirmed (if ≥80% of subsequent trials agree) or purged (and the family is blacklisted for 50 trials). This separates the *cold-start* problem from the *sustained-corroboration* problem.

(E) This pushes past gate-side ceiling (3) by creating a pathway for the 278 correct high-confidence percepts that currently die at the gate — many are likely from families that are *new* to the system and have no install history to gate against. The 53% novelty accuracy is low, but this mechanism only needs the *first* install to be *possibly* right; subsequent trials correct it. It respects ceiling (2) because the provisional install is explicitly provisional and subject to historical review. It survives the evidence because it converts the 824/1,102 correct-but-uninstalled bottleneck into a filtered stream where novel-correct percepts get a chance to enter the system.

(K) Kill test: measure the 60-trial accuracy of percepts that entered via the novelty-first path. If provisional installs from novel families are wrong >40% of the time at the 60-trial checkpoint (worse than random within the family distribution), the path is injecting too much error. Kill bar: >40% wrong rate among provisional installs at 60-trial checkpoint.

H5: **Counter-Corroboration Trap** (challenges ceiling 2 partially)

(H) Maintain a *negative exemplar bank*: percepts that were installed then later *reversed* by explicit deliberate re-inspection. Before any new install, the gate checks whether the candidate percept is within a defined distance of any negative exemplar (same family, confidence within 150, margin within 2000). If it is, the install is BLOCKED regardless of evidence score, until three deliberate re-inspections from three different temporal crops all independently confirm the candidate — at which point the negative exemplar is *removed* from the bank (the system has explicitly re-judged and confirmed). This weaponizes *past errors* as *active gates* rather than passive history.

(E) The six wrong agrees (seq 10983–11192), once discovered and reversed, become negative exemplars. Any future percept from the same sub-family with similar confidence/margin is blocked until explicitly re-verified — preventing the correlated-corroborator pattern from recurring. This partially challenges ceiling (2) by making single-trial evidence comparison *sufficient* to block (not to install): the candidate is compared to the exemplar for blocking, but installation still requires three-crop confirmation. The key insight is that blocking is safer than installing — blocking a correct percept merely delays it, while installing a wrong percept corrupts the system. It respects ceiling (1) because it operates on install-side, not judgment-side.

(K) Kill test: insert 300 correct percepts that happen to be similar to established negative exemplars (same family, close confidence/margin). If >25% of these correct percepts are blocked for more than 50 trials before installation, the mechanism is over-blocking and its delay cost exceeds its safety benefit. Kill bar: >25% of near-exemplar correct percepts delayed >50 trials.

---

**Ranking.** H5 (Counter-Corroboration Trap) ranks first by expected value: it directly weaponizes the already-discovered errors into active defenses, has the lowest implementation complexity (lookup + block + confirm), and its kill condition (over-blocking) is far more graceful than installing wrongs — a delayed correct percept is recoverable, a installed wrong is not. H1 (TCAT) ranks second: its corridor-level auditing is the most natural fit for the correlated-corroborator problem and is purely additive to existing gates. H3 (Confidence-Decay) ranks third: it addresses the root cause of sustained errors but risks destabilizing correct installs, making it higher-variance. H4 (Novelty-First) ranks fourth: it is the only mechanism that attacks the gate-side ceiling directly, which is the hardest structural bottleneck, but its 53% novelty accuracy makes it high-risk. H2 (ADRS) ranks fifth: theoretically strongest against sophisticated attackers, but the requirement to construct transform-invariant adversarial families for the kill battery suggests the attack surface is narrow enough that simpler mechanisms may suffice.
