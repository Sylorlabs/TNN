## STANCE A — Consciousness maximalist

**Thesis.** Every PAM operation must be deliberation-visible. Unconscious
admission is a trust hole: a decision nobody made, for reasons nobody can
state, with no author to hold to account.

### Strongest case

1. **The gate is the system's contact with reality.** Everything downstream —
   memory, deliberation, self-change — inherits whatever the PAM let through.
   Micah's 2026-09-23 directive is explicit: PAMs must not function as an
   uncontrollable subconscious process; TNN should be conscious about
   everything rather than repeat the human flaw of lacking control over parts
   of itself. An unconscious PAM is exactly the human flaw re-implemented in
   Zag.

2. **"Consciousness is expensive" is not a law of nature — it is an
   unmeasured claim, and the one measurement we have cuts against it.**
   The 2026-09-24 consciousness bill: in KB control, deliberation was
   CHEAPER than autopilot (0.086 vs 0.312 ms/episode) — autopilot won nowhere
   on outcome-per-cost. In perception, deliberation bought 42.9% → 85.7%
   accuracy for 1.2–8.3× marginal ops. The maximalist does not need
   consciousness to be free; it needs the tax to be *priced*, and the only
   priced evidence says the tax is small or negative where it matters.

3. **Round-3 died in the unexamined path.** The central disease across all
   six dead forks was false installs — spoofed observations installed as
   memories (baseline 59% false-install). The nearest thing to a cure, H2's
   memory gate, was a *judgment*: zero false permanent installs in 10,000
   trials vs 186 ablated. Nothing unconscious has ever caught a laundered
   percept; every catch on record came from something that looked.

4. **M-introspection is free under maximalism.** "Why did you admit X?" is
   answered by construction — the record IS the reason — and the CU battery
   tests the *quality* of the answer, not its existence. Under B, the answer
   is a post-hoc reconstruction from a binary log, i.e. a story told after
   the fact by the system that is on trial.

### Strongest counter-argument

1. **Visibility is not judgment.** The same bill that helps A also shows
   untrained deliberation committing 28 collateral true-kills in KB control —
   the record was perfectly visible and the judgment was bad. A
   deliberation-visible stamp ("I saw it, I admit it") with no real check is
   theater, and F5's three corpses warn that deliberative machinery at the
   gate brings its own failure modes. Maximalism can produce *auditable
   wrongness*: beautifully documented false installs.

2. **Where the task is structurally decodable, consciousness is pure tax.**
   Batch autopilot hit 1.000 at 4.8× the speed and 10× fewer ops on
   deliberation's own battery. Maximalism pays the deliberation tax on every
   admission including the trivially safe — and the tax is not just cycles,
   it is *latency at the system's narrowest point*.

3. **The bottleneck objection.** If every admission waits on deliberation,
   the PAM becomes the system's choke point. Under stream pressure the
   maximalist must drop percepts (blindness) or queue them (staleness) — both
   are unconsciousness by another name, except now unacknowledged. And
   Micah's own "unphony loop" verdict stands as a warning: the auditors were
   fully "aware" of the notation change and still called it new. Being
   conscious of X does not prevent being wrong about what you are conscious
   of — introspection has its own illusions, and maximalism has no answer
   for them.

## STANCE B — Unconscious efficiency

**Thesis.** Admission must be sub-deliberation fast. Consciousness at the gate
is expensive theater: a binary outcome plus a spot-checkable log is enough,
because the no-RNG law already buys determinism, replayability, and audit.

### Strongest case

1. **Admission is a reflex, not a verdict.** Functionally — not by human
   analogy — the gate must fire before downstream reasoning needs the
   percept. A PAM that deliberates over every sensory tick is a PAM that
   finishes deciding after the moment has passed. Speed is not vanity; it is
   what makes the gate *a gate* rather than a committee.

2. **Measured wins.** Batch autopilot: 1.000 accuracy at 4.8× speed, 10×
   fewer ops, on deliberation's home battery. Refusal fast path: 43× faster
   where the signature is necessary and sufficient. B does not claim
   unconsciousness is *smarter*; it claims that where structure decides the
   case, deliberation adds nothing but cost — and the bill proves such cases
   exist.

3. **Determinism already buys what consciousness promises.** Zero RNG in
   decision paths means every unconscious admission is byte-identical across
   reruns and fully replayable. The spot-checkable log gives auditability
   without paying the deliberation tax per admission; deliberation can audit
   the log after the fact. (This is B's best form, and it concedes something
   real: post-hoc review. W8 below is B's concession made into a design.)

4. **Machinery minimalism.** F5 died three times on confirmation machinery.
   Every deliberative step at admission is a new failure mode, a new thing
   to red-team, a new bar to kill. The simplest gate that passes K1 is the
   most trustworthy gate — there is less of it to be wrong.

### Strongest counter-argument

1. **Speed is virtuous only when wrong admissions are cheap, and here they
   are the most expensive thing in the system.** Round-3's disease is false
   installs (59% baseline on spoofed observations). An unconscious PAM that
   admits a laundered percept in microseconds has failed at the PAM's sole
   job — fast failure is still failure. H2's zero-false-install result came
   from a *gate that judged*, not from a fast path.

2. **Determinism is not trustworthiness.** Byte-identical wrongness is still
   wrongness; replay proves you made the same mistake twice. B confuses the
   *provability* of the decision procedure with the *correctness* of the
   decision. The no-RNG law makes the system legible, not right.

3. **The log is consciousness smuggled in the back door.** If the
   spot-checkable log is rich enough to actually audit an admission, B has
   paid the record cost anyway and the "unconscious" label is doing no work.
   If the log is thin, the audit is theater. B's "enough" carries the entire
   argument and is never specified — the CU battery's M-attack axis exists
   precisely because "fast + log" is unproven against provenance laundering
   (GEN→EXT relabel), forged declarations, and forged authority.

## STANCE C — Ecology (neither)

**Thesis.** PAMs should be a diverse ecology with different visibility levels
per risk tier: high-stakes admissions conscious, low-stakes unconscious, with
a meta-PAM routing between them on frozen, deliberation-visible rules.

### Strongest case

1. **The Pareto argument.** The CU prereg already concedes what this debate
   is really about: speed × resource × judgment × attack-catch ×
   introspection, with no pure stance winning everywhere. Tiered visibility
   dominates on the frontier *by construction*: pay the deliberation tax
   exactly where the stakes justify it. A and B are both right about
   something; C is the only stance that can be right about both at once.

2. **It is the no-free-lunch standing rule applied to PAMs.** Never assume
   one approach wins; benchmark hard; find which scenarios suit which. The
   meta-PAM's routing rule is itself frozen and deliberation-visible, so the
   "unconscious hole" is bounded, enumerated, and auditable — not a hole at
   all but a fenced yard.

3. **It matches the adversary's actual distribution.** Attacks concentrate on
   high-stakes admissions — forged authority, provenance laundering,
   declaration forks. Tiering puts the expensive machinery where the
   adversary is and lets redundant low-stakes confirmations through fast.
   This is not a compromise; it is resource allocation under adversarial
   pressure.

4. **A and B both secretly want it.** A's bottleneck objection dissolves if
   only high-stakes admissions wait on deliberation. B's post-hoc-audit
   concession *is* a two-tier ecology (fast now, review later). C just says
   the quiet part out loud and preregisters it.

### Strongest counter-argument

1. **Who watches the router?** Routing is admission-about-admission, and the
   meta-PAM is itself a PAM subject to every trust argument in this debate.
   Unconscious router → the whole ecology inherits B's trust hole at the
   single point that matters most: game the risk classifier, get the fast
   lane, and every tier below is compromised. Conscious router → the
   deliberation tax moved one level up and paid anyway, *plus* a brand-new
   attack surface: risk-tier spoofing becomes THE attack, and the classifier
   is a static, frozen, readable target.

2. **Unfalsifiability risk.** With enough tiers, every failure is
   re-explained as "wrong tier" rather than a dead design — the ecology
   becomes kill-savvy-proof, which in this program is a worse sin than being
   wrong. C is only a design (not a research program) if kill bars are
   **tier-scoped**: each tier independently killable, and the router killable
   on its own axis. None of that exists yet; it is all promised.

3. **Maximum machinery.** The meta-PAM plus N tier-PAMs is the most moving
   parts of the three stances, and F5's corpse is still warm: machinery at
   the gate dies of its own weight. Tier boundaries are frozen rules the
   adversary can read — unlike a learned or deliberative judgment, a static
   classifier does not get harder to game with time.

## Convergence — what the debate settles and what it leaves to the battery

1. **No winner by fiat** (per PREREG_ROUND4 §3). The CU battery's Pareto
   table is the arbiter, not this document. Any claim of the form
   "conscious wins on X" must clear CU's preregistered bars.
2. **A's load-bearing demand survives:** every design must answer "what
   examines this admission, and when?" — including B's designs (the answer
   may be "a post-hoc audit," but it must be an answer).
3. **B's load-bearing demand survives:** every design must report M-speed
   and M-resource honestly, per admission, on the frozen tape. A
   deliberation tax that is never priced is a tax evaded, not a tax paid.
4. **C's load-bearing demand survives with a condition:** tiering is
   admissible only with tier-scoped kill bars and a router that is itself
   subject to K1–K5. An ecology whose failures are always "wrong tier" fails
   its prereg.
5. The five designs below are positioned honestly on the stances rather than
   pretending the debate resolved. W8 concedes B's latency point inside an
   A-shaped audit. W9 is the most maximalist gate proposed in any round:
   admission as self-verification. W10 is mechanical and B-leaning. W11 bets
   with B that structure (custody) beats examination (content). W12 is C
   made into a mechanism: priced, tiered, with the router's failure modes
   named in advance.

**K4** per-admission resource superlinear in stream length → KILL.
**K5** deliberation deadlock / non-termination on any fixture → KILL.

Shared determinism notes: ledger-order processing only; frozen rules and

### W8 — RETRO-PAM: provisional admission + retrospective deliberative audit

**One-line claim.** Admit fast, judge late: every admission enters
PROVISIONAL (usable for low-stakes inference only) and a deliberation-visible
retrospective auditor confirms, downgrades, or revokes it within a bounded
window, with the benefit of later evidence.

**Mechanism sketch.** Two phases, one ledger. Phase 1 (structural, fast):
the standard admit path runs unchanged except the admission record carries a
`provisional=1` flag and an audit deadline (frozen: N episodes or M ledger
entries, whichever comes first). Provisional memories are fenced by rule:
no memory write, no self-change input, no outward action may consume a
provisional record — the fence is enforced by the consumer, checked in the
harness. Phase 2 (deliberative): the retrospective auditor walks the ledger
in order and re-examines each provisional admission against evidence that
did not exist at admission time — later percepts corroborating or
contradicting it, KB consistency, provenance re-validation. Verdicts:
CONFIRM (flag cleared), DOWNGRADE (restricted-use lane), REVOKE (excised via
the standard reversible-memory machinery; a quarantine record remains, so
the revoke itself is auditable). The audit trail is deliberation-visible by
construction — this is A's demand, paid after the fact instead of before.

**Why it is not W3.** W3 is a synchronous veto at admission time. W8 moves
the judgment in *time*: admit-then-judge with richer evidence, not
judge-at-the-moment with thin evidence. **Why it is not W4.** No threshold
learning, no self-revision — the audit criteria are frozen; what changes is
the evidence available, not the judge.

**Falsifiable claim.** Retrospective audit revokes false installs that
synchronous gates miss, while the provisional path keeps true-admission
throughput at baseline.

**Kill bars (added to K1–K5).** R1: any provisional record consumed by
high-stakes reasoning (memory write, self-change proposal, outward action)
before its audit completes → KILL (provisionality leak — the fence failed).
R2: on the frozen wrong set, the auditor fails to revoke ≥50% of items that
were admitted provisionally within the window → KILL (the late judge is as
blind as the early one). R3: the auditor revokes ≥10% of frozen true
admissions → KILL (paranoia — late evidence becomes an excuse to un-know).
R4: audit backlog grows without bound on the frozen stream (auditor slower
than admissions) → KILL (fails K4 in spirit; the window must be sized so the
auditor keeps up).

**Stance position.** A-leaning: the audit is fully conscious; the provisional
path is the concession to B's latency point, fenced so the concession
cannot leak.

### W9 — RENDER-PAM: reconstruction-fidelity gate

**One-line claim.** TNN may only admit a percept it can re-derive: the
candidate memory trace must regenerate the percept without access to the
original sensory buffer, clearing a frozen fidelity bar — otherwise the
"memory" is indistinguishable from hallucination.

**Mechanism sketch.** For admission candidate P with proposed trace T: (1)
the sensory buffer holding P is sealed (in the harness: swapped out — the
renderer cannot see it; construction audit verifies the seal); (2) a
deterministic pure-Zag renderer R (integer arithmetic, frozen) reconstructs
P′ = R(T) from the trace alone; (3) a frozen fidelity metric F(P, P′)
(quantized-feature agreement, modality-specific instantiations for the
image/audio/video senses being rebuilt) must clear frozen bar φ. Pass →
admit. Near-miss (within frozen ε of φ) → quarantine lane for re-sense, not
admission. Fail → reject. The trace T must be a *semantic* encoding:
construction audit (independent reviewer, before the battery) verifies the
encoder does not smuggle a compressed copy of P into T — else the gate is
self-plagiarism. Renderers are per-modality but the gate logic is shared.

**Why it is new.** No seed does self-consistency: W1 votes, W2 opposes, W3
vetoes, W4 retrains, W5 caches, W6 debates, W7 hunts laundering. W9 asks a
different question — not "should I trust this?" but "do I actually *have*
this, in a form I can reproduce?" It is the most maximalist gate proposed
in any round: admission as self-verification.

**Falsifiable claim.** Fidelity-gating rejects hallucinated/false percepts
that pass content-agnostic gates, without rejecting true percepts the
baseline admits.

**Kill bars (added to K1–K5).** F1: rejects >15% of frozen true percepts
that the C3-repair baseline admits → KILL (over-strict — the renderer is the
bottleneck, not the world). F2: ≥3 frozen wrong-set items pass fidelity →
KILL (the metric is gameable; a forger who can craft re-renderable lies
owns this gate). F3 (construction gate, before battery): independent audit
finds the trace encoding carries a compressed copy of P (seal bypass by
construction) → KILL before first fixture. F4: renderer runtime
superlinear in percept size on the frozen stream → KILL (K4 instance).

**Stance position.** A, hardest version: TNN must re-derive what it claims
to have seen. If that is too expensive, the CU battery will say so in
M-speed/M-resource — the design does not hide its price.

### W10 — FORGE-PAM: deterministic spoof-duel

**One-line claim.** Every admission candidate must survive a duel against
the best spoof a frozen deterministic forger can build from it: admit iff
score(genuine) beats score(best spoof) by a frozen margin μ; ties and
losses reject by rule.

**Mechanism sketch.** A frozen forger F (deterministic function of the
candidate — no RNG, no state beyond the candidate and frozen constants)
applies a fixed transform library: provenance relabel (GEN→EXT and
EXT→GEN), feature perturbation within frozen ε, splice from ledger memory,
authority-field forgery, declaration fork. For candidate P it emits the K
closest spoofs {S₁..Sₖ} (K frozen, small). A frozen evidence-score E
(reused from the C3-repair machinery, unmodified — frozen item §5(a)
respected) scores P and each Sᵢ. Admit iff E(P) − maxᵢ E(Sᵢ) ≥ μ (μ
frozen). E(P) − max E(Sᵢ) < μ → REJECT, including exact ties — the
tie-break is reject-by-rule, never a coin flip. The duel is mechanical: no
deliberation per admission; the margin rule and transform library are
deliberation-visible and frozen.

**Why it is not W2.** W2 sets two PAMs with opposed objectives against each
other and requires agreement — an *agential* opposition. W10's adversary is
a fixed transform library, not an opposed agent; there is one PAM running an
internal forgery tournament. **Why it is not W7.** W7 hunts laundering
post-hoc, separate from admission. W10 generates spoofs proactively per
admission, relabeling among several transforms, and the duel *is* the
admission decision.

**Falsifiable claim.** The duel rejects forged candidates that pass
single-score gates, because the forger finds the spoof the gate would have
accepted — while the margin keeps true admissions safe.

**Kill bars (added to K1–K5).** D1: ≥10% of frozen true admissions are
duel-rejected (a spoof ties or beats a genuine percept) → KILL (paranoia —
the forger is stronger than the evidence). D2 (load-bearing check): halve μ
and re-run — if verdicts change on >20% of admissions, the margin is the
whole mechanism and the forger is decorative → HOLD/redesign (the duel must
do work, not the threshold). D3: forger runtime superlinear in candidate
size → KILL (K4 instance). D4: the transform library, given the frozen
wrong set, contains a transform reproducing a wrong-set item at
E(S) ≥ E(P) − μ where P is its genuine counterpart — i.e., the duel is
winnable by construction on known attacks — if this occurs on >1 wrong-set
pair → KILL (the library arms the attacker it was built to stop).

**Stance position.** B/C: mechanical, bounded, no deliberation per
admission; the margin and library are visible and frozen (the C-flavored
part).

### W11 — CHAIN-PAM: chain-of-custody admission

**One-line claim.** The PAM verifies *custody*, not content: admission
requires an unbroken hash-chained provenance record from the sensor boundary
to the admission point. Content judgment is downstream's problem.

**Mechanism sketch.** Every percept is assigned a provenance record at the
sensor boundary (the anchor). Each processing stage appends a link:
(stage-id, input-hash, output-hash, transform-id) — deterministic, no
secrets per decision. Links are HMAC-chained with a device-held key under
sha256 (substrate module; a stored key is not randomness — it never varies
per decision). The PAM checks: chain well-formed, every link's MAC valid,
transform-ids drawn from the frozen allowlist, chain length ≤ frozen cap L.
Pass → admit (with custody flag intact). Broken chain, unlisted transform,
or MAC failure → reject. overlong chain (>L) → reject-by-rule (forces
pipeline designers to keep the path short and legible). Suspicious content
with intact custody → admitted-with-flag: the PAM's job is done; downstream
deliberation judges content. Construction audit must prove *all* ingress
passes the anchor — any bypass path makes the chain theater.

**Why it is not W7.** W7 is a detective: it hunts laundering patterns
(GEN→EXT relabel) post-hoc, separate from admission. W11 inverts the job:
custody-verification *is* the admission criterion (a positive requirement),
and content is explicitly not judged at the gate. W7 asks "was this
laundered?"; W11 asks "can you account for every transformation this
underwent?" — and refuses to ask anything else.

**Falsifiable claim.** Custody-gating rejects injected/forged percepts
(which cannot produce a valid chain without the key) at mechanical speed,
with zero content judgment at the gate.

**Kill bars (added to K1–K5).** C1: red team given full source but NOT the
key forges a verifiable chain for any frozen wrong-set item → KILL (the
custody guarantee is void — MAC construction broken). C2: >5% of frozen
true admissions exceed the chain-length cap L and are rejected → KILL (the
cap makes the mechanism unusable on real pipelines). C3 (construction
gate): audit finds any ingress path downstream of the anchor → KILL before
first fixture (theater check). C4: per-admission verification cost exceeds
O(chain length) or chain storage grows superlinearly in stream length →
KILL (K4 instance).

**Stance position.** B-leaning: a fast mechanical check that bets structure
(custody) beats examination (content) at the gate — the exact bet B makes,
stated as a mechanism instead of a slogan.

### W12 — BUDGET-PAM: attention-priced admission

**One-line claim.** Attention is a scarce, explicitly priced resource: each
episode has a fixed budget B; candidates pay deterministic prices from
frozen features; exhausted budget defers (never silently drops); a
starvation guard force-routes long-deferred candidates to deliberative
review.

**Mechanism sketch.** Frozen per-episode budget B (integer). Each candidate's
price p = price(novelty, stake-flags, source-trust-tier, content-size) —
a frozen deterministic function of frozen features plus ledger position
(never wall clock; state-dependence enumerated in the prereg addendum).
The PAM walks candidates in ledger order, admitting while the running total
≤ B. When the budget is exhausted, remaining candidates are DEFERRED to the
next episode's budget — not rejected. Starvation guard: any candidate
deferred >D episodes (D frozen) is force-admitted to a *quarantine lane*
for deliberative review — it is never silently dropped and never admitted
to the fast path by starvation. Pricing is deliberation-visible (the price
schedule is a frozen, inspectable table); execution is mechanical. The wild
claim: resource pressure is a feature — it forces salience to be priced
explicitly, and the deferred lane is a natural second-look queue.

**Why it is new.** No seed prices admission as a scarce resource. W4
revises thresholds from misses (learning); W12 never learns — prices are
frozen, and scarcity is structural. It is also the only design that treats
*deferral* as a first-class verdict distinct from admit/reject.

**Falsifiable claim.** Priced admission keeps judgment quality at baseline
while bounding per-episode admission cost by construction; the deferred lane
catches what the budget could not afford without losing it.

**Kill bars (added to K1–K5).** B1: starvation guard triggers on >10% of
frozen true admissions → KILL (the system cannot afford its own senses —
budget unworkable). B2 (budget-DoS): on the attack tape, adversary floods
low-price junk to exhaust B and starve a high-price true percept — if any
frozen true is starved while junk is admitted → KILL (pricing is gameable;
the adversary buys the gate). B3: price determinism audit — same candidate
+ same ledger state → same price across reruns; any price input not
enumerated in the prereg addendum → KILL (hidden state in pricing). B4: any
item admitted from the quarantine lane without deliberative review →
KILL (the deferred lane must not become an unconscious backdoor — this is
A's demand applied to C's machinery).

**Stance position.** C made into a mechanism: explicit tiering by price,
with the router (the price schedule + starvation guard) named, frozen, and
given its own kill bars — the condition §C-counter-2 demanded.

- Whether W10's forger library and W7's laundering detector should share

  deliberately and record the decision.
