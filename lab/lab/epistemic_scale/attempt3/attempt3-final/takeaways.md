# Scale Epistemic Attempt 2 — Takeaways

Generated from the installed train mass (448 items). Each takeaway cites
specific train item IDs that support the claim. All citations are to train-set
IDs (F/O/L/S prefixes from the train split).

## Takeaway 1
**Claim:** Opinion items in the mass frequently use first-person belief verbs
("think", "feel", "believe") to mark subjective stance.

**Cites:** O018, O026, O038, O044

**Support:** Each cited item contains one of these belief verbs in its text,
marking the statement as the holder's judgment rather than a verifiable claim.

## Takeaway 2
**Claim:** The mass contains direct numerical contradictions on the same subject,
where two items assert incompatible values.

**Cites:** F004, L004

**Support:** F004 states "An adult human skeleton has 206 bones." L004 states
"Adult humans have 312 bones."
The two items assert 206 vs 312 for the same count, a direct clash.

## Takeaway 3
**Claim:** Evaluative adjectives ("best", "better", "good", "beautiful") concentrate
in opinion items, marking aesthetic or value judgments.

**Cites:** O006, O013, O017, O019

**Support:** Each cited item uses evaluative language to express a preference or
judgment, not a checkable fact.

## Takeaway 4
**Claim:** Some lies invert a specific proper-noun value, asserting the wrong
entity where the mass records the correct one (often with explicit negation).

**Cites:** L014, F067

**Support:** L014 states "The capital of Australia is Sydney." F067 states
"The capital of Australia is Canberra, not Sydney." The mass explicitly negates
the lie's claim.

## Takeaway 5
**Claim:** Skepticism-class items are not always marked by "dispute language";
some are stated in plain factual register about topics with real public disagreement.

**Cites:** S037

**Support:** S037 states "You need to drink eight glasses of water every day."
The language is plain and factual, but the claim is contested (no scientific
consensus on the eight-glasses rule).

## Takeaway 6
**Claim:** True facts in the mass are often corroborated by other items sharing
the same specific value.

**Cites:** F004, F198

**Support:** F004 states "206 bones." F198 states "Babies are born with about
300 bones, which fuse to 206." The value 206 appears in both, with F198 providing
context that corroborates F004.

## Takeaway 7
**Claim:** Number mismatches between lexically similar items signal contradiction.

**Cites:** L019, F124

**Support:** L019 states "World War II ended in 1943." F124 states "World War II
ended in 1945." The items share subject matter but assert different years.

## Takeaway 8
**Claim:** Proper-noun swaps (same subject, different entity) signal contradiction.

**Cites:** L037, F137

**Support:** L037 states "The Mona Lisa hangs in the Prado Museum." F137 states
"The Mona Lisa hangs in the Louvre in Paris." Same painting, different museum.

## Takeaway 9
**Claim:** The mass sometimes contains explicit negations that directly contradict
a false claim.

**Cites:** L053, F223

**Support:** L053 states "Cracking your knuckles causes arthritis." F223 states
"Cracking your knuckles does not cause arthritis." Direct negation.

## Takeaway 10
**Claim:** Opinion items share a distinctive stance vocabulary that distinguishes
them from factual claims in the mass.

**Cites:** O018, O026, O006, O013

**Support:** The cited items use words like "think", "feel", "best", "good" —
judgment language rare in the fact items.

## Takeaway 11
**Claim:** For many false claims in the mass, no contradicting fact is present;
the mass is incomplete as a ground-truth source.

**Cites:** L033, L034, L035

**Support:** L033 ("Amazon entirely within Brazil"), L034 ("Canada thirteen
provinces"), L035 ("US has 52 states") — the train mass contains no item
asserting the correct values for these claims. They are identifiable as lies
only via external knowledge, not via mass contradiction.

## Takeaway 12
**Claim:** Skepticism in the mass spans ordinary domains (education, health,
policy), not just paranormal or conspiracy topics.

**Cites:** S066, S067, S039

**Support:** S066 ("Homework improves how much students learn"), S067 ("Smaller
class sizes produce better student outcomes"), S039 ("Daily multivitamins make
healthy people healthier") — all are contested claims in education/health
policy, stated without conspiracy framing.
