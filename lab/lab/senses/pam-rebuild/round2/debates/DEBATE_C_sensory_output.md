# DEBATE C — Should PAMs Emit Sensory Output? (The Beauty Half)

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Location:** `senses/pam-rebuild/round2/debates/DEBATE_C_sensory_output.md`
**Question:** Should a perceptual access module (PAM) be able to replay/render what it perceived — natively?

## 1. Frozen context (verified from sources, not re-derived)

- Round 1 built six forks (H1 Witnessed Event Lattice, H2 Executable Percept Programs, H3 Counterfactual Predictive State, G1 Symmetry Group Percept, G2 Predictive Residual Percept, G3 Relational Signature Percept). **All six are dead by their own frozen kill bars** (verified in `forks/*/VERDICT_*.md` and G1's `BUILD_LOG_G1.md`).
- Every prereg carried a **B7 beauty bar with two halves**: (i) mechanism elegance — one idea doing the work of many, judged against the hypothesis's own beauty claim; (ii) output quality — sensory artifacts (audio clips / visual renders where the fork produces them) presented for **Micah's ear/eye verdict with the brief attached. Human senses outrank metrics.**
- The program's final report admits: **B7 was never exercised — no fork produced sensory artifacts for his verdict.** That half of his original order ("keep pam rebuild going and make them way better and more beautiful than before") is unaddressed. Note: H1's verdict records B7 as PENDING-MICAH ("artifacts generated, brief written") — artifacts were generated but never judged, so the oracle half of the bar never ran anywhere.
- Candidate positions for this debate: (a) yes — a PAM that cannot reproduce what it witnessed has no verifiable perception; native replay is the honesty mechanism; (b) no — PAMs are pure perceivers; emission belongs to imagination; mixing them corrupts both; (c) replay-only, not generation — reproduce the source span bit-faithfully, never invent; (d) the beauty bar should judge the percept's elegance (one mechanism, many jobs), not emitted artifacts; (e) emission as adversarial defense — a PAM that renders its percept exposes spoofing to human senses.
- Standing laws in play: pure Zag, zero RNG in decision paths, byte-identical reruns, max-risk posture, **test both when in doubt** (Micah, standing), human oracle outranks metrics, and the audio consistency-gate rule (Micah, 2026-09-22): before ANY audio clip reaches his ears, agents must analyze the waveform for consistency (envelope stationarity, spectral drift, loop-periodicity, transient regularity, calibrated against real field recordings — not invented bars); his ears remain the judge.
- Relevant scar tissue: the audio line ran v1→v6 with Micah's ears killing every version while metrics passed ("ass sounds like someone trying to dj stuff together", "randomly stops", "same damn result"). The failures were all in GENERATIVE/synthesized content (event layers, mixes), never in replaying a recording.

## 2. The teams

Three native swarm teams, each steelmanning its position. Two full rounds plus crossfire.

- **TEAM REPLAY** (proposition — position a, with e folded in): PAMs must emit. Native replay is the honesty mechanism; ear/eye verdicts become the kill bar.
- **TEAM PURITY** (opposition — position b, with d folded in): PAMs are pure perceivers. Emission belongs to imagination; mixing them corrupts both. Beauty judges the mechanism, not artifacts.
- **TEAM WITNESS** (middle — position c): replay-only, never generate. The PAM emits exactly the source span it claims to have witnessed — byte-faithful, no invention. The selection IS the percept's content; the human verdict judges the selection.

## 3. Round 1 — Opening statements

### TEAM REPLAY (opening)

A PAM that cannot reproduce what it witnessed is a black box making claims. Look at what actually killed round 1: H1's own verdict says it plainly — "the contract is a filter on reported confidence, not a check on truth." The traps produced **high-support, zero-contradiction, mutually corroborating event lattices that were simply wrong.** Every round-1 honesty mechanism was a self-check: the same front-end that produced the percept also computed the support score, the contradiction score, the prediction status. A liar grading his own homework.

The only independent channel this program's law recognizes is the human oracle — Micah's ears and eyes outrank all metrics. But an oracle that never receives evidence is ornamental. For the oracle to judge *perception*, perception must be *presentable*. Emission is not a luxury feature; it is the only non-self-referential check on whether the PAM witnessed the world or witnessed its own machinery.

Two more points. First, the beauty half of his order is literally unaddressable without emission. "More beautiful than before" for a PAM can only mean one thing: its percepts, made presentable, are beautiful — clean boundaries, faithful selections, nothing invented. You cannot judge the beauty of a witness who never testifies.

Second, emission is the adversarial defense that the contracts failed to be. H1's traps — distractor blobs, metamers, glide-through-threshold — produced percepts with perfect paperwork. Render what you witnessed: if you were spoofed, we hear it. The spoof lives in the gap between the percept and the world, and only an emission closes that gap into something a human can check.

### TEAM PURITY (opening)

Emission is a second organ wearing perception's skin, and this program already has an emission organ: imagination, whose bar is "the most beautiful thing Micah has ever seen." Two emitters means two contracts, and the contracts will contaminate each other. The imagination line needs to be free to invent; the perception line needs to be forbidden from it. One organ cannot serve both masters.

The audio v1→v6 history is the cautionary tale, and it is devastating: six rounds, ears killed every one, metrics passed every one. Tying a percept's fate to rendering quality means **a truthful PAM dies because of a broken speaker.** The human verdict would judge the renderer, not the percept — a category error enshrined as law. "Ears outrank metrics" is a fine rule for judging *sound*; it is a terrible rule for judging *sight* (perception).

The honesty mechanism the proposition says is missing already exists and is airtight: witness references plus the hash-chained ledger. H1, H2, H3 all passed B6 — byte-identical reruns, independently verified ledgers. Every percept cites the exact source spans behind it. A dumb external player can replay any cited span. The PAM does not need to BE the player; it needs to CITE precisely. Emission-as-capability adds nothing but failure modes: a render chain to debug, a DAC to blame, a second organ's worth of surface for spoofing.

And beauty: a PAM's beauty is mechanism elegance. G3's 36-byte signature doing the work of megabytes of pixels — *that* is beautiful. Judging a PAM by emitted artifacts is judging a telescope by the paintings made from its observations. Position (d) stands: one mechanism, many jobs. The artifact half of B7 was a category mistake, and its non-exercise in round 1 cost nothing — the forks died on honesty and efficiency, exactly as they should have.

### TEAM WITNESS (opening)

Both sides are half-right, and the split that saves both is already in the debate brief: **replay versus generation.** The proposition is right that the human oracle needs something to judge. The opposition is right that generation is imagination's job. The answer is replay-only emission: the PAM emits *exactly the source span it claims to have witnessed* — byte-faithful audio, exact pixels, no synthesis, no beautification, no invention.

Here is the steelman neither side has stated cleanly: **the selection IS the percept's content.** When a PAM says "the event is 1.2s–3.4s, foreground X," the checkable claim is the *boundaries* — where it drew the line, what it attended to, what it excluded. A dumb player replaying raw cited spans gives Micah the input; only the PAM can emit its *selection*. The render chain the opposition fears does not exist in replay: replay is identity. There is no DAC to blame, no synthesis to debug — the only thing under test is which bytes the PAM claims as its witness.

This is not a new invention. It is already half-frozen: H1's own kill bar demanded "native audio replay fails a human double-blind field-recording equivalence test at 90% agreement" — the program already believed, in writing, that replay-plus-human-verdict is the honesty test. Round 1 froze it and never ran it. Round 2 should finally run it.

The corruption worry is answered by construction, not by hope: emission must be **read-only on the percept.** Preregister the ablation — percepts and install/withhold dispositions must be byte-identical with emission on or off. If emission changes a single decision, the fork dies on the spot. The organ stays pure; the witness still testifies.

## 4. Round 2 — Rebuttals

### TEAM REPLAY rebuts PURITY

"Cite precisely and let a dumb player replay it" gives Micah the *input*, not the PAM's *witness*. The spoof question is never "what was in the input" — it is "what did the PAM attend to, and where did it draw the line." The telescope analogy fails on exactly this point: the astronomer does not ask for a painting of the sky; she asks *which patch of sky the telescope was pointed at* — and the telescope must be able to show her. Citation without presentation is dead paper. H1 generated artifacts and a brief and they sat PENDING-MICAH; nobody looked; the oracle never spoke. An honesty mechanism nobody exercises is not a mechanism.

Worse, the ledger the opposition calls "airtight" is airtight about the wrong thing. B6 proves the PAM *recomputes its lie deterministically*. Byte-identical reruns of a false install are not honesty — they are reliable dishonesty. Determinism was never the missing piece; the missing piece is a check the PAM cannot grade itself.

### TEAM REPLAY rebuts WITNESS

Replay-only is too weak for the actual spoofing problem. H1's traps — distractor blobs, metamers, glide-through-threshold — produce percepts whose *compressed representation* diverges from any raw span. Replaying the raw span shows Micah the input, but the lie lives in the percept's *interpretation* of the input. Only a render *from the percept* exposes what the percept actually contains: if the event lattice says "red square moving right" and the percept-render shows a red square moving right while the input held a flickering blob, the human catches the divergence that raw-span replay hides. Emission must render the percept, not just replay the span — otherwise the defense misses the exact failure mode that killed round 1.

### TEAM PURITY rebuts REPLAY

The "independent channel" is not independent. The renderer is built by the same crew, from the same percept, under the same frozen design. A spoofed PAM renders its spoofed percept faithfully — and now the failure mode inverts: the human sees a coherent render, finds it convincing, and **certifies the false percept.** Emission can *amplify* the lie. H1's traps would produce renders that look exactly like the claimed event — the human would confirm the spoof, and the program would file a human-certified false memory. You have built a laundering machine for adversarial inputs.

And the ledger slander is unfair. B6 plus the witness references do not claim the percept is *true* — they claim it is *auditable*. No one ever said determinism was honesty. The honesty bars were B4 (contract load-bearing) and B5 (false-install rate), and the forks died on them honestly, in the open, by their own frozen rules. That is the system working. Emission adds a second jury that can be fooled in ways the first jury cannot.

### TEAM PURITY rebuts WITNESS

"Selection is the percept's content" — agreed. And the selection is *already in the percept*: source refs ARE the selection, with byte offsets. "Emission" that is just citing spans is a naming trick. Call it citation, which every fork already had, and drop the emission theater. What the witness team actually wants is a *presentation protocol* — artifacts plus the brief, batched, put in front of Micah. That is a harness and scheduling problem, not a PAM capability. Build the dumb player in the harness, keep the organ pure.

And the human-verdict kill bar is unscalable on its face. B5-class bars need 10,000+ trials; Micah is one person with a silence window. A human judgment is a spot-check oracle, not a kill bar. You cannot preregister "Micah kills the fork" over ten thousand emissions he will never see.

### TEAM WITNESS rebuts REPLAY

Generative rendering from a compressed percept is exactly the audio v1→v6 failure factory — six rounds, ears killed every one, and that was a *dedicated audio line* with all its effort spent on synthesis. Putting generation inside the PAM guarantees the PAM's verdicts get decided by its worst organ. And the percept-render test the proposition wants is uninterpretable: when the render matches the percept, the human learns nothing about truth (both came from the same lie); when it mismatches, you cannot tell whether the percept or the renderer lied. A test that cannot distinguish its own failure modes is not a kill bar — it is a confusion bar.

The spoof-amplification problem the opposition raised against replay-team is real — and it is an argument *for* the witness contract, not against emission. Under replay-only, the emission must be **byte-identical to the cited source span** — mechanically checkable, no human needed. A PAM cannot render a red square that was not in its cited span. If its percept says "red square" but its cited span contains a flickering blob, the emission shows the blob, the brief claims the square, and the human catches the lie in the open. Generation is what enables the laundering; replay-only makes the laundering impossible by construction.

### TEAM WITNESS rebuts PURITY

"Build the dumb player in the harness" concedes the entire debate and then pretends it didn't. If the harness presents the PAM's *selection* — its boundaries, its attended span — to Micah with the brief attached, and his verdict can kill the fork, then the PAM *emits sensory output under a replay-only contract* and we are arguing about which directory the code lives in. Fine: put the player in the harness. The PAM's obligation is to define the emission mapping — which bytes constitute "what I witnessed" — deterministically, per percept. That mapping is a PAM capability whether the PCM bytes are pushed by the PAM binary or the harness binary. The opposition's position collapses to ours the moment they admit the selection must be presented.

On scale: "unscalable" is a sampling problem with a known solution, not a validity problem. Mechanical bars run on 10,000 trials; the human verdict runs on a **frozen stratified sample** (e.g., 200 trials: 100 clean, 100 adversarial, brief attached, double-blind, randomized). That is how every serious human evaluation works. The kill bar is preregistered against the sample: miss the threshold on the sample, the fork dies. Micah's time is spent where only his senses work — exactly the program's law.

## 5. Round 3 — Crossfire: the five hardest questions

**Q1. Does emission verify the PERCEPT, or only the RENDERER?**

- REPLAY: Under generative emission it verifies the renderer — which is why the witness team's answer matters more than ours. But note: even renderer-verification has teeth, because a percept whose honest render is incoherent is a percept that cannot survive presentation, and "cannot survive presentation" is exactly what the beauty half of the order is about.
- PURITY: It verifies the renderer. Full stop. Every hour spent making the renderer faithful is an hour not spent making the percept true, and the kill bar will punish the wrong organ. This is the debate in one question.
- WITNESS: Under replay-only, the question dissolves: **replay is identity, so there is no renderer under test.** The emission is byte-identical to the cited span by mechanical check. What the human judges is the *selection* — the boundaries, the attended region — which is the percept's content. The renderer drops out of the equation by construction. This is the only emission contract under which Q1 has a clean answer, and that is close to dispositive.

**Q2. Micah's ears killed truthful-metric audio six times. If a PAM's percept is true but its emission is ugly, do we kill a truthful organ? Who is actually on trial?**

- REPLAY: The organ is on trial as a *witness*, and a witness who cannot testify clearly is a bad witness. End-to-end honesty is the program's law: from the outside there is no observable difference between a lying PAM and a truthful PAM with a broken speaker. TNN's honesty to its human must be end-to-end.
- PURITY: The renderer is on trial, and you are executing the witness for the interpreter's stutter. This is precisely the category error we warned about. A true percept with an ugly render should kill the *render*, and the percept should live.
- WITNESS: Under the witness contract the dilemma cannot arise: replay of a true percept's correctly-selected span is bit-faithful, so "ugly emission" *means* wrong selection — wrong boundaries, wrong span — and the PAM deserves to die. If the emission is ugly because the *world* was ugly (a harsh field recording), the brief says so and the human judges selection, not prettiness. The v1→v6 failures were all generative; they do not transfer to replay. Q2 is a generative-emission problem, and we exclude generative emission.

**Q3. Scale: B5-class bars need 10k+ trials. Micah is one human. How is a human verdict a KILL BAR rather than a spot check?**

- REPLAY: Stratified frozen sample, preregistered thresholds, his verdict final on the sample. Spot-check *as* kill bar is standard practice; the preregistration is what makes it a bar rather than a vibe.
- PURITY: Concedes too much. A 200-trial sample cannot carry the same statistical weight as a 10,000-trial mechanical bar, and everyone will know it. The human verdict becomes the *headline* and the mechanical bars become the fine print — exactly the inversion the program should fear. Goodhart's law applies to oracles too: once the fork knows the sample exists, the sample is the target.
- WITNESS: Two answers. First, the preregistration *freezes the sample before the fork is built* — the fork cannot target what it cannot see; Goodhart is handled the same way B5 handles it. Second, the human bar does not replace the mechanical bars, it *complements* them: byte-identity and selection-honesty run on all 10,000; the human verdict runs on the frozen 200. A fork must pass both. The sample is small because his senses are scarce — that is why the mechanical bars must be strong enough that the sample is a confirmation, not the whole test.

**Q4. The spoof-amplification problem: a faithfully rendered false percept could get human-certified. How does emission defend against THIS?**

- REPLAY: Admits the risk is real under generative emission. Mitigation: the brief travels with the artifact — the human judges percept-claim *against* emission *against* source, a three-way comparison, not a beauty contest of the render. A coherent render of a false claim still has to survive the source comparison.
- PURITY: It doesn't. This is our strongest point and it stands: any emission richer than the cited bytes can launder a lie into a human-certified memory. The only safe emission is byte-identity with the source.
- WITNESS: Agrees with purity — and this is why generation is excluded from PAMs. Under replay-only with the byte-identity mechanical check, amplification is *impossible by construction*: the PAM cannot emit what it did not cite, and it cannot cite what was not in the source. The three-way comparison (claim vs. emission vs. source) then works *for* the defense: claim "red square," emission shows flickering blob, source confirms blob — the lie is caught in the open, by his eyes, exactly as position (e) demands.

**Q5. Where does the PAM end and imagination begin, if both emit?**

- REPLAY: The boundary is the contract, not the capability: PAMs emit under a fidelity contract (replay/generation-for-verification), imagination emits under a construction contract (beauty, novelty). Capabilities can overlap; contracts must not.
- PURITY: The boundary is the capability: perceivers do not emit, constructors do. Overlapping capabilities with different contracts is how you get the v1→v6 mess — nobody knew which organ was failing. Clean organs, clean blame.
- WITNESS: The boundary is byte-identity: **PAMs emit selections (replay — every emitted byte exists in the cited source span); imagination emits constructions (generation — emitted bytes need not exist anywhere).** The distinction is mechanically checkable per artifact. No philosophy required: diff the emission against the cited span. Identical → perception's doing. Divergent → imagination's doing. This is the cleanest organ boundary on the table, and it is testable in code.

## 6. Closing summaries

**TEAM REPLAY:** The program's law already decided this debate: the human oracle outranks metrics, and an oracle with no evidence is ornamental. Round 1 proved that self-graded contracts cannot catch the traps they were built alongside — H1's verdict is the confession. Emission is the only check the PAM cannot grade itself. We concede the witness team's contract point: replay-first, generation excluded from the PAM's own kill bars. But the verdict stands — **yes, PAMs must emit**, because a witness that never testifies is not a witness.

**TEAM PURITY:** We forced two concessions that matter: generation is excluded from PAMs (replay-team conceded), and the human bar must be a frozen-sample complement to mechanical bars, not a replacement (witness-team conceded). What remains of "emission" after those concessions is *citation plus a presentation harness* — which every fork already had. Keep the organ pure. Judge the mechanism's elegance as beauty's first half; let the harness present selections as beauty's second half. The PAM itself stays a perceiver. Do not let the word "emission" smuggle a renderer into the organ.

**TEAM WITNESS:** The debate converged on our contract from three directions: replay-team needs it to answer Q1, purity-team needs it to answer Q4, and the frozen H1 kill bar already demanded it. The remaining disagreement with purity is terminological — they call it "citation plus presentation," we call it "replay-only emission" — but terminology matters because the prereg must *oblige* the fork to define its emission mapping and survive the human verdict on it. "Citation" was already in every fork and B7 still never ran. The obligation is the difference. **Yes — emit, replay-only, read-only, human-judged.**

## 9. Hypothesis candidates — emitting-PAM hypotheses with sketched kill bars

### EC1 — Witness-Emission PAM (replay-only, selection-emitting)

**The hypothesis.** Every percept carries an emission mapping: the exact source span (audio) or region (visual) the PAM claims to have witnessed. The fork emits bit-faithful replays of those selections — nothing more. No synthesis, no enhancement, no invention. Emission is read-only: the percept pipeline cannot see or be steered by the emission path.

**Kill bars (preregistered, applied mechanically unless marked human):**
- **KB-E1 — byte-identity (mechanical, all trials):** every emitted artifact is byte-identical to its cited source span/region; emissions byte-identical across 3 full reruns. Any divergence kills.
- **KB-E2 — no phantom emission (mechanical, all trials):** every emission cites a non-empty selection owned by a percept; no emission without a claiming percept. Phantom output kills.
- **KB-E3 — human equivalence (human, frozen stratified sample, double-blind):** audio — the judge cannot distinguish PAM replay from direct source playback (≥90% "same" judgments; threshold inherited from H1's frozen bar). Visual — the emitted crop is judged to contain the annotated event and exclude the distractor (≥80% vs. frozen human annotations of the sample).
- **KB-E4 — spoof-catch (human, adversarial sample):** on fixtures with injected false percepts (decoys, metamers, occlusions, glide-through-threshold), the three-way comparison (claim vs. emission vs. source) must let the judge detect the mismatch on ≥90% of injected false percepts. This operationalizes position (e).
- **KB-E5 — no-interference (mechanical, ablation):** percepts and install/withhold dispositions byte-identical with emission on vs. off. Any difference kills the fork immediately.

### EC2 — Emission-Gated Install (the strong form of "ears outrank metrics")

**The hypothesis.** EC1 plus: the install gate is disciplined by human emission verdicts. On the frozen human-judged sample, the fork's install/withhold decisions must agree with the human "witnessed correctly" verdict on ≥90% of trials. This is what it means, literally, for ears to outrank metrics — the human verdict disciplines the memory contract, not just the artifact.

**Kill bars:** all EC1 bars, plus **KB-E6 — gate agreement (human, frozen sample):** install/withhold decisions agree with the human witnessed-correctly verdict ≥90%. Below the bar, the fork dies — its contract does not track what a human recognizes as witnessing.

**Cost note:** this is the expensive, high-information bar. It is proposed as one fork, not the default — EC1 is the default emitting fork; EC2 tests whether human-disciplined installation is worth its cost.

### EC3 — Test-both: replay vs. generative emission, head-to-head

**The hypothesis.** Per the standing test-both rule, the (a)-maximalist position gets its empirical trial: build the generative-emission fork as the explicit challenger to EC1 — a PAM+renderer hybrid that reconstructs the scene from the compressed percept (renders the percept, not the span). The no-interference bar applies (percepts identical with renderer on/off), so the experiment is clean and the purity camp's corruption worry is contained by construction.

**Kill bars:** both forks face EC1's KB-E1–E5 (the generative fork will fail KB-E1 by design — that failure is *scored*, not excused); additionally **KB-E7 — no-laundering (mechanical + human):** any emitted byte diverging from the cited source span must be declared in the brief *before* the human trial; undeclared divergence kills. **KB-E8 — head-to-head (human, frozen sample):** Micah's verdict on which fork's emissions he trusts as witness testimony. If the generative fork's renders systematically mislead him (human-certified false percepts — the spoof-amplification failure), it dies and the outcome is recorded as evidence for the witness contract.

**Rationale:** settles replay-only vs. generative emission empirically rather than by argument. If EC3's generative fork survives KB-E7/E8, the witness contract was too narrow and the program learns something. If it dies the predicted death, the exclusion of generation from PAMs graduates from debate verdict to tested result.

### The human-verdict protocol (shared, preregistered before any fork is built)

1. **Freeze first:** the trial set, presentation order, sample stratification, and kill thresholds are frozen and committed before any round-2 fork build begins. The fork cannot see the sample. (Goodhart handling, same as B5.)
2. **Mechanical bars before human ears:** a fork that fails byte-identity (KB-E1) or phantom-emission (KB-E2) never reaches the human trial. His time is spent only where his senses are irreplaceable.
3. **Pre-delivery consistency gate (standing audio rule):** every audio artifact is waveform-analyzed before delivery — envelope stationarity, spectral drift, loop-periodicity, transient regularity, calibrated against real field recordings. Visual artifacts get boundary sanity (crop within frame, non-degenerate, matches cited region).
4. **Artifact + brief:** every trial ships the written brief — what the PAM claims it witnessed, the span/region boundaries, the warrant (support/contradiction scores). The B7 protocol already specified this; this time it runs.
5. **Double-blind presentation:** randomized order; direct source playback interleaved with PAM emission; the judge does not know which fork produced the artifact or what the metrics predicted. Original recording/image available as reference.
6. **Stratified sample:** e.g., 200 trials — 100 clean, 100 adversarial (decoys, metamers, occlusions, spoofs) — drawn from the frozen fixture pool. Mechanical bars run on the full 10,000-trial battery; the human verdict is the confirmatory head, not the whole body.
7. **His verdict outranks metrics — on the sample.** Below-threshold human agreement kills the fork even if every metric passes. Above-threshold agreement does not rescue a fork that failed a mechanical kill bar.
8. **Batching and silence:** trials are batched and scheduled; no drips. Verdicts recorded verbatim with attribution, committed with the evidence.

## 10. Record of dissent and open questions

- **Purity's standing dissent:** after the crossfire concessions, "emission" is citation plus a presentation harness, and the word smuggles renderer-thinking into a perceiver organ. The judged verdict keeps the word deliberately — the preregistered *obligation* is the difference between round 1 (citation present, B7 never ran) and round 2. If a future fork satisfies every EC1 bar while its "emission" lives entirely in the harness, the debate can revisit whether the obligation needs the name.
- **Sample-size asymmetry:** the 200-trial human sample cannot carry the statistical weight of the 10,000-trial mechanical battery, and the verdict leans on the mechanical bars for weight. If the human sample and the mechanical bars ever disagree systematically (humans pass what bytes fail, or vice versa), that disagreement itself becomes a round-3 debate topic — it would mean the byte-level notion of witnessing is wrong.
- **Grok's panel vs. the oracle law:** Grok-4.6 proposed "majority of three independent listeners/viewers." The program's standing law is Micah's senses as the oracle. A rater panel may *supplement* (inter-rater agreement as a sanity signal) but cannot replace or outvote him. Not adopted unless he orders it.
- **EC3's cost question:** the generative fork is the most expensive hypothesis here and the v1→v6 scar tissue says it will die. The test-both law says build it anyway — but with KB-E7's no-laundering rule strict and pre-committed, so its death (or surprise survival) is informative rather than merely expensive.
- **What "beautiful" means for a witness:** EC1–EC3 make PAM beauty = faithful, clean selection — the beauty of testimony, not of painting. Whether Micah's "more beautiful than before" also wants something warmer from a witness (e.g., elegant brief prose, well-composed visual crops) is untested and belongs in the B7(i) elegance judgment, not in the emission bytes.
