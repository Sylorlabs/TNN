S|# Protecting your Personal Information with Secure Passwords | Homeland Security From email and banking to social media and mobile apps, the average person has a long list of passwords.
S|While keeping track of numerous account logins can be tedious, there could be serious implications if a cybercriminal gains access to your email, financial information, social security number, or even your medical records.
S|Passwords are the most common means of authentication online, and that is why it is critical to use strong passwords and keep them confidential.
S|Here are some the tips below from the Stop.Think.Connect.
S|campaign to help make your passwords stronger and help keep them secure: - Don't use words that can be found in any dictionary of any language and use a combination of upper and lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols.
S|- Don't use passwords that are based on personal information or that can be easily accessed or guessed including birthdays, names of pets, or favorite movies and books that can be found by a quick search on social networking sites.
S|- Use passphrases like "Thispasswdis4myemail!" to help you remember complex passwords.
S|- Write down your passwords and store them in a secure place away from your computer if necessary.
S|For example, passwords locked in your desk drawer are secure, but passwords on a sticky note stuck to the monitor are not.
S|- Use different passwords for different accounts and change them regularly.
S|- Make sure account login pages use encryption including a URL that begins with "https:" instead of "http:".
S|Look for the padlock icon in the browser bar, too.
S|If the padlock icon appears on the webpage, but not in the browser bar, it might just be a graphic that a cybercriminal embedded to trick you into feeling secure.
S|Cybersecurity is a shared responsibility and we are all called on to ACT or Achieve Cybersecurity Together.
S|We know it only takes a single infected computer to potentially infect thousands and perhaps millions of others.
S|But at the end of the day, cybersecurity is ultimately about people.
S|The most impressive and sophisticated technology is worthless if it's not operated and maintained by informed and conscientious users.
S|If each of us commits to staying informed of cybersecurity risks and takes a few simple steps, we can all make a big difference to stay safe online.

H|www.ssa.gov
S|# Protect Your Online Identity With Strong Passwords | Social Security Blog | SSA by Mark Majestic, May 2, 2024 World Password Day is an annual event dedicated to promoting awareness about the importance of strong password security practices.
S|On May 2, people and organizations all over the world will work to encourage better password habits to keep our online data and accounts safe and secure.
S|Here are some tips to help make sure your passwords are strong: - Create passwords with at least 12 characters and include letters, numbers, and symbols.
S|- Instead of passwords made up of random, mixed characters, use phrases that are longer and easier to remember.
S|- Use a unique password for each account to prevent hackers from accessing multiple accounts if one password is stolen.
S|- Change your passwords regularly and avoid using easy to guess information such as birthdays or a pet's name.
S|- Add an extra layer of security to your accounts by using multi-factor authentication, a sign-in process that requires a password plus additional information.
S|That second factor could be a one-time only code sent by text or email.
S|The protection of your online identity is not only about creating passwords that are hard to guess.
S|It's also about being careful how you store and share your passwords.
S|Stay informed about the latest online threats and trends.
S|By being vigilant, you can protect yourself and your online activity from people who want to do harm.
S|Remember, your passwords are the keys to your online identity.
S|So, make sure they are strong, secure, and unique.
