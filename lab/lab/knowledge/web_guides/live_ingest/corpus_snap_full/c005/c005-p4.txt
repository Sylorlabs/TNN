TITLE: Reflection vs Refraction of Light: Key Differences Explained
Light is one of nature's most captivating phenomena, bending, bouncing, and transforming in ways that have puzzled scientists and amazed observers for centuries. Understanding the **difference between reflection and refraction** isn't just academic—it's the key to unlocking the secrets behind everything from why the sky appears blue to how fiber optic cables carry our internet data.

## What Is Reflection of Light?

**Reflection** occurs when light waves bounce off a surface and return to the medium from which they came. Think of it like a ball bouncing off a wall—the light hits a surface and rebounds at the same angle.

### Key Characteristics of Reflection:

- Light stays in the same medium
- Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection
- No change in speed or wavelength
- Creates mirror images and shiny surfaces

The word "reflection" comes from Latin "reflectere," meaning "to bend back"—exactly what light does!

## What Is Refraction of Light?

**Refraction** happens when light passes from one medium to another (like air to water) and changes direction due to a change in speed. This bending of light is responsible for some of the most beautiful optical illusions in nature.

### Key Characteristics of Refraction:

- Light enters a different medium
- Light changes speed and direction
- Wavelength may change
- Creates bending effects and optical illusions

A pencil partially submerged in water appears bent because light refracts as it moves from water to air!

## Side-by-Side Comparison: Reflection vs. Refraction

| Aspect | Reflection | Refraction |

| **Medium Change** | No - stays same | Yes - enters new medium |

| **Light Path** | Bounces back | Bends/changes direction |

| **Speed** | Remains constant | Changes speed |

| **Angle Relationship** | Angle of incidence = angle of reflection | Follows Snell's Law |

| **Common Examples** | Mirrors, shiny metals | Lenses, prisms, water surfaces |
