# Universal vs per-path authority rules — the story team's position

**Position: universal PRINCIPLE, per-path MECHANISMS.** The authority rule
should be stated once, at the principle level, and instantiated
differently on each path — because the thing that justifies each
mechanism (the information structure of the path) differs per path, while
the thing the principle protects (plan authority over generated bytes)
is the same everywhere.

## The universal principle (proposed wording)

1. **Generated output never conditions generation.** The renderer (whatever
   computes the next output unit) reads the plan and its own legitimate
   carried state; it does not read emitted output bytes as input.
2. **Output may be measured against plan-derived expectations** by a
   stateless detector or by deliberation. Measurement is evidence, never
   instruction.
3. **Correction is plan-pure and contractive**: exception repair replaces
   corrupted units with `F(plan)` — a constant map (Lipschitz 0),
   idempotent, one-step. It never invents content or adjusts parameters
   to "compensate."
4. **Plan changes enter only as discrete, deliberated plan events**,
   recorded in the plan's own audit trail. The renderer never sees the
   event; it sees the amended plan.

Everything in the audio hybrid v2 (HYBRID_SPEC.md) is an instance of this:
Piece 1 is (1); Piece 2 is (2)+(3); Piece 3 is (4) — the RESPOND latch
re-enters as plan input, one-shot, octave-quantized, never a renderer
parameter. Story's recommended rule (AUTHORITY_RECOMMENDATION.md) is the
same four clauses with different machinery.

## Why the principle must be universal

The failure it guards against is architectural, not acoustic. The audio
v1 servo failed because output conditioned generation through a
non-contractive loop (involution, 2-cycles, rail pins — Attack 4); the
story (G) renderer fails because output conditioning inverts the
information hierarchy (the plan knows thread structure; the bytes don't —
C1) and makes the past unhealable (C2/F3). Different physics, same
disease: **once emitted bytes become generative input, the plan loses
last word, and no bound on the feedback fixes it** — the hybrid's Attack
11 verdict ("no single loop can both remember and be history-independent")
generalizes: no generative feedback loop can both exploit output and keep
the plan authoritative. Bounds limit damage; they don't restore authority.
That's a claim about feedback, not about audio, so the principle banning
(G) belongs at the universal level.

## Why the mechanisms must be per-path

A universal *mechanism* — "every path shall have a 1024-unit detect window
and an octave-scale event latch" — fails on contact with what story
actually is. Three grounded reasons from this path's mechanics:

**R1 — Different information gaps.** Audio's Piece 3 exists because the
plan *cannot compute* what the synth rendered (the K_PLAN synth↔model
gap, hybrid §5). The sensor reads physics. Story's renderer is a pure
function: the plan can compute every byte it will render, so there is no
gap for a sensor to fill. A mandated story "output-sensing latch" is C3 —
theater or fabrication. The mechanism is justified by the gap; no gap, no
mechanism. (Converse: if a future story renderer gets a real language
model with genuine uncertainty about its own output, *then* a story
sensor earns its keep — the mechanism follows the information structure,
not the mandate.)

**R2 — Different natural units.** Audio's exception window is 1024 samples
because corruption is a waveform-scale phenomenon and the detection
predicate is statistical (RMS bands). Story's corruption is symbolic: a
dropped anchor word, a truncated beat. A 1024-*byte* window is meaningless
across a beat boundary; the natural unit is the *beat*, and the predicate
is exact (anchor presence), not statistical. Forcing audio's window onto
story would miss beat-truncation faults that don't move byte-level
statistics — the mechanism would be *less* safe than the per-path one.

**R3 — Different deliberation surfaces.** Audio's plan event (RESPOND
latch) is a quantized physical measurement re-entering as a pitch value.
Story's plan events are symbolic: thread audits (H2) and external edits
(H3), recorded as new rule codes (r=4 AUDIT_REPAIR, r=5 EXTERNAL_EDIT) in
the existing `seq_r` audit trail. The audit-trail shape already exists
natively in story (`seq_w/seq_b/seq_r` — the planner logs every assignment
decision); audio has no such trail. Per-path mechanisms get to use what
the path already built.

## The steelman for fully-universal rules (and its answer)

**For universal mechanisms**: "One rule, one audit. Per-path mechanisms
multiply the attack surface for clever redefinitions — every path team
will argue its path is special and smuggle (G) back in under a per-path
exemption. The audio battery proved a *specific* architecture (three
pieces); generalizing the principle but not the architecture invites
dilution. And Micah's law is 'no randomness in decision paths' —
universal, no per-path exemptions. Why should authority be different?"

**Answer**: the no-randomness law is universal because randomness is a
*property of the mechanism* (present or absent, checkable everywhere).
Authority mechanisms are *justified by information structure*, which
genuinely differs: audio needs a sensor because of the synth↔model gap;
story must not have one because there is no gap. A universal mechanism
mandate doesn't prevent smuggling — it *causes* it: path teams forced to
install theater will either leave it dead (audit noise) or wire it to
something real (fabrication risk, F8). The anti-dilution protection is not
"same machinery everywhere" but "same principle, with each mechanism
required to name the information gap it fills" — a universal
*justification burden*. Any path team proposing output→plan contact must
show, in writing, what information the output contains that the plan
cannot compute. Audio can (waveform physics). Story cannot (pure
function) — so story gets no latch, honestly, rather than a decorative
one.

**For fully-per-path rules** (no universal principle at all): "If
mechanisms differ anyway, why not let each path team derive its own rule
from scratch? A universal principle is just poetry over divergent
practice."

**Answer**: because the (G)-ban needs to hold on paths nobody has attacked
yet. The next path team (image? video?) shouldn't have to re-derive from
zero that generative feedback destroys plan authority — the audio battery
paid for that theorem (Attack 11) and the story analysis extends it to
symbolic generation (F1–F4). The universal principle is the *accumulated
verdict*; per-path mechanisms are its *instantiation*. Poetry would be a
principle with no teeth; these four clauses have teeth — clause (1) alone
kills every (G) proposal on every path, and clause (3)'s contractivity
requirement (Lipschitz 0, idempotent) is a checkable bar for any exception
mechanism on any path.

## Concrete proposal for the authority question

Adopt the four-clause principle as **universal law** (same standing as
no-randomness: checkable, no exemptions). Require each generation path to
publish a **per-path instantiation** naming:

1. what the "plan" is (beat sheet / score / scene graph / …),
2. the exception detector: unit, predicate, correction map — with the
   contractivity argument (constant map, or a proven contraction),
3. the plan-event surface: what discrete deliberated events may amend the
   plan, and where they're audited — plus the **information-gap
   justification**: what does output contain that the plan cannot compute,
   or "none — no sensor installed" (story's honest answer),
4. the (G)-ban restated for the path's renderer: what "the renderer has no
   eyes" means concretely there.

Story's instantiation is AUTHORITY_RECOMMENDATION.md; its gap justification
is "none — the renderer is a pure function, so the plan is complete with
respect to its own output; the only output facts are fault facts (Piece 2)
and outside-edit facts (plan events)."

## One-line version for Micah

The *law* is universal (output never generates; correction is plan-pure;
plan changes are deliberated events) because the disease — feedback
stealing the plan's last word — is architectural. The *machinery* is
per-path because the cure has to fit the information structure: audio
needs a sensor (the plan can't compute waveforms); story must not have
one (the plan computes every byte). Universal mechanisms would mandate
theater; per-path principles would re-litigate the theorem on every path.
