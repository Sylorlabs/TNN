The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It does not produce its own light. Instead, it reflects sunlight. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different 
portions of its sunlit side. This creates the different **phases of the Moon**. Understanding Moon phases helps us know why the Moon looks different on 
different nights.

## 1. Why Does the Moon Change Its Shape?

The Moon does not actually change its shape. It always remains a sphere (ball-shaped). What changes is how much of the Moon's sunlit side we can see from Earth.

- **The Moon orbits Earth:** It takes about **29.5 days** (roughly one month) to complete one orbit around Earth.

- **The Sun lights up half the Moon:** Just like Earth, half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun.

- **Our view changes:** As the Moon moves around Earth, the lit portion that we see from Earth changes daily.

- **Moon phases:** These different shapes we see are called the **phases of the Moon** or **lunar phases**.

## 2. The Eight Main Phases of the Moon

The Moon goes through **eight major phases** during its monthly cycle. Each phase has a specific name and appearance.

### 2.1 New Moon

- The Moon is between the Earth and the Sun.

- The sunlit side faces away from Earth.

- We **cannot see the Moon** in the sky during this phase.

- The Moon rises and sets with the Sun.

- This is the **starting phase** of the lunar cycle.

### 2.2 Waxing Crescent

- **Waxing** means the lit portion is growing or increasing.

- A small, curved sliver of light appears on the **right side** of the Moon.

- This crescent shape is thin and looks like the letter "C" reversed.

- Visible in the evening sky after sunset.

### 2.3 First Quarter

- Exactly **half of the Moon** appears lit from Earth.

- The **right half** is illuminated (in the Northern Hemisphere).

- Called "First Quarter" because the Moon has completed **one-quarter** of its orbit around Earth.

- The Moon is at a 90-degree angle from the Sun as seen from Earth.

- Visible from afternoon until midnight.

### 2.4 Waxing Gibbous

- **Gibbous** means more than half but not fully lit.

- More than half of the Moon is visible and the lit portion is still **growing**.

- The Moon appears almost full but is not completely round.

- Visible most of the night.

### 2.5 Full Moon

- The **entire face** of the Moon visible from Earth is lit.

- Earth is between the Sun and the Moon.

- The Moon looks like a **complete, bright circle**.

- Rises at sunset and sets at sunrise.

- The **brightest phase** of the Moon.

- Occurs approximately **15 days** after the New Moon.

### 2.6 Waning Gibbous

- **Waning** means the lit portion is shrinking or decreasing.

- More than half of the Moon is visible, but the lit area is **getting smaller**.

- The left side starts to darken gradually.

- Visible most of the night and early morning.

### 2.7 Last Quarter (Third Quarter)

- Exactly **half of the Moon** appears lit again.

- This time, the **left half** is illuminated (in the Northern Hemisphere).

- Called "Last Quarter" because the Moon has completed **three-quarters** of its orbit.

- Visible from midnight until morning.

### 2.8 Waning Crescent

- A small, curved sliver of light appears on the **left side** of the Moon.

- The lit portion continues to **decrease**.

- This crescent shape looks like the letter "C".

- Visible in the early morning before sunrise.

- After this phase, the cycle returns to **New Moon**.

## 3. Understanding Waxing and Waning

These two terms help describe whether the Moon is growing brighter or darker from our view on Earth.

- **Waxing:** The illuminated portion of the Moon is **increasing**. Occurs from New Moon to Full Moon. We see more of the lit side each night.

- **Waning:** The illuminated portion of the Moon is **decreasing**. Occurs from Full Moon to New Moon. We see less of the lit side each night.

### 3.1 Easy Way to Remember

- **Right side lit = Waxing (Growing):** If the right side of the Moon is lit, the Moon is waxing.

- **Left side lit = Waning (Shrinking):** If the left side of the Moon is lit, the Moon is waning.

- Think of the letter **"D"** for the shape when waxing (right side curved).

- Think of the letter **"C"** for the shape when waning (left side curved).

- **Lunar Cycle:** The complete period of all Moon phases from one New Moon to the next. Takes approximately **29.5 days**.

- **Crescent:** When less than half of the Moon is visible. Appears as a thin, curved shape.

- **Quarter:** When exactly half of the Moon is visible from Earth.

- **Gibbous:** When more than half but not all of the Moon is visible. Appears oval or egg-shaped.

- **Orbit:** The path the Moon follows as it moves around Earth.

- **Satellite:** An object that revolves around a planet. The Moon is Earth's natural satellite.

## 5. Common Student Mistakes (Trap Alerts)

- **Mistake 1:** Thinking the Moon produces its own light. *Correction:* The Moon only **reflects sunlight**. It does not generate light.

- **Mistake 2:** Believing the Moon changes its actual shape. *Correction:* The Moon is always spherical. Only our **view of the sunlit portion** changes.

- **Mistake 3:** Confusing "Quarter" with appearance. *Correction:* First Quarter and Last Quarter are named for the Moon's **position in its orbit** (1/4 and 
3/4), not because we see one-quarter of the Moon. We see **half** the Moon during Quarter phases.

- **Mistake 4:** Thinking Moon phases are caused by Earth's shadow. *Correction:* Moon phases are caused by the **changing angle** between the Sun, Moon, and 
Earth. Earth's shadow causes **lunar eclipses**, not phases.

## 6. Why Do We Always See the Same Side of the Moon?

- The Moon takes the same time to **rotate once on its axis** as it takes to **orbit Earth once**.

- Both take approximately **27.3 days**.

- This is called **synchronous rotation** or **tidal locking**.

- As a result, we always see the **same face** of the Moon from Earth.

- The side we cannot see is called the **"far side"** of the Moon (not "dark side" because it receives sunlight too).

Understanding Moon phases helps us appreciate the beautiful dance between the Sun, Earth, and Moon. Remember that the Moon itself does not change-only our view
