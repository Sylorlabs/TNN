TITLE: The Great 3 Laws of Motion According to Newton
The 3 Laws of Motion: Sir Isaac Newton is famous for one story above all others: an apple falling from a tree. But the real breakthrough that grew out of that moment wasn’t just about gravity. Newton went on to work out three simple rules that explain how everything around us moves, from a football rolling across a playground to a rocket blasting into space. Together, these became Newton’s three laws of motion, first published in 1687 and still taught in primary and secondary classrooms across the UK today.

This guide breaks each law down in plain English, with no scary formulas or dense physics jargon. It’s built for Year 5 and Year 6 pupils studying forces as part of the UK National Curriculum, KS3 pupils meeting the topic in more depth, and any parent trying to make sense of a homework sheet covered in arrows and equations. Every law comes with a playground example, a classroom-friendly explanation, and a look at why the idea still matters more than 300 years on.

## What Are Newton’s Three Laws? (The Cheat Sheet)

In short: things at rest stay at rest until something pushes or pulls them; the amount something speeds up depends on how hard you push it and how heavy it is; and every push has an equal pushback in the opposite direction. Those three ideas, inertia, force and reaction, cover almost everything you’ll ever see move.

**Table 1: Newton’s Three Laws at a Glance**

| The Law | What It Means | A Playground Example | Key Word |

| First Law (Inertia) | An object stays still, or keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed, until a force acts on it | A football on the grass stays put until someone kicks it | Inertia |

| Second Law (Force and Acceleration) | The bigger the force, the faster something speeds up; the heavier it is, the harder it becomes | Pushing a loaded wheelbarrow takes more effort than pushing an empty one | Acceleration |

| Third Law (Action and Reaction) | Every push or pull has an equal push or pull back the other way | Jumping off a skateboard sends the board shooting backwards | Reaction |
