S|# Growing Gardeners: Mighty Seed Activity Kids often think of seeds as "baby plants." This activity will help clear up the confusion around how a seed becomes a plant.
S|For this activity, you will need: A few large seeds (Green bean, kidney bean, and pumpkin make great choices.
S|Smaller seeds will work, but it may be harder to see the results.) - Paper towel - Plastic bag - Tape - A sunny window - Water - Cup or bowl (optional) First, soak your seeds overnight in a cup or bowl of water (just enough water to cover the seeds).
S|This will help "wake up" the seed.
S|This step is optional but will help speed up the results.
S|Next, dampen a paper towel with water and lay it flat on a counter or table.
S|Remove the seeds from the bowl and place them on the paper towel.
S|Place 3–4 seeds on the paper towel.
S|Using several will help ensure that you will see at least one sprout.
S|Bean seeds are an easy seed to germinate with kids.
S|Helen Erdner, Penn State Master Gardener Fold the paper towel over the seeds and gently slide the paper towel into the bag.
S|Seal the bag and tape it to a sunny window.
S|Make sure you seal the bag tightly so that no water can escape.
S|Wait and watch what happens!
S|Depending on what seeds you choose, you should seed sprouts within a week.
S|You may want to have your child keep a journal of what is happening with their seed.
S|They can date each page, draw a picture, and maybe even write down some of their observations.
S|Seeds waiting to germinate.
S|Helen Erdner, Penn State Master Gardener So, what exactly is going on?
S|Seeds have a coating on them called a seed coat.
S|When the dry seed coat comes in contact with moisture, either in the soil or in the paper towel used in our activity, it begins to absorb water, and the seed begins to expand.
S|Then, the seed coat cracks open.
S|Inside the seed is an embryo that is made up of a small root and a small shoot.
S|The root emerges first in a process called germination.
S|As the root emerges, it anchors the seed to the ground and begins to take in more water.
S|As the root absorbs more and more water, the shoot will emerge.
S|With this experiment, your child will be able to see the root emerge from the soil first, and then the shoot follows.
S|As the shoot begins to emerge, you can transfer your seeds to a soil medium, such as potting soil, and continue to watch your plant grow!
S|Seeds germinating - growing roots and breaking out of the seed coat.
S|Helen Erdner, Penn State Master Gardener

S|# Germination Exploration ## Overview Old seed packets make the perfect tools for exploring seed germination .
S|##### Grade Level/Range: 2-5 Objective: Students will learn: • A seed contains a new plant.
S|• Seeds need certain conditions to grow and not all seeds need the same conditions .
S|• Some seeds may not germinate .
S|Time: 1-2 weeks Materials: • seed packets • plastic sandwich bags • paper towels • planting containers such as small pots, paper or plastic cups • potting soil ### Background Information The new plant (also known as the embryo) is found inside the seed and lies in a dormant state protected by a seed coat until exposed to certain conditions .
S|The sprouting process is called germination .
S|Most seeds just need moisture and warm temperatures for germination .
S|When they are exposed to these two environmental factors, they begin to grow.
S|##### Some seeds need special treatment beyond moisture and warm temperatures for ##### germination to begin .
S|Two common treatments needed include exposure to cold ##### temperatures (also called stratification) and exposure to conditions that cause chemical or mechanical damage to the seed coat (also known as scarification).
S|##### These special treatments evolved as survival mechanisms .
S|For example, many ##### seeds of northern plants need a certain amount of exposure to cold temperatures before they germinate .
S|If a seed dropped to the ground in the fall, would it be a good idea for it to begin growing and be a young seedling right before winter arrived?
S|Of course not, which is why this builtin need for a period of cold before sprouting is so important.
S|Other seeds germinate better in dark conditions (example is a pansy seed) and others need light exposure to germinate (example is a lettuce seed).
S|When you purchase seeds, you will most likely find planting instructions on the seed packet.
S|If you collect seeds from nature, you may need to research their germination requirements online .

S|# Understanding Seed Germination for 10-Year-Olds: What Makes a Seed Grow?
S|/ Learning Corner ## What is Seed Germination?
S|Seed germination is the process by which a seed starts to grow into a new plant.
S|This happens when the right conditions meet, allowing the seed to wake up from its dormant state.
S|## Key Factors That Make a Seed Germinate 1.
S|**Water:** Seeds need water to germinate.
S|When a seed is planted in the soil and it gets wet, it absorbs the water.
S|This process is called *imbibition*, and it helps the seed swell and breaks down stored food inside the seed.
S|**Temperature:** The right temperature is important for seeds to germinate.
S|Most seeds prefer a warm environment, typically between 65°F to 75°F (18°C to 24°C).
S|If it’s too cold or too hot, the seed may not germinate at all.
S|**Oxygen:** Seeds need oxygen to grow.
S|During germination, the seed uses stored energy to start growing, and it requires oxygen to do this.
S|Normally, there is enough air in the soil to give the seed the oxygen it needs.
S|**Light:** Some seeds need light to germinate, while others prefer darkness.
S|Generally, small seeds like lettuce need light, whereas larger seeds like beans do not.
S|It depends on the type of seed.
S|## The Germination Process Once a seed has absorbed enough water, warmed up, and, depending on its type, feels it’s in the right light conditions, it begins the germination process.
S|Here’s what happens step by step: 1.
S|The seed expands and cracks open.
S|The embryonic root, called the **radicle**, pushes down into the soil to anchor the new plant and absorb water and nutrients.
S|Next, the stem, or **shoot**, pushes up towards the sunlight.
S|As the shoot breaks through the soil, the first leaves, known as **cotyledons**, begin to grow.
S|These leaves provide energy to the growing plant until it can produce its leaves.
S|## Conclusion In summary, seeds germinate when they have enough water, the right temperature, oxygen, and sometimes light.
S|This exciting process is the beginning of new life, leading to beautiful plants that provide us with food, oxygen, and much more!
S|## Frequently Asked Questions **Q: Why is germination important?** A: Germination is important because it is how new plants begin to grow.
S|Without it, there would be no new trees, flowers, or crops.
S|Here are some easy-to-understand reasons why germination matters: It makes food: Germination grows the plants that become fruits, vegetables, grains, and seeds we eat.
S|It gives us air: Plants made from seeds produce oxygen, which people and animals need to breathe.
S|It helps animals: Many animals eat plants or live in them (like birds in trees), so germination helps make homes and food for animals.
S|It keeps soil healthy: Plant roots hold soil in place so it doesn't wash away when it rains.
S|It keeps nature balanced: New plants help forests and meadows stay full of different kinds of animals and plants (this is called biodiversity).
S|It helps people: Farmers, gardeners, and many jobs depend on new plants growing from seeds.
S|So, germination is the first step that leads to everything plants do for the planet and for us.
