# Second-opinion request — TNN audio de-synthesis line, planner calibration wall

You are acting as an approach-skeptic / design critic. Below is the full context; you see only this file.

## The system
We are building TNN, a native (non-LLM) AI architecture. One line of the program is **audio de-synthesis**: given a reference recording, a **planner** predicts a sequence of synthesis actions (each action = a timbre atom + an F0 pitch contour + an amplitude envelope class), and a **deterministic renderer** (pure code, no randomness, no trig libraries — strict integer/fixed-point math) renders the plan to a WAV. A perceptual score `err_pm` (lower is better) measures the render against the reference; pitch is verified with a YIN tracker run over the rendered WAV. Everything is byte-identical across reruns.

A **probe** is our measurement instrument: we feed the renderer known inputs (e.g. a flat constant-F0 contour through each atom) and measure what comes out (actual F0 track, harmonic amplitudes). **Calibration** maps "what the planner asks for" to "what the renderer will actually produce," using probe measurements.

## The residual (round 2 white-box, committed)
Three test fixtures — t8, t12, t14 — share one mechanism, diagnosed as **dishonest contour-action predictions**: the planner's calibration was built from a **flat probe** (constant-F0 contours only), and the planner acts as if that calibration transfers to real, moving contours. It does not — the probe's measured behavior is contour-dependent (**measured probe bias**), and the flat-probe calibration is a fantasy when the contour moves. So the planner confidently predicts contours the renderer cannot realize, and the score suffers.

## The failed repair (the anti-pattern — must NOT ship)
A 3-part "honest prediction" fix was tried: it made the planner's predictions honest about what flat-probe calibration actually supports. Battery mean err_pm improved 425 → 380.5, t8 improved 816 → 500, t12 went 172 → 0 — **but t14 regressed 146 → 513 and t11 regressed 80 → 588**. It fixed one fixture by shifting the bias, not by removing it. A repair that wins one fixture while badly regressing others is our canonical failure mode and is not shippable. Standing law: repairs must be **broad mechanisms**, never per-case patches; an honest, well-evidenced "unfixable at this layer" verdict beats a trade-off that ships.

## What we need from you
1. **Critique the "honest prediction" direction**: is it attacking the right layer, or is it papering over a deeper problem (e.g. the probe is the wrong instrument, or the planner's action space can't express what the renderer needs)?
2. **Propose 2–3 genuinely different broad mechanisms** for probe generalization — e.g. calibrating per contour class rather than flat-only; having the planner predict directly in probe-measured space; closing the loop (render → measure → replan) instead of open-loop calibration. For each, name the key assumption it bets on and the measurement that would falsify it.
3. **Approach-skeptic check**: what single experiment best distinguishes "calibration transfer failure" (fix the calibration) from "the probe is the wrong instrument" (fix the measurement)? Be concrete about the experimental setup.

Keep your answer structured and concrete. No flattery, no hedging — if a direction is weak, say so and name the mechanism-level reason.
