# Adjudication: meter crew's "refutation" of the Build 0 vibrato bug — REJECTED

Date: 2026-09-24. The meter crew's final report claims the 2π vibrato bug
was "refuted": "beta in phase-cycles is correct (0.696 cyc × 5.5 Hz = 15 cents)".

## The meter crew's math error

Their computation 0.696 × 5.5 = 3.83 Hz drops the 2π from the time derivative.

The renderer computes total phase (in cycles) as:
  phase(t) = f0·t + beta·sin(2π·fv·t),  with beta = df/fv (cycles)

Instantaneous frequency is d(phase)/dt:
  f(t) = f0 + beta·2π·fv·cos(2π·fv·t)

Peak frequency deviation = beta·2π·fv = (df/fv)·2π·fv = **2π·df**.

For the planned ±15-cent vibrato at 440 Hz: df = 3.83 Hz, fv = 5.5 Hz,
beta = 0.696 cycles. Actual peak deviation = 0.696 × 2π × 5.5 = **24.06 Hz**
= 6.28 × 3.83 Hz. The bug is real; the "refutation" differentiated
sin(2π·fv·t) as if it were sin(fv·t).

## The meter crew's own measurement confirms the bug

Their report states: "the renderer's 15c/5.5 Hz PM is modulation index
4.37 rad". Modulation index m = peak phase deviation in radians =
beta_cycles × 2π = 0.696 × 2π = 4.37 rad. A true ±15-cent vibrato at
5.5 Hz has m = df/fv = 0.696 rad. **m = 4.37 rad IS 2π too large** —
their measurement agrees with the audit, their conclusion contradicts
their measurement.
