# 1. What IS a Fraction?


##### Imagine you have a whole pizza. You're hungry, but your friend is too. So you cut the pizza


##### into 2 equal pieces. Each piece is a HALF of the pizza.


##### That's a fraction! A fraction is a way of talking about a PART of something that's been split


##### into EQUAL pieces.


One WHOLE pizza:


One pizza, cut in HALF — you eat one half:


One pizza, cut into 4 EQUAL pieces — you eat one piece:


##### Big Idea


A fraction tells you about EQUAL parts of a whole.

When the pizza is cut into 4 equal pieces and you eat 1 piece, you ate ONE FOURTH of the pizza.

Numerator — the TOP number. It says how many parts you have.

# 4. Meet the Fraction Family


## Here's a whole family of fractions, all shown as bars. Each bar is the same TOTAL size — but


## split into different numbers of pieces.


1 whole (no fraction) Halves (1/2)Thirds (1/3)Fourths or quarters (1/4)Fifths (1/5)Sixths (1/6)Eighths (1/8)Tenths (1/10)

##### Notice this!


The bigger the bottom number (denominator), the SMALLER each piece is.

One eighth of a pizza is much smaller than one half of a pizza — even though the WHOLE pizza is the same size!


# 5. Same Fraction, Different Look


##### Some fractions look different on paper but show the SAME AMOUNT. We call these


##### EQUIVALENT fractions.


Look at these: 1/2 — half is shaded


2/4 — half is shaded (the SAME amount, just cut into more pieces!)


4/8 — STILL half!


##### Cool Fact


1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 5/10


All of these are exactly the same amount! They're all HALF.


##### How do you make an equivalent fraction? Just multiply (or divide) the top AND the bottom


##### by the same number.


\[

Example: \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{2}{2} = \frac{2}{4} (multiply top and bottom by_2)Example: \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{25}{25} = \frac{75}{100} (multiply top and
 bottom by 25)

\]

# 6. Fractions Live on the Number Line, Too!


##### Fractions aren't just for cutting pizza. They're real NUMBERS — and they sit on the number


##### line between the whole numbers.


A number line from 0 to 1, cut into halves:


│ │ │ 0 1/2 1


Cut into fourths:


│ │ │ │ │ 0 1/4 2/4 3/4 1


Cut into eighths:


│ │  │  │  │ │  │  │  │ 0 1/2 1


##### Notice that 1/2 lines up with 2/4 and with 4/8 — they're all in the same SPOT on the


##### number line. That's exactly why we call them equivalent!

When the TOP number is the same (both are "1"), the fraction with the SMALLER bottom number is bigger.
