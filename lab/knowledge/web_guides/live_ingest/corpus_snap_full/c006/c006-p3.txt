TITLE: Speed of Light
# Speed of Light

### Introduction

Light can travel a distance comparable to seven and onehalf times around the Earth in one second. The speed of light in a vacuum is usually denoted by the letter c and has a value approximately equal to 3×108 m/s. When light moves through some medium, such as air or glass, it slows down.

## Procedure

Wear laser safety goggles. Handle goggles with care, avoid touching the lens.

Do not look directly into the laser beam or shine the laser in anyone’s eyes.

Do not touch the mirrors. They are extremely delicate.

In this experiment, a laser will be the source of light. A beam splitter, which reflects a portion of the beam and allows a portion to pass though, is used to create two separate beams. The reflected beam is directed along a short path (A-F-2) to the photodetectors.

The beam that passes through the splitter is directed down a long path (A-B-C-D-E-1) to the photodetectors (see Figure 1).

The intensity of the laser is modulated at a frequency of 2 MHz. When measured by a photodetector, which has been connected to an oscilloscope, the laser output will appear on the scope as a 2 MHz waveform. The waveform on channel 1 comes from the beam following the short path striking a photodetector. The waveform on channel 2 comes from the beam following the long path striking a photodetector. The time difference between the peaks of the two waveforms can be used to determine the time it took the beam to travel the additional distance of the long path.

1. Measure the length of the short path (A-G-H) using a meter stick. Make the measurement in centimeters and convert to meters (1 cm = 0.01 m). The total distance will be the sum of each segment length.

2. Measure the length in meters of the long path using the long measuring tape. The total distance will be the sum of each segment length.

3. When you look at the oscilloscope, you will see two distinct waveforms that resemble a typical sine wave. You will notice that the two waves are “out of phase”. This means that the waves are reaching the detector at different times.

You can measure the time difference using the oscilloscope.
