S|The SI base units are the standard units of measurement defined by the International System of Units (SI) for the seven base quantities of what is now known as the International System of Quantities: they are notably a basic set from which all other SI units can be derived.
S|The seven SI base units are: the second (symbol s) for time, the metre (symbol m, also spelled meter) for length or distance, the kilogram (symbol kg) for mass, the ampere (symbol A) for electric current, the kelvin (symbol K) for thermodynamic temperature, the mole (symbol mol) for amount of substance, and the candela (symbol cd) for luminous intensity.
S|The SI base units form a set of mutually independent dimensions as required by dimensional analysis commonly employed in science and technology.
S|The names and symbols of SI base units are written in lowercase, except the symbols of those named after a person, which are written with an initial capital letter.
S|For example, the metre has the symbol m, but the kelvin has symbol K, because it is named after Lord Kelvin and the ampere with symbol A is named after Andre-Marie Ampere.
S|On 20 May 2019, as the final act of the 2019 revision of the SI, the BIPM officially introduced new definitions of the SI base units, replacing the preceding definitions.
S|The second, symbol s, is the SI unit of time.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the caesium frequency, the unperturbed ground-state hyperfine transition frequency of the caesium 133 atom, to be 9192631770 when expressed in the unit Hz, which is equal to s^-1.
S|The metre, symbol m, is the SI unit of length.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the speed of light in vacuum c to be 299792458 when expressed in the unit m s^-1, where the second is defined in terms of the caesium frequency.
S|The kilogram, symbol kg, is the SI unit of mass.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Planck constant h to be 6.62607015 x 10^-34 when expressed in the unit J s.
S|The ampere, symbol A, is the SI unit of electric current.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the elementary charge e to be 1.602176634 x 10^-19 when expressed in the unit C, which is equal to A s.
S|The kelvin, symbol K, is the SI unit of thermodynamic temperature.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Boltzmann constant k to be 1.380649 x 10^-23 when expressed in the unit J K^-1.
S|The mole, symbol mol, is the SI unit of amount of substance.
S|One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 x 10^23 elementary entities.
S|This number is the fixed numerical value of the Avogadro constant, N_A, when expressed in the unit mol^-1 and is called the Avogadro number.
S|The candela, symbol cd, is the SI unit of luminous intensity in a given direction.
S|It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 x 10^12 Hz to be 683 when expressed in the unit lm W^-1.
