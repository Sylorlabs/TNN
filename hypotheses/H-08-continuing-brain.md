# H-08 — Anti-self-destruction: one continuing brain, no newborn restarts

## Claim
A capability gain does not buy the right to destroy an accepted capability. One
developmental lineage continues; accepted floors are explicit regression
constraints; new structures begin shadowed and reversible.

## Why it matters
It makes the research deliberately hard — no restarts to hide interference —
which is why retention/preservation is the central empirical battleground.

## Evidence
Mostly negative/mixed, honestly reported:

| Experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| E51AH (native) | Preservation replay **failed the frozen zero-loss development gate**: global replay lost 236 union successes, rescued 6; local lost 121, rescued 159; stages 109/110 stayed sealed |
| E51AI (native) | Replay reduced anchor losses 22→9, but real history gained known cases while losing no-unique preservation |
| E51AJ (native, 1.1M probe rows, 3 replicas, independently verified) | Replay lowered ever-lost and worst-loss in every replica but **failed the all-three-replica endpoint retention rule** (final anchor losses 1/14, 9/11, 17/6) and **failed the no-final-behavioral-tradeoff rule** (known reachability decreased in every replica) |
| E51AJ A-only arm | Continued fitting lost shared-anchor successes in all replicas *including its own cohort* — damage without any cohort alternation |

The repo's reading: "endpoint recovery can disagree with cumulative
disruption"; "aggregate gains never cancel pointwise or cohort-specific
damage." Neither exact preservation nor memory necessity is established.
Cumulative disruption ≠ endpoint recovery: replay consistently lowers
ever-lost/worst-loss counts yet fails endpoint retention rules.
