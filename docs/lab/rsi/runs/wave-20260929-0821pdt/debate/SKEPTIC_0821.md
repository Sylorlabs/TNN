The skeptic accepts this provenance and attacks the claim itself.

The skeptic argues AGAINST adoption, or for a narrower verdict:

1. The "experiment" was never run. The frozen claim covers
   selection plus trace, but no selected experiment was ever
   executed against a hidden law and no hypothesis was ever
   refuted. Calling this "experiment invention" banks the easy
   half (ranking) and queues the hard half (H-EXP2). A selection
   that never touches a world is a ranking function, not an
   experiment.
2. The hypothesis pairs are authored by the same author as the
   implementation. P3's "generalization" is generalization across
   data files written by the same hand in the same hour, not across
   authors or domains. The anti-hardcoding evidence is real but
   shallow.
3. The prereg was amended after freezing. The amendment was
   transparent and pre-execution, but a frozen fixture set that
   needed repair before first use shows the freeze discipline was
   weaker than claimed. The skeptic asks the judge to record this
   as a hygiene demerit, not a pass.
4. Enumerate-and-select over 258 sequences in a 3-variable world is
   the same family as procedure v1's enumerate-and-select over 1055
   programs, which the morning red team classified bounded L2+.
   "Toward Level D" risks implying progress on the L-scale that the
   evidence does not support: the module never obtains missing
   information by acting. Level D requires acting; this module only
   proposes.
5. Only three pairs were tested. The CHECKED counts (32, 10) match
   hand computation, which is good, but the test surface is three
   hand-built pairs. One adversarial pair designed by a second
   author (not the implementer) would be the real test, and it was
   not done.

The skeptic asks the judge to either REJECT adoption until H-EXP2
executes a selection against a hidden law, or ADOPT with the
classification narrowed to "bounded discriminating-sequence
selection (no world contact)" and the amendment recorded as a
demerit.
