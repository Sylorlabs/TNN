## S1: F3a BUILD-PASS must not stand; the bar text controls

Provenance: prereg new this wave; implementation inherited; evidence
new. The numbers for K-F3-1/2/3 are not disputed. The dispute is
K-F3-4, and the dispute is textual, which is exactly where bars live
or die.

The bar text: "'w' occurs in none of the frozen T, F1, or R fixture
inputs (audit of the frozen fixture strings)." The audit found 'w' in
"xqw", a frozen F1-reuse fixture input. The bar fails literally. The
advocate's rescue imports a bullet-taxonomy distinction ("F1" vs
"F1-reuse") that the bar text does not make. That is a post-hoc
reinterpretation to save a verdict, and the owner's rule is explicit:
never weaken a frozen kill bar to force a pass. If "F1" needed to
mean "the F1 counterexample only", the prereg should have said so
before the run. It did not.

Deeper: the bar's intent is that the adversary byte be disjoint from
the learner's frozen experience. The learner experienced 'w' (in
"xqw", P7, before P8). A byte the learner has seen is not an
adversary byte in the sense the judge ordered. The advocate's
"mechanistically inert" argument is a substantive claim about the
diagnosis operator, not a reading of the bar; it belongs in a
re-frozen bar, not in a verdict.

Further, the audit exposes that the parent prereg's allowed-set
rationale is inaccurate: j, l, n, o, t, u all occur in R inputs, and
w occurs in the F1-reuse fixture. Only {i,k,m,r,v} are genuinely
absent from all frozen fixture inputs ('i' already spent on F2). The
adversary-byte hygiene of the whole lane needs re-freeze, not a
pass on a technicality.

The frozen verdict rule says: any bar failing means BUILD-FAIL with
the killing line cited. The killing line is K-F3-4: 'w' in "xqw".
The verdict is BUILD-FAIL. This is not an indictment of the
mechanism: K-F3-1/2/3 passing with a clean white-box trace shows the
mechanism behaved correctly; the kill is on the family/bar design.
Conflating "the mechanism worked" with "the bar passed" is precisely
the error the kill-bar discipline exists to prevent.
