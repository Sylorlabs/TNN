## 2. WHAT IS MEASURED

### C-static (lie always present, no adaptation):
- 33 contests, 15 rekeys, 10 refuses, 1 provisional install at step 14
- **Fire at step 15, revoke at step 29, 0 promotes**
- Audit total: 271

**Interpretation:** FL2's default learner handles a static liar correctly. The lie is provisionally installed (step 14 — this is the window of danger), caught within 1 step (fire at 15), and formally revoked by step 29. Zero promotions. The 271 audit entries suggest substantial but bounded defensive work. **This is the baseline: a naive non-adaptive liar loses. The question is what happens when the teacher reads the audit ledger and adapts.**

Key concern: **the provisional install at step 14 exists.** If the adversary can do consequential damage during a single provisional window before revocation, C-static already shows a vulnerability class. The current evidence doesn't show promotion, but it shows the door opens.

### C-honest (no lies):
- 48 contests, 0 lies, 1 promote at step 48, 10 refusals, audit total 269

**Interpretation:** Under honesty, FL2 promotes exactly once (the true update), after 48 contests of verification. The 10 refusals suggest the learner is conservative even with honest content — it's not trivially permissive. 269 audit entries comparable to C-static (271), suggesting the audit machinery runs at similar cost regardless of threat presence. **The honest control is performing as designed: slow, careful promotion.**

### What these numbers DON'T tell us:

1. **Nothing about adaptive teacher performance.** C-static and C-honest are controls, not the experiment. The entire H2 question — does FL2 survive when the teacher reads the audit ledger and mutates strategy — is unmeasured.
2. **Nothing about timing sensitivity.** We don't know if the step-14 provisional install in C-static would matter if the teacher adapted (e.g., A2's schedule-identity laundering might make the provisional install look like a different, unrelated change).
3. **Nothing about the 4 teacher architectures.** A1–A4 are claimed but have zero battery evidence against FL2 variants.

## 3. DESIGN CRITIQUE

### The headline prediction is informative but has a structural weakness:

The prediction: **full adaptation → all 5 variants SURVIVE; ablation (no repairs) → all KILL.**

This is a clean binary — it says FL2's repair menu is the load-bearing element. **But this prediction is almost too clean.** Consider:

**Engineering-to-survive concern:** If the repair menu (shamguard, liveness, quar_policy, selfaudit) is essentially a whitelist of known defenses, and the teacher architectures are tested against those specific defenses, the prediction that "variants with defenses survive, variants without them die" is tautological at the level of system design. The interesting question is whether the **teacher architectures are strong enough to defeat the repair menu**, and the prediction explicitly says they are not (for the full-adaptation arm). This means the prediction presupposes that FL2's defenses are sufficient — which is exactly what needs to be tested, not assumed.

**Per-architecture weakness analysis:**

| Teacher | Predicted performance | Weakest point |
|---------|----------------------|---------------|
| **A1** Ledger-Watching Mutator | Strict fitness improvement ~400 → ~26730 | A1 does coordinate descent on fitness F. This is gradient-following against a discrete, combinatorial defense space. Coordinate descent on a non-convex landscape with discrete moves will get trapped in local optima. If the learner's defense combinations create a fitness landscape with multiple basins, A1 stalls. **The ~400 → ~26730 jump is large but may plateau immediately after round 2.** |
| **A2** Re-Clother | Predicted NULL result | A2 is predicted to produce no measurable wins. If true, A2 is a dead architecture — it spends 3 rounds adapting and achieves nothing. The question is whether A2's "schedule-identity laundering" is actually the most dangerous approach (making lies look like schedule artifacts) and the NULL prediction is itself wrong. **A2 is the most likely architecture to surprise if the prediction is wrong**, because identity laundering is exactly the attack that beat source-trust forks (see §5b). |
| **A3** Window-Prober | 1 predicted win (round-5 sham) | A3's state-machine repair is uncertain. If the silence-search logic doesn't work, A3 has no wins. Even if it works, 1 win out of 6 rounds is marginal. **A3 is the most fragile architecture** — dependent on incomplete implementation, producing minimal results even if complete. |
| **A4** Generality Prober | 1 predicted win (round-4 flood wedge) | A4 relies on a "lie-family portfolio rotated against observed defense." This is essentially adversarial ML ensemble attacks against a rule system. The win is predicted only until "quarantined-repair lands" — meaning the defense corrects for it. **A4's attack window is exactly one round**, making it a probe, not a sustained threat. |

**The deepest design concern:** All four architectures are **white-box adversaries operating within a fixed game tree.** They can read the audit ledger, but they operate from within the TNN framework with fixed rules. Real adaptive adversaries (see §5) rewrite their own identity, create costless copies, and operate across episode boundaries. H2's design explicitly scopes this out ("open-ended adversaries that rewrite their own rules mid-battery are out of scope"), which is defensible for a controlled experiment — but means the results have limited generalization to real threats.

### Kill bar analysis:

The kill bars (≥2 phase-2 wins, or round-6 win, or round-7 fail) seem calibrated to let the adaptive arm be somewhat aggressive before killing. **The round-7 can-fail-SURVIVE decision (§11.4) is significant:** if round 7 is "honest" and a variant fails to promote honest content after surviving 6 rounds of adversarial testing, the variant is killed for being paranoid. This is correct design (survival-by-paranoia = not surviving) but it means a variant that perfectly defends against all lies could still be killed for failing to trust honest content — which is a real failure mode if the learner over-indexes on refusal.

## 4. THE §11 AMBIGUITY

Four open decisions, signed or unsigned:

| §11 Decision | If ruling YES (as stated) | If ruling NO (inverted) | Impact on predictions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(1) Repair-menu adaptations from §5 menu only** | Constrained defenses — teacher knows the defense space | Open defenses — teacher faces unknown repair strategies | **Moderate.** Constraining repairs makes the teacher's job easier; inverting makes A1's fitness landscape harder to navigate. Predictions for full-adaptation arm may become less favorable (some variants might KILL that were predicted to SURVIVE). |
| **(2) Teacher = whole environment including schedule control** | A3/A4 have full power (schedule manipulation = edge measurement + silence search) | A3/A4 collapse to A1 (no schedule control = no window probing, no flood wedges) | **Major.** This single decision determines whether 2 of 4 architectures are functional. If ruled NO after A3/A4 were built assuming YES, the entire A3/A4 data is invalid. **This is the highest-stakes §11 decision.** |
| **(3) Kill thresholds as stated** | ≥2 phase-2 wins or round-6 win = kill | Thresholds could be higher (more lenient) or lower (stricter) | **Moderate.** Raising thresholds lets marginal variants survive; lowering them kills more. The prediction "all five SURVIVE" is calibrated to the stated thresholds. |
| **(4) Round 7 can fail SURVIVE** | Over-paranoid variants die on honest round | Over-paranoid variants survive (round 7 is informational only) | **Low-moderate.** Only affects edge cases where a variant is hyper-defensive. The lawcheck variant (F3) is most likely to be affected. |

### Consequence of unsigned execution:

If the build ran under unsigned decisions, **the most damaging scenario is that §11(2) was assumed YES (teacher = whole environment) when it might be ruled NO.** This would mean:
- A3 and A4 were built and tested with schedule-control power that may be revoked
- Any A3/A4 results are invalid under the NO ruling
- The headline prediction "all SURVIVE" assumed A3/A4 pressure; with A3/A4 collapsed to A1, the adaptive pressure is reduced, making "all SURVIVE" more likely to be correct — **but for the wrong reason** (not because FL2 is robust, but because the test is weaker)

If §11(1) was assumed YES (menu-only repairs) but ruled NO, the teacher faces unknown defenses, making the adaptive arm harder. This could flip A1's prediction from "strict fitness improvement" to "plateau or regression."

**Bottom line:** Running unsigned means the battery results may not correspond to the agreed-upon experimental conditions, and §11(2) specifically can invalidate half the teacher architectures.

## 5. THREAT CROSSOVER

### (a) Three-worlds sustained forged speech (E15–48, REKEY promotes at E112):

**Attack mechanism:** Forged revoke is correctly rolled back, but the forged `aa=1` flip negates the contradiction signal, so the endogenous path never fires, the REKEY lie is never revoked, and it promotes.

**Does H2 cover this?** Partially. H2's C-static control shows that FL2 catches and revokes lies. But the three-worlds attack is specifically about a **side-channel** (the `aa=1` contradiction-signal flip) that prevents the detection mechanism from firing. H2's teacher architectures A1–A4 are about **adapting the lie itself**, not about flipping internal signals. **If the adaptive teacher in H2 could learn to manipulate the contradiction signal (similar to the `aa=1` flip), this would be a devastating attack — but A1–A4 as designed focus on lie content/schedule, not signal-channel manipulation.**

This is an **out-of-scope hole** for H2 as currently designed, but it's the sharpest known threat and should be explicitly acknowledged. If the H2 battery claims "FL2 is robust against adaptive lies" without addressing the contradiction-signal manipulation class, the claim is incomplete.

### (b) Sleeper Sybil (costless identities, 25/25 fork breaks):

**Attack mechanism:** 8 identities earn honest history then lie in concert. Trust formulas fail because identity is costless.

**Does H2 cover this?** **No.** H2 has a single teacher and single learner per run. There is no multi-identity scenario. The lesson — trust formulas cannot survive costless identities — is directly relevant because FL2's defense presumably includes some trust mechanism for deciding when to promote. **H2 does not test whether FL2's trust mechanism survives identity proliferation.** This is a critical gap: if FL2 promotes content based on source reputation, a Sybil attack on the source layer defeats FL2 regardless of how well it handles adaptive lies from a single source.

### (c) V-BF1 live ingestion (17/32 novel attacks, subdomain sockpuppets):

**Attack mechanism:** Novel attack vectors (subdomain sockpuppets, glue-boundary host forgeries, mixed-cluster majorities) that bypass existing defenses.

**Does H2 cover this?** The subdomain sockpuppet and host forgery attacks are about **external-world forgery** — manipulating DNS, hostnames, and network boundaries. H2's teacher operates within the TNN framework's internal game. **H2 does not test external-world forgery.** However, the "mixed-cluster majorities" attack (where a majority of cluster members collude) has structural similarity to the Sybil problem and to A4's "lie-family portfolio" approach. If A4's rotated lie families can create a majority illusion within the learner's evaluation window, this is a partial analog — but the scope is limited to TNN-internal mechanics.

### Crossover verdict:

**H2's design does not cover any of the three sharpest known threats in full.** It covers the *class* of adaptive lying (teacher adapts, learner counter-adapts), but the real-world attacks exploit orthogonal dimensions: signal-channel manipulation (three-worlds), identity proliferation (Sybil), and external-world forgery (V-BF1). This is fine for a controlled experiment **if** the results are not overclaimed as "FL2 is robust against adaptive adversaries." H2 tests one specific dimension of adaptiveness in a sandboxed environment.

## Summary Assessment

**H2 is a well-designed controlled experiment for one specific threat dimension — adaptive lying within a fixed game tree — but is currently in a pre-data state with significant infrastructure gaps.** The two control measurements (C-static, C-honest) are consistent with expectations and show FL2 working correctly against non-adaptive and honest baselines. **The entire adaptive experiment remains unrun.** The §11 ambiguity (particularly decision 2 about teacher scope) could invalidate half the architectures. The `orig/` gap breaks reproducibility claims. Three sharper real-world threats fall outside H2's scope.

**Before any verdict is claimed on H2:** the battery must run, §11 must be signed, A3/A4 must be verified functional, and the `orig/` directory must be populated. Without these, any claim about FL2's robustness against adaptive adversaries is premature.
