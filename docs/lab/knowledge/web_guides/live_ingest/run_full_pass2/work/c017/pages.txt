S|## **What is Metric?** The metric system uses units such as meter, gram, and liter to measure length, mass, and liquid volume (capacity), just as the U.S.
S|customary system uses feet, ounces, and quarts to measure these.
S|In addition to the difference in the basic units, the metric system is based on 10s, and different measures for length include kilometer, meter, decimeter, centimeter, and millimeter.
S|Notice that the word “meter” is part of all of these units.
S|The metric system also applies the idea that units within the system get larger or smaller by a power of 10.
S|This means that a meter is 100 times larger than a centimeter, and a kilogram is 1,000 times heavier than a gram.
S|You will explore this idea a bit later.
S|For now, notice how this idea of “getting bigger or smaller by 10” is very different than the relationship between units in the U.S.
S|customary system, where 3 feet equals 1 yard, and 16 ounces equals 1 pound.

S|Identify the Starting Unit and the Desired Unit: Determine the unit you have and the unit you need to convert to.

S|The *toise* contained 6 *pied* (feet), changed from 324.8 mm to 1 ⁄ 3 of a metre (333.33 mm).
S|The *pied* had 12 *pouces* (inches) and the *pouce* had 12 *lignes*.[15][24]: 334 ### Second attempt to metricate France In 1837 the metrication laws were revived by the July Monarchy in France and in 1840 the system became compulsory throughout France, almost 50 years after it was first introduced.[24]: 451 ### Wider adoption of the metric system During the nineteenth century many small countries started cooperating with each other.
S|In 1815 the Kingdom of the Netherlands was formed from seventeen small states.
S|Each state had its own system of measurement.
S|In 1820 they decided that it would be better if everybody used the metric system.[27] In 1815 the German Confederation was formed.
S|It was an association of 39 different states.
S|Each state had its own system of measurements.
S|In 1834 the German Confederation formed a customs union called the *Zollverein*.
S|In 1851 the *Zollverein* decided to use metric units for trade between the many different states.
S|In 1871, most of the states in the German Confederation were joined together to form the German Empire.
S|The German Empire continued to use the metric system.[27] In the same year, Italy was also formed from a large number of small states.
S|Italy also decided to use the metric system rather than choosing one of the old systems of measurement.[27] By 1875 many European and Latin American counties were already using the metric system.
S|These countries included France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Peru and Columbia.
S|Between 1875 and 1914 many more countries including Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Paraguay, Philippines and Vietnam started to use the metric system.
S|In 1917, during the Bolshevik Revolution, the USSR (now Russia) adopted the metric system.
S|By the start of the Second World War most non-English speaking countries had adopted the metric system.[27] #### United States In 1866, after most of the South American countries started to use the metric system, the United States passed a law that allowed people to use either the metric system or United States customary units for trade.
S|Before 1893 the yard was defined as the length of the "standard yard" which was kept by the United States Treasury.
S|The pound was defined as being the mass of the "standard pound".
S|In 1893 the United States Congress passed the Mendenhall Order.
S|This order defined the yard as being exactly 3600⁄3937 metres and the pound as being exactly 0.4535924277 kilogram.
S|The order only changed the definitions of the pound and the yard.
S|It had no other effect on people's lives.[28] In 1975 the Metric Conversion Act started a formal metrication process.
S|Metrication was to be voluntary.
S|It was to be coordinated by the U.S.
S|In 1988 the Omnibus Trade and Competitiveness Act said that metric units had to be used for all federal projects.[29] The Act did not apply to state projects.
S|Some states demanded that metric units be used but other states did not.
S|Some industries changed to using metric units but others did not.
S|Soft drinks are sold in metric quantities.
S|Milk is sold in customary units.
S|Metric units are widely used in the design of motor cars.[30] Aircraft such as the Boeing 787 Dreamliner were designed using mainly customary units.[31] Some people in the United States want to complete the change-over to the metric system.
S|They say that it will make things easier for everybody.[32] Other people say that it will cost too much money.[33] Some people want to use the metric system because it will make it easier to export goods.[34] Other people say that metrication can only work if all fifty states metricate at the same time.
S|This will not happen unless the Federal Government takes the lead.[35] However, in the U.S.
S|territories of Puerto Rico and Guam, the metric system is already officially used and is dominant.
S|The metric system was introduced to both territories, when they were Spanish colonies, and has remained official and dominant after they became U.S.
S|territories.[36] #### United Kingdom In 1897 the United Kingdom passed a law allowing people to use either the metric system or Imperial units for trade.[37] By the late 1960s three quarters of British exports were to countries that used the metric system.
S|However people in the United Kingdom still used imperial units.[38] The Metrication Board was set up in 1969 to help Britain change to the metric system.
S|Each company had to pay their own expenses.
S|Some companies saved a lot of money by changing to the metric system because they could make the same goods for export as they made for sales in the United Kingdom.[39] For example, almost all motor cars use metric-sized nuts and bolts.
S|Other companies lost money because they had to make many changes but did not have any benefit from the changes.
S|When the Metrication Board was closed down in 1981 most of government and industry had changed to the metric system but a lot of everyday things like road signs had not been changed.[40] A survey taken in 2013 showed that metric units and imperial units were both widely used by British people in their private lives.[41] ## Related pages - Conversion of units - International System of Units, describes more advanced aspects of the metric system ## Notes 1.
