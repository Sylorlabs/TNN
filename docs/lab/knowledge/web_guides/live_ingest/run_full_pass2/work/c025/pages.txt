S|# Science for Kids: The Digestive System Our body needs food to provide it with energy, vitamins, and minerals.
S|However, in order use food, we must first break it down into substances that the various organs and cells in our body can use.
S|This is the job of our digestive system.
S|The digestive system acts in stages to digest our food.
S|Each stage is important and prepares the food for the next stage.
S|The entire length of our digestive system is around 20 to 30 feet!
S|**Here are the major stages of the digestive system:** **1.
S|Chewing** - Chewing is the first stage of the digestive system.
S|When you chew your food it breaks up big pieces into little pieces that are easier to digest and swallow.
S|Also, your saliva is more than just water.
S|It has special in it that start to break down starchy food (potatoes, bread) while you chew.
S|Swallowing** - Swallowing may seem like a simple process to us.
S|It just sort of happens.
S|But food doesn't just fall down our throats into our stomach.
S|First, our tongue helps to push food into the back of our throat.
S|Then there are special throat muscles that force the food down into a long tube that leads to our stomach, called the esophagus.
S|The food doesn't just fall down the pipe, muscles push the food along until it gets to our stomach.
S|At the same time all this is going on, a flap blocks off our windpipe making sure food doesn't go the wrong way.
S|We call this "going down the wrong pipe" and it can make us choke.
S|This flap is called the epiglottis and, fortunately for us, it works automatically.
S|Stomach** - The next stage is the stomach.
S|Food hangs out in the stomach for around four hours.
S|While the food sits there, more enzymes go to work on it, breaking down things like proteins that our bodies can use.
S|The stomach kills a lot of bad bacteria as well, so we don't get sick.
S|Small Intestine** - The first part of the small intestine works with juices from the liver and pancreas to continue to break down our food.
S|The second part is where the food gets absorbed from the intestine and into our body through the blood.
S|Large Intestine** - The last stage is the large intestine.
S|Any food that the body doesn't need or can't use is sent to the large intestine and later leaves the body as waste.
S|**The Liver and Pancreas** The liver and pancreas do a lot to help the digestive system along.
S|Both work with the small intestine.
S|The liver provides bile (stored in the gall bladder) that helps break up fat into smaller bits.
S|The pancreas provides additional enzymes to help digest all sorts of food.
S|The liver also processes the digested food from your blood before it gets sent to various places in your body to be used.

S|Categories The Human Digestive System Table of Contents The Fantastic Journey of Food Have you ever wondered what happens to your food after you swallow it?
S|It goes on a fantastic journey through your body in a process called digestion.
S|This is how your body turns food into the energy you need to grow, play, and learn.
S|The Journey The Starting Point – Your Mouth : When you take a bite of food, your teeth break it into smaller pieces.
S|Your saliva (the watery stuff in your mouth) helps soften the food and makes it slippery enough to travel easily down your throat.
S|The Food Slide – The Esophagus : After you swallow, the food moves down a tube called the esophagus.
S|It’s like a slide that takes the food from your mouth to your stomach.
S|The Mixing Bowl – The Stomach : Next, the food lands in your stomach, where it gets churned around with stomach acid and digestive enzymes.
S|This turns the food into a soft, mushy substance called chyme.
S|The Nutrient Road – The Small Intestine : The chyme then travels to the small intestine.
S|Here, all the good stuff from your food (like vitamins, minerals, proteins, fats, and sugars) gets absorbed into your bloodstream and delivered to every part of your body.
S|The Recycle Bin – The Large Intestine : Anything your body can’t use moves into the large intestine.
S|Here, water is absorbed from the waste material and it forms into stool (or poop).
S|The Exit Door – The Rectum and Anus : The stool is stored in the last part of the large intestine, the rectum, until it’s ready to leave your body.
S|When you go to the bathroom, the stool exits through an opening called the anus.
S|Special Helpers – The Liver and Pancreas : Some organs give extra help during digestion.
S|The liver makes a liquid called bile that helps break down fats, and the pancreas makes a chemical called insulin.
S|Insulin is like a signal to your cells that tells them to suck up sugar in your bloodstream.
S|Bacteria: The Tiny Helpers Believe it or not, you have billions of tiny organisms living inside you that help with digestion!
S|These bacteria break down tough parts of food that your body can’t digest on its own.
S|They also fight off harmful bacteria and strengthen your immune system.
S|Wrapping Up Isn’t it incredible to think about all the things your body does just to turn food into energy and get rid of waste?
S|So the next time you eat your favorite meal, remember the amazing journey that food takes through your body.
