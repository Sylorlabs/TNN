S|# Flexi answers - Explain how the Earth's tilt is the cause of the seasons, NOT the distance from the Sun.
S|| CK-12 Foundation Flexi Says: ## The Earth's tilt is the primary reason for the seasons.
S|The Earth's axis is tilted at an angle of about 23.5 degrees relative to its orbit around the Sun.
S|This tilt means that as the Earth orbits the Sun, different parts of the Earth receive different amounts of sunlight at different times of the year.
S|During summer, the Earth's tilt causes the Sun's rays to hit that part of the Earth more directly, resulting in more concentrated sunlight and warmer temperatures.
S|In contrast, during winter, the tilt causes the Sun's rays to hit at a more oblique angle, spreading the sunlight out over a larger area and resulting in cooler temperatures.
S|It's important to note that the Earth's distance from the Sun varies slightly during its orbit due to its elliptical path, but this variation has a much smaller effect on the Earth's climate than the tilt of its axis.

S|FAQs: **When Are The Four Seasons in Australia?** - Spring - September, October, and November - Summer - December, January, and February - Autumn - March, April, and May - Winter - June, July, and August **Why Do We Have Winter and Summer Seasons on Earth?** It’s all to do with the Earth’s tilt, which is known as the axial tilt.
S|According to the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) the axial tilt varies between ‘22.1 and 24.5 degrees’, during a cycle that averages 40,000 years.
S|So, we can say that the Earth’s axis is tilted at around 23.5 degrees from the plane of its orbit around the Sun.
S|What this means is that sometimes the top half of the Earth is pointing towards the Sun, and sometimes it is pointing away.
S|This is why we get seasonal changes, such as winter and summer.
S|**Does The Sun Rotate Around the Earth?** The short answer is no!
S|But, let’s explain this in more detail.
S|Firstly, there is a difference between ‘rotation’ and ‘revolution’.
S|‘Rotation’ is talking about an object’s spinning motion about its own axis.
S|‘Revolution’ on the other hand, is the object’s orbital motion around another object.
S|For example, NASA says that ‘Earth revolves about the Sun, producing the 365-day year’.
S|So, here is the answer to the question: the Sun does not rotate around the Earth, but the Earth does ‘revolve’ about the Sun.
S|The Earth also ‘rotates’ on its axis, which gives us the 24-hour day.
S|**What Causes Day and Night?** So, we already mentioned how the Earth rotates about its own axis once every 24 hours, and this is why we get day and night on Earth.
S|So, when bits of Earth are facing the Sun, it is daytime.
S|And, when those bits are facing away from the Earth, it’s nighttime.
