TITLE: What counts as evidence
PRINCIPLE:
Evidence is proposition, not prose. A sentence carries its claim in its
content-bearing tokens: the entities, relations, quantities, and names that
would still be asserted if the sentence were reworded. Function words (the,
of, is, at), auxiliaries (has, was, does), and light verbs (serves, making)
are surface form: they shape the sentence but assert nothing by themselves.
Two sentences that reduce to the same sorted set of canonical content tokens
assert the same proposition and must stand or fall together.
WORKED:
  "The Eiffel Tower is tall."
  -> drop {the,is} -> {eiffel,tower,tall} -> kernel "eiffel tall tower"
  "Tall is the Eiffel Tower."
  -> drop {is,the} -> {eiffel,tower,tall} -> same kernel: same proposition.
