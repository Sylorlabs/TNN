S|## Understanding Fractions, Percentages and Decimals - **Fractions, percentages, and decimals** are all ways to represent a portion of a whole.
S|They can all represent the same values just in different forms.
S|- A fraction is a way of expressing a number as a ratio of two numbers.
S|For example, “half” can be represented as the fraction **1/2**.
S|- A decimal is another way of representing a fraction, specifically a fraction where the denominator is a power of 10.
S|For example, “half” can be written as the decimal **0.5**.
S|- A percentage is a fraction where the denominator is always 100, expressed with a percent sign (%).
S|For example, “half” can be represented as the percentage **50%**.
S|## Conversion Between Fractions, Percentages, and Decimals - To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.
S|- To convert a decimal to a fraction, consider the place value of the decimal and use it as the denominator.
S|Remember to simplify the fraction if possible.
S|- To convert a decimal to a percentage, multiply it by 100 and add a % sign.
S|- To convert a percentage to a decimal, remove the person sign and divide by 100.
S|- To convert a fraction to a percentage, first convert the fraction to a decimal then to percentage.
S|## Practice Problems - Problem: Convert the fraction 3/4 to a decimal.
S|- Solution: 0.75 - Problem: Convert the decimal 0.45 to a percentage.
S|- Solution: 45% - Problem: Convert the percentage 35% to a fraction.
S|- Solution: 7/20 ## Key Points to Remember - Fractions, decimal numbers, and percentages are just different ways of **expressing the same thing**.
S|- **Practice converting** between fractions, decimals and percentages.
S|The more you practice, the more natural it will become.
S|- Don’t forget to **simplify your fractions** if possible.
S|- Always remember to **multiply by 100** when converting a decimal to a percentage, and **divide by 100** when converting a percentage to a decimal.

S|{18} A guide for teachers # HISTORY ## DECIMALS Decimal fractions had been employed for the extraction of square roots by Islamic mathematicians such as Al-Kashi (1380 – 1429) and also by the Chinese at an even earlier time .
S|The introduction of decimal fractions as a common computational practice can be dated back to the Flemish pamphlet De Thiende (36 pages), published at Leyden in 1585, together with a French translation, La Disme, by the Flemish mathematician Simon Stevin (1548-1620), then settled in the Northern Netherlands.
S|No one realized their widespread potential for their daily use before Stevin .
S|He felt that this innovation was so significant, that he declared the universal introduction of decimal coinage, measures and weights to be merely a question of time .
S|Stevin’s notation was, however, rather unwieldy.
S|The point separating the integers from the decimal fractions seems to be the invention of Bartholomaeus Pitiscus who used it in his trigonometrical tables (1612) .
S|It was also used by John Napier in his logarithmic papers (1614 and 1619) .
S|It should be noted that the Babylonians used a sexagesimal system .
S|That is, instead of the ‘places’ of the decimal system they used ‘places’ 1 60, 1 3600 etc., This is recorded for instance on Plimpton 322 which was written in about 1800BC.
S|The equivalent to the decimal point did not occur in Babylonian writings until much later (c 300 BC) .
S|## PERCENTAGES Long before decimals were used, the need to work with tenths, twentieths and hundredths was evident.
S|Roman taxes were calculated on the basis of these types of fractions.
S|For example, Augustus levied a tax of 1% on goods sold at auction and there was a tax of 4% on every slave sold .
S|The Romans did not use this notation but certainly employed the concept that we use today.
S|The concept was used throughout the ages, however the use of the sign, % , is quite recent and its exact history uncertain .

S|The formula for percentage change is: **Percentage Change = \(\frac{\text{Change in} x}{\text{Original Value of} x}\)** Here, the "Change in x" can be calculated as the new value minus the original value.
S|For example, if last year's sales revenue was \$550,000 and this year's sales revenue increased to \$800,000, you can find the percentage change as follows: 1.
S|Calculate the change in sales revenue: **Change in Sales Revenue = New Value - Original Value** Change in Sales Revenue = \$800,000 - \$550,000 = \$250,000 2.
S|Now, apply the percentage change formula: **Percentage Change = \(\frac{250,000}{550,000}\)** Percentage Change = 0.4545 (as a decimal) 3.
S|To convert this decimal into a percentage, move the decimal point two places to the right: Percentage Change = 45.45% This means that the sales revenue increased by 45.45% from last year to this year.
S|Understanding how to calculate percentage change is essential for analyzing growth or decline in various contexts, such as sales, population, or any measurable quantity.
