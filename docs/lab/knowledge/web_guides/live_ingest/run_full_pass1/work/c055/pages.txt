S|# The Time Zones Reading Comprehension When the sun shines on one part of the world, it is day.
S|However, during that same time-period, in another part of the world, it is night.
S|The world has been divided into different **Time Zones** so that the same hour of the day in every country is in the same part of the day.
S|For example, if it is 9 a.m.
S|where we live, if we were in China, it would be dark.
S|Some large countries may have more than one time zone.
S|Most smaller countries have just one.
S|The world didn't have time zones for thousands of years.
S|People didn't even keep track of time very much.
S|Hour glasses and water clocks and sundials helped them do this somewhat.
S|When watches were invented, people became more aware of time.
S|In 1878, **Sanford Fleming** suggested that the world be divided into **24 time zones**, each one hour apart.
S|The dividing lines run **north to south**.
S|Railroads were growing rapidly in the United States.
S|The railroad companies wanted to keep very strict schedules.
S|In **1883**, the big railroad companies made an agreement to make their time zones official.
S|Each time zone in the United States would differ by an hour.
S|In 1918, Congress passed the **Standard Time Act**, dividing the country into 4 time zones.
S|The lines run north to south, going from the eastern part of the country to the west.
S|As one travels west from the Atlantic Ocean, one travels one hour earlier through each time zone.
S|California is 3 hours earlier than New York City, for example.
S|The four zones east to west are called the **Eastern Standard Time Zone**, the **Central Time Zone, Mountain Time Zone**, and **Pacific Time Zone**.
S|Some states are in two time zones because the zones aren't always divided down state lines.
S|Florida, Indiana, and Kentucky are examples of states which have 2 time zones.
S|One reason for the fact that some states have more than one time zone is that not every state had become a state when railroad companies set up the schedules in 1883, or even by 1918.
S|Hawaii and Alaska which are quite a distance away became states much later.
S|Hawaii is 3 hours earlier than California.
S|Alaska is one hour behind California.
S|Areas which have **Daylight Savings Time (DST)** change their time during a specific period, moving the time one hour later or earlier for a certain number of months.
S|This is done in the spring and fall.
S|Some areas don't observe DST, so their time stays the same all year.
S|This is called their standard time.
S|DST is used to allow more hours of daylight at different periods of the year.
S|The **International Date Line** is an imaginary line running north to south through the Pacific Ocean, although not exactly a straight line so that it won't divide countries.
S|To the left of the line, the day is one day earlier or ahead of time than just to the right of the line.
S|They are the same time, though, only a day apart.
S|The starting time for **Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)** is in **Greenwich, England**.
S|This is where the zero line is.
S|**Greenwich Mean Time**, now also called UTC, is the time when the sun is at its highest point in Greenwich, England.
S|It was invented in 1675 at the **Royal Observatory** in England.
S|As you travel east from Greenwich, each zone is one hour later.
S|Some governments make decisions to have time zones which are not just an hour apart from the next zone.
S|For example, India is 51/2 hours later or east of Greenwich, England.
S|As one travels west from Greenwich, England, each time zone is one hour earlier, not counting changes due to DST.
