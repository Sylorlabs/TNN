S|# What kids really want to know about recycling | National Geographic [Image 0: Father and daughter are putting paper into a garbage bin at home.
S|They are recycling trash.
S|(Slovenia)] Photograph by AzmanL / Getty Images - FAMILY - KEEPING IT GREEN We’ve collected puzzling recycling questions from children around the country.
S|The answers could help your family understand the process better.
S|By Allyson Shaw Published November 11, 2021 Can you recycle a tissue?
S|When we asked kids for their questions about recycling, they did not disappoint.
S|After all, just because children are excited to help the environment doesn’t mean they’re experts in the recycling process, too.
S|“Many kids are eager to help with recycling,” says Chrystelle Panatier, a fifth-grade teacher in Silver Spring, Maryland.
S|“But they don’t always know the right place to put things, or where the recyclables go next.
S|They see the truck coming to pick it up, but I don’t think they understand what happens from there.” So we went straight to the source to find out what kids wanted to know and become better environmental stewards.
S|(In case you’re wondering: Tissues go in the trash; rubber bands generally aren’t recyclable but they are reusable; and clean tinfoil can go right into your curbside bin—aluminum is infinitely recyclable.) The following questions from real kids are ones that you might’ve heard from your own children—plus you might find the answer to something you’ve been wondering yourself.
S|**How much is recycled each day?
S|*(**fourth graders, North Kingstown, Rhode Island**)*** According to the most recent statistics from the Environmental Protection Agency, 69 million tons of waste were recycled in the United States in one year, which works out to about 1.6 pounds per person each day.
S|But in the same year, 146.1 million tons of waste were sent to landfills.
S|**How do recycling machines work?
S|*(Autumn, second grade, Overland Park, Kansas)*** Drivers haul curbside recycling to a local materials recovery facility (MRF) that uses machines and people power to sort the items into whatever they’re made of, like glass or plastic or paper.
S|The MRF can sell the separated items to companies that will turn them into new products.
S|“A glass manufacturer doesn't want paper in with their glass, and a paper manufacturer doesn’t want glass in their paper,” says Randy Hartman, an expert in waste management and a senior director at Keep America Beautiful.
S|“Recycling is all about getting the right materials in the right place at the right time.” Curious kids can probably see the machines in action at your local sorting center.
S|“A lot of MRFs actually have observation rooms where you can go and safely see the process,” Hartman says.
S|Or show children this kid-friendly cartoon that illustrates how a recycling center works.
S|**Why do people make recycled stuff into a cube shape?
S|*(Smith, second grade, Overland Park, Kansas)*** After the items are sorted at the MRF, they’re formed into blocks of material called bales.
S|The cube shape allows the materials to be stacked and moved by forklifts.
S|Manufacturers purchase these bales and process the materials at other facilities.
S|Paper is washed to remove the ink, then crushed and rerolled; metal is melted and reformed; plastic is shredded and melted down.
S|Then the material is used to make new products.
S|**What happens if you put something wet in the recycling bin?
S|*(Miles, second grade, Lynnwood, Washington)*** Wet stuff—especially wet paper—can be a problem when machines try to separate the recyclables.
S|“Think of what a wet newspaper does versus a dry one,” Hartman says.
S|“At the collection site, it’s sticky and gets tangled up on other materials or even around the wheels.” **Can a COVID-19 mask be recycled?
S|*(Second graders, Lynnwood, Washington)*** Yes!
S|But not in your curbside bin.
S|The recycling company TerraCycle will accept PPE like masks, gloves, and safety glasses (though you have to pay for the box).
S|**Why can’t I recycle Styrofoam?
S|*(fifth graders, North Kingstown, Rhode Island)*** Actually, Styrofoam (or its real name, expanded polystyrene foam) is recyclable—just not at the curb.
S|That’s because it’s about 95 percent air and therefore very light and bulky.
S|That makes it expensive for the MRFs to collect, transport, and process the material.
S|“Recyclers are also thinking about environmental impact in addition to the cost when deciding what to accept,” Hartman says.
S|“For foam, the emissions and cost to move it is higher than the benefit of recycling it.” If your kid is concerned about all those foam lunch trays, encourage the school to investigate companies that are coming up with creative solutions.
S|For instance, a company in Oregon collects the foam and has special machines that can turn it into new products.
S|(Here’s how to help your kids make big eco-friendly changes at their school.) **What happens if I recycle something that can’t be recycled?
S|*(fifth graders, North Kingstown, Rhode Island)*** Recyclers have a word for that: wish-cycling.
S|Non-recyclable items that end up at an MRF are separated and usually sent to a landfill or an incinerator.
S|But the people working there don’t always catch everything.
S|Some items, like batteries, can cause fires; tangly things like hoses, holiday lights, plastic bags, and electrical cords can damage machines.
S|The good news is that lots of these items are recyclable, just not in your curbside bin.
S|(For example, many grocery stores collect plastic bags for special recycling.) Help your kid figure this out by exploring this website.
S|You can also check your local rules and recycling options by putting your zip code into the website to see nearby recyclers.
S|**Why don’t more schools have recycling programs?
S|*(Cadence, 11th grade, Billings, Montana)*** Schools don’t always have the money or people to handle a recycling system.
S|“Who’s going to collect it?
S|Where will they store it before collection?” Hartman says.
S|“It’s a long-haul commitment that takes a lot to sustain on your own.” But that doesn’t mean kids shouldn’t try.
S|This guide from the environmental nonprofit Action for Nature can give older children some great ideas to bring to school officials.
S|“We’ve seen many successful volunteer recycling programs, so it’s certainly possibly to create one,” Hartman says.
S|**Why can’t I recycle a greasy pizza box?
S|*(fifth graders, North Kingstown, Rhode Island)*** In most cases, you can!
S|“It’s been a raging debate in the recycling community for a long time,” Hartman says.
S|“But you actually can.” You still have to check your local rules, though.
S|All that gooey cheese and oil can be difficult to separate from the paper fiber, plus some facilities don’t want any extra pizza crust attracting pests to the MRF.
S|If your local MRF doesn’t accept the greasy bits, just rip off the clean cardboard and add it to your bin.
S|**How do you recycle a plastic water bottle?
S|(*Sawyer, first grade, Vero Beach, Florida*)** Plastic water bottles can go into your curbside recycling bin, but most end up in landfills or as litter.
S|According to the EPA, only about 30 percent of plastic bottles are actually recycled.
S|Just like Styrofoam, plastic bottles aren’t as profitable to recycle because it’s usually cheaper to create new, raw plastic.
S|But you can help: Purchasing products made of 100 percent recycled material increases demand for the product.
S|And of course, we have to plug reusable water bottles.
S|“One of the other important steps that we sometimes forget is ‘refuse,’” Hartman says.
S|“If there's an alternative that doesn't generate waste, then use that.” **What's it like driving a recycling truck?
S|*(Spencer, first grade, Vero Beach, Florida)*** “You're sitting up really high and so you have a good view of the street," Hartman says.
S|“It's disappointing when you only see one or two recycling containers set out for pickup.
S|But the most exciting part is seeing kids wave and watch us as we pick up their recyclables.

S|April 2, 2014 Dean Botham [Image 0: Recycling Guide for Primary School Children] ## **Recycling Guide for Primary School Children** ### **Redu****ce, Reuse, Recycle** It’s easy to think that we have as much paper, plastic and metal on the planet as we’ll ever need, but that’s not true.
S|We need to be very careful not to waste anything, and that also includes water, electricity, clothes, furniture and anything else you can think of.
S|The best way to do this is to use less where we can – this is when we **reduce**.
S|Then we see if we or someone else can use things a second time – this is when we **reuse**.
S|If something can’t be used again, like an empty tin, we send it away to be turned into something new – this is called **recycling**.
S|### **How does recycling work?** Recycling works in different ways, depending on the material.
S|**Paper** is mixed with water and the ink is removed.
S|The wet pulp is then dried and squeezed very tightly to make new sheets of paper.
S|**Aluminium** **cans** are melted down and cleaned.
S|The aluminium is then shaped into new cans or turned into other items.
S|**Tin cans** are soaked in special chemicals to separate the tin coating from the steel.
S|The tin is melted down and the steel is crushed.
S|The tin and steel are then used to make new cans and other metal objects.
S|**Plastic** is washed and cut into small pieces.
S|Any glue is removed and the clean plastic is melted and made into small pellets.
S|Depending on the type of plastic, these pellets can then be used for anything from new bottles to toys.
S|**Glass** is broken into tiny pieces, mixed with special materials and melted.
S|The hot, liquid glass is then made into new shapes and used again.
S|**[Image 1: Children Recycling]** ### **Be green!** When we recycle and look after our planet, we call it being green.
S|It’s also called being environmentally friendly.
S|This is because we’re helping to keep the environment alive with plenty of green grass, plants and trees.
S|### **Find your bin** Do you have a recycling bin at home?
S|Ask your mum and dad if you’re not sure.
S|You don’t want to accidentally put things that can be recycled into the wrong bin.
S|### **What can I recycle?** There are lots of things that can be recycled when you’re finished with them.
S|Old magazines and comics, empty cans and plastic bottles, even old bus tickets!
S|Food and garden waste such as grass can also be recycled, but this needs to go in a special bin or taken to a recycling centre.
S|### **Can everything be recycled?** Most things can be recycled but there are some types of plastic, glass and packaging that can’t be put into a recycling bin.
S|Check the packaging for recycling information, and ask a parent of teacher if you need help.
S|### **Clean and crush** You can keep your recycling bin from smelling bad and help the recycling people by cleaning tins and bottles first.
S|Crushing plastic bottles and aluminium cans also helps because they then use less room.
S|Be careful with sharp edges and ask an adult for help if you’re not sure.
S|### **Recycle at school** You throw away a lot of paper at school and you may have empty bottles and food packaging left over from lunchtime too.
S|If you have something you don’t need anymore, ask a teacher if there’s a recycling bin nearby and you’ll have the greenest school around.
S|### **It’s not just paper, plastic and cans** All kinds of electronic items can be recycled, like old mobile phones and cameras, games consoles and computers, and even single batteries.
S|Ask your parents or teacher about nearby recycling centres, and make sure your parents take broken and unwanted appliances to them as well, whether it’s a TV or a toaster.
S|Also see if your supermarket has a special bin for used batteries so that they can be melted down and made into new ones.
S|### **Can you reuse it?** If you’ve written a note and you’re about to throw it away, save the paper and use the other side another time.
S|Yoghurt pots are great for growing small plants, so save some for when you want to do some gardening.
S|You could even be really creative and use old paper and plastic bottles to create colourful art projects.
S|Then, when you don’t want them anymore, they can still be recycled.
S|### **Can it be shared?** If you have books, toys, games or clothes that you don’t want, see if a friend would like them before throwing them away.
S|If they don’t, ask your parents to take you to a charity shop so that someone else can enjoy them.
S|If you have old magazines and comics, you could take them to a dental clinic, hospital or health centre for visitors to read.
S|And always look out for charity bags through your letterbox, as it’s a great way to get rid of old clothes whilst helping people in need.
S|### **You can even reuse food!** Many families save leftovers from meals and use them the next day.
S|If you have some tasty meats and vegetables left after a salad, why not use them in a sandwich or as a delicious pizza topping tomorrow?
S|Just always make sure that leftovers are stored in the fridge and covered up properly.
S|For food that needs throwing away, ask your parents and teachers about composting – a great way to reuse food and help gardens and allotments grow!
S|[Image 2: Leftovers] ### **It’s so easy!** Recycling is so much easier than it was when your parents and teachers were kids.
S|Almost everything can now be recycled and most homes have their own recycling bins.
S|The important thing is that you remember to recycle, so don’t throw something into a normal bin if it can go in a recycling bin.
S|By working together, our planet will always have enough materials and you’ll be doing your bit to help the environment.

S|https://pixabay.com/vectors/woman-girl-black-female-rubbish-160281/ ## Recycling for Kids Instead of throwing everything away in the trash so it will end up in a landfill, you can recycle many things so they can become new products.
S|Recycling reduces waste and helps save natural resources such as wood and water.
S|You can feel good about helping the environment when you recycle because you'll be saving energy and reducing pollution.
S|Recycling involves separating items that you're getting rid of into different groups.
S|Some items can't be recycled, but you can separate out many paper products, cans, glass, and plastic so these things can be taken to special facilities to be recycled for future use.
S|- Recycling facilities sort, clean, and process items so they can be used to make new things.
S|- After being sorted and processed, these materials are ready to be sold to manufacturers to be formed into new items.
S|Businesses and schools can recycle, and families can recycle, too.
S|- When you recycle at home, you can use separate bins to collect the different items.
S|But in some places, you can put everything recyclable into one big bin without having to sort it.
S|- When you recycle at school, pay attention to the bins they have so you throw items into the correct ones.
S|## What Can You Recycle?
S|Recyclable materials include cardboard, newspapers, plastic and glass bottles, plastic containers, aluminum and steel cans, and some other types of paper.
S|- Look on the bottoms of plastic bottles and containers to find a symbol.
S|The symbol will tell you whether you can recycle the item.
S|- Most communities accept glass bottles and containers made out of different colored glass.
S|- Communities often accept paper and paper products, too.
S|## What Can't You Recycle?
S|Some items are not recyclable.
S|It can be tempting to place some items into the recycling bin, even if you're not sure if they're recyclable, but when people do this, they can place facility workers in danger as they're sorting items.
S|- Plastic bags and plastic wraps may be recyclable, but not in curbside pickup bins.
S|- Electric cords, chains, wires, and hoses are not recyclable in curbside pickup bins.
S|These items can tangle in sorting equipment.
S|- Hazardous materials such as paint, oil, gas, or poisons are not recyclable.
S|- Diapers are not recyclable.
S|- Packaging with food stuck to the inside should be cleaned first before recycling them.
S|- Propane tanks are not recyclable.
S|- Large and bulky plastic items such as laundry baskets and play furniture are not recyclable.
S|## How Are Things Recycled?
S|Recycling involves collecting and processing materials into a form that can be used to make new things.
S|Recycled raw materials are sold to manufacturers to make new items.
S|- Collection includes curbside pickup, drop-off centers, and buy-back centers that will pay consumers to bring their items to be recycled.
S|- Processing involves cleaning and sorting the items so they can be turned back into raw materials.
S|Items might be broken down into little pieces or melted down.
S|- After processing, the materials are sold to manufacturers, who use them either alone or by mixing them with other materials to make new products.
S|## Why Do You Recycle?
S|It's helpful to think of recycling as a closed loop.
S|The loop includes using something, collecting it, processing it, and making something new to be used.
S|It's important to keep that loop closed to protect our environment.
S|- By keeping items inside the recycling loop, we reduce waste and keep products out of landfills.
S|- Recycling reduces pollution, saves energy, and makes jobs.
