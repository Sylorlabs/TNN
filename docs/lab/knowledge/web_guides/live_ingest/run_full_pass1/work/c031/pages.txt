S|# 3 States of Matter for Kids (Solid, Liquid, Gas): Science for Children - ClassX This lesson introduces the concept of matter, defining it as anything that occupies space and has weight, and explains its composition of tiny particles called atoms.
S|It outlines the three main states of matter—solids, liquids, and gases—highlighting their distinct properties such as shape, mass, and volume, and discusses how matter can change from one state to another, exemplified by the transformation of water through freezing, melting, and evaporation.
S|### Understanding Matter: The Three States Matter is all around us!
S|It’s everything from the air we breathe to the rocks we step on.
S|But what is matter, and how can we tell it apart?
S|Let’s learn about matter, its properties, and the three main states it can be in: solids, liquids, and gases.
S|Matter is anything that takes up space and has weight.
S|This means everything you see and touch is made of matter, even if it looks different.
S|Matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms, which join together in different ways to make all the things we see every day.
S|#### The Properties of Matter To figure out the state of matter, we look at its properties.
S|A property is something we can measure.
S|Here are some important properties: - **Shape**: The form that an object takes.
S|- **Mass**: How much matter is in an object.
S|- **Volume**: How much space the matter takes up.
S|These properties help us understand the three main states of matter.
S|#### The Three States of Matter #### 1.
S|Solids Solids are easy to recognize.
S|Examples are rocks, apples, pencils, and plants.
S|Solids have a shape that stays the same no matter where you put them.
S|They also have a consistent mass and volume because their atoms are packed tightly together.
S|A solid only changes shape if you break or smash it.
S|Liquids Liquids are different from solids.
S|They have a definite volume and mass, but they don’t have a fixed shape.
S|The atoms in a liquid are close but can move around, letting the liquid flow.
S|Liquids take the shape of their containers.
S|For example, water looks different in a glass than when spilled on the floor.
S|Common liquids include water, milk, and juice.
S|Gases Gases are the third state of matter.
S|Like liquids, gases have a definite mass but no fixed shape or volume.
S|Gases fill the shape of their containers and spread out to fill all the space available.
S|This is because the atoms in a gas are far apart and move freely.
S|The air we breathe is a gas, made up of different gases like oxygen and nitrogen.
S|#### Changes in States of Matter Matter can change from one state to another.
S|For example, water can be a solid (ice), a liquid (water), or a gas (water vapor).
S|When water freezes, it becomes ice.
S|When it melts, it turns back into liquid.
S|When it evaporates, it becomes vapor.
S|#### Conclusion Matter is a big part of our world in all its states.
S|By learning about the properties and types of matter, we can see the different forms it takes around us.
S|Look around you and see if you can find examples of solids, liquids, and gases!

S|# States of Matter ### Teacher Lesson Plan ## Background Information – Matter • Matter is anything that takes up space and has mass.
S|It is all around you.
S|• All matter is made of atoms, the building blocks of the universe.
S|• Matter can be found in different states - solid, liquids and gases.
S|o Solids – atoms are tightly packed and do not move freely.
S|Solids have a definite shape and volume.
S|Particles vibrate in place and have a low amount of energy.
S|If you put a solid such as a book or pencil in your backpack, it will maintain its shape and will only take up so much space or volume.
S|o Liquids - atoms are loosely packed and slide past each other.
S|They have an indefinite shape but a definite volume.
S|Liquids flow and take the shape of the container they are in.
S|Think of milk in a glass versus in your cereal bowl.
S|Particles can flow past each other and have a medium amount of energy.
S|o Gases – atoms in a gas are far apart and full of energy.
S|Gases have no definite shape and no definite volume.
S|They also take the shape of their container.
S|Particles move very fast and have a high amount of energy.
S|They will spread out in all directions and fill as much volume (space) as they are given.
S|o Absolute Zero - the point at which the atoms have no energy at all, not even vibrational, is called absolute zero.
S|This only exists in outer space.
S|• For example, our bodies contain all 3 states of matter: o What are examples of solids in you?
S|Bones, some organs o What about liquids?
S|Blood, saliva, water o What about gases?
S|Air in our lungs, gases in our stomach (air bubbles) • Matter can change states by adding or taking away heat energy.
S|• The space between molecules increases as heat is added.
S|Heat gives molecules more energy, so they move faster, and bounce hard off each other, creating more space between them.
S|Solids expand a little, liquids expand more, and gases expand a lot as heat is added.
S|## Content Standards • Physical Science o All objects in the physical world are composed of matter.
S|o Matter exists in different states, each of which has different properties.

S|Heat seeks balance, too.
S|Energy flows from hotter places (high energy) to colder places (lower energy) and from hotter substances to colder substances.
S|Lesson Materials: • Energy In/Energy Out Arrows • Melt/Boil-Evaporate/Freeze/Condensation Arrows • Water Cycle Handout • Optional-If you have a heat source in your classroom, you can put the ice cube on a hot plate and show all three states of matter.

S|Water can change states by adding or taking away heat energy.
S|Using the states of matter cards, have students use the word arrows to show how energy is transferred to change states of matter.
S|• Melting – if an ice cube is left on the counter, the ice will take heat ENERGY IN (show with red arrow on solid ice to liquid) from the air and will MELT to form water.
S|• Evaporation/Boilingif the water is left on the counter, it will take ENERGY IN from the air and EVAPORATE.
S|If the cube is put on a hotplate, the water will take ENERGY IN (show with red arrow on liquid to gas) and will BOIL and turn into a gas or steam.
S|• Condensation - if we take ENERGY OUT (show with red arrow on gas to liquid) of a GAS, it will become a liquid again or CONDENSE.
S|• Freezing – if we take ENERGY OUT (show with red arrow on liquid to solid) of a liquid to go back to an ice cube, the liquid will have to go into the freezer to remove even more heat energy.
