S|Energy exists in many different forms.
S|Animals get energy from eating food.
S|Electrical energy is associated with the tiny units called atoms that make up everything in the universe.
S|Particles called electrons may move from one atom to another.
S|In doing so, they store or release energy.
S|Heat and light are also forms of energy.
S|One form of energy can also be transformed into another.
S|Through a battery stored chemical energy changes into electric energy.
S|In a lightbulb, electric energy changes to light and heat.
S|#### Potential and Kinetic Energy Each of the different forms of energy can be described as either potential energy or kinetic energy.
S|Potential energy is stored energy.
S|For example, the chemical energy of food is stored energy.
S|When people eat, their bodies change the stored energy into moving energy such as heat energy or mechanical energy.
S|Potential energy can also come from the position of an object.
S|An object with potential energy because of its position has the ability, or potential, to move.
S|For example, potential energy is stored in a rock perched on a cliff and in an arrow stretched back on a bowstring.
S|If the cliff crumbles under the rock, the rock falls.
S|If the string is let go, it moves forward and pushes the arrow through the air.
S|As the rock and the arrow move, they gain kinetic energy.
S|Kinetic energy is moving energy.
S|All moving objects have kinetic energy—even atoms.
S|The total random moving energy of all of the atoms and molecules in an object is called heat energy (or thermal energy).
S|The moving energy of a rock rolling down a hill is called mechanical energy.
S|#### Mechanical Energy Mechanical energy is potential energy plus kinetic energy.
S|Unlike the other forms of energy, mechanical energy can exist as both potential energy and kinetic energy.
S|It is all the energy that an object has because of its motion (kinetic energy) and its position (potential energy).
S|Machines use mechanical energy to do work.
S|For instance, a hammer uses mechanical energy to drive a nail into a board.
S|When the hammer is raised, it has potential energy from the work done in lifting it.
S|When the hammer is moved toward the nail, the potential energy becomes kinetic energy, which can do the work of driving the nail into the board.
S|When the hammer hits the nail, energy is transferred to the nail and then to the board.
S|#### Heat Energy (Thermal Energy) All substances are made up of particles, or bits, called molecules.
S|Molecules are always moving around randomly within a substance.
S|Heat energy is the total moving energy (or kinetic energy) of all of the molecules in a substance.
S|All substances have heat energy, since the molecules that make up all substances are always moving.
S|#### Light Energy Light energy is sometimes called radiant energy.
S|It is the only kind of radiant energy that is visible to the naked eye.
S|Radiant energy is the energy that is carried by electromagnetic radiation.
S|Some other examples of radiant energy are radio waves, microwaves, X-rays, and gamma rays.
S|Electromagnetic radiation travels in waves.
S|In addition to light energy, the Sun gives off the entire spectrum of radiant energy carried by electromagnetic radiation.
S|Light energy is moving energy.
S|It moves in the form of waves and can travel through empty space and air.
S|Different wavelengths of visible light are seen as different colors.
S|#### Sound Energy Sound energy is moving energy.
S|It is produced by the back-and-forth motion of a vibrating object.
S|This motion produces sound waves that travel away from the object.
S|The sound waves can travel only through a substance, such as air, water, or solid objects.
S|Sound travels more slowly than light does.
S|#### Electrical Energy Electrical energy is moving energy.
S|It is the movement of tiny particles called electrons and protons.
S|Electrical energy can be seen in nature in a bolt of lightning, which is a large number of electrons flowing through air all at once.
S|People have learned how to produce electrical energy and control it.
S|Electrical energy is sent through wires or the air to power such things as lightbulbs, ovens, and washing machines.
S|#### Chemical Energy Chemical energy is stored energy (potential energy).
S|It is stored in the bonds between atoms and molecules.
S|Chemical energy is what holds the atoms in a molecule together.
S|It also is what holds molecules in a substance together.
S|The chemical energy in food is converted, or changed, by the body into moving mechanical energy and heat energy.
S|The chemical energy inside a battery can be converted into electrical energy to turn on a flashlight.
S|#### Nuclear Energy Nuclear energy is stored energy.
S|It is the energy that holds together the nucleus of an atom.
S|Nuclear energy is also called atomic energy.
S|The nucleus is the central part of an atom.
S|Nuclear energy can be released by splitting an atom.
S|It can also be released by joining two nuclei together to form a single nucleus.

S|Energy is one of those words students hear all the time, but ask them what it actually is, and you’ll get a lot of shrugs.
S|Teaching the **types of energy for kids** starts with a simple idea: energy is what makes things move, change, heat up, light up, or make sound.
S|From there, students can sort the world into a handful of forms they can see and feel every day.
S|Energy is the ability to cause change or do work.
S|When something moves, gets warmer, makes a sound, or lights up, energy is behind it.
S|Students don’t need a formal definition to start.
S|They need to notice that energy is everywhere: in a kicked ball, a glowing lamp, a ringing bell, and the food they eat.
S|Once they see that, the different types of energy become easier to name.
S|## The Two Big Groups: Kinetic and Potential Energy Before naming all the forms, it helps students to know that energy comes in two big groups: - **Kinetic energy** is the energy of motion.
S|Anything moving has it: a rolling ball, a running student, blowing wind.
S|- **Potential energy** is stored energy, waiting to be used.
S|A stretched rubber band, a ball at the top of a ramp, and the food in your lunch all hold potential energy.
S|## Types of Energy Explained, With Examples Here are nine common forms students meet in elementary science, each with an example they already know.
S|- **Heat (thermal) energy** comes from the motion of particles.
S|The warmer something is, the faster its particles move.
S|Examples: a stove, a campfire, the sun.
S|- **Light (radiant) energy** travels in waves and lets us see.
S|Examples: the sun, a lamp, a flashlight.
S|- **Sound energy** is made by vibrations traveling through the air.
S|Examples: a drum, a voice, a guitar string.
S|- **Electrical energy** comes from moving electric charges.
S|Examples: lightning, a wall outlet, a battery powering a toy.
S|- **Mechanical energy** is the energy of moving objects and machines, a mix of motion and stored energy.
S|Examples: a moving bike, a swinging pendulum, a wind-up toy.
S|- **Chemical energy** is stored in the bonds of substances and released during a reaction.
S|Examples: food, batteries, gasoline, burning wood.
S|- **Elastic energy** is stored in objects that are stretched or squeezed, a kind of potential energy.
S|Examples: a stretched rubber band, a pulled-back slingshot, a coiled spring.
S|- **Gravitational energy** is stored in objects held up high, ready to fall, another kind of potential energy.
S|Examples: a ball at the top of a hill, water held behind a dam, a ski jumper at the top of the ramp.
S|- **Nuclear energy** is stored inside the tiny atoms that make up everything, and it’s released when atoms join together or split apart.
S|The sun makes its light and heat this way.
S|One form often turns into another: the chemical energy in food becomes thermal energy (body heat) and kinetic energy when they run, the radiant energy from the sun becomes chemical energy in plants through photosynthesis, and the electrical energy in a TV becomes light and sound.
S|## Frequently Asked Questions **Q: What are the main types of energy for kids?** A: The types of energy kids encounter most in elementary science are heat, light, sound, electrical, mechanical, and chemical energy.
S|All of them are really forms of two big groups: kinetic energy, which is motion, and potential energy, which is stored.
S|**Q: What is the difference between kinetic and potential energy?** A: Kinetic energy is the energy of something moving, like a rolling ball.
S|Potential energy is stored and waiting to be used, like a ball held at the top of a ramp.
S|When the ball rolls down, its potential energy turns into kinetic energy.
S|**Q: How many types of energy are there?** A: It depends on how you group them.

S|Every one of them is really a kind of kinetic energy (motion) or potential energy (stored).
