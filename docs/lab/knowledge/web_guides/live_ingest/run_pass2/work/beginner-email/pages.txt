S|# How to Send an Email | Announce | University of Nebraska-Lincoln Emails Matter!
S|Archived Story: This article is part of our newsletter archives.
S|It has been preserved for reference, but the information may no longer be current.
S|Emails are used for a variety of purposes including communicating with teachers and counselors, asking for information, applying for jobs/internships, networking, and much more!
S|Depending on what purpose, the emails you send can differ in formatting as well as whether they are formal or informal.
S|However, here is a general format that is useful to follow when writing emails: Include a subject line.
S|Serves as an explanation for what the email consists of.
S|Should be brief but specific.
S|Include a quick greeting.
S|Helps acknowledge who you are writing to.
S|A simple "Hi" or "Hello" followed by the recipient's name.
S|Introduce yourself if necessary.
S|State the purpose of the email.
S|Unless required otherwise, keep it short and concise.
S|When asking questions or for certain information, make sure to be specific (Example: if asking for a Zoom Link, identify for which event).
S|Proofread in case of spelling/grammatical errors that may confuse the recipient.
S|Be considerate and write in a respectful manner.
S|Can say "Thank you" to show appreciation.
S|Example email: "Hi Selma!
S|What is the Zoom link for today's Park Club?

S|# E-mail Etiquette -or- How to write an e-mail Here are some suggestions about how to send a professional e-mail.
S|The better your e-mail conforms to this, the more likely it will pass a spam-checker, be read, and receive a prompt reply.
S|- Send the e-mail to me (where my address appears in the "To:" line) if you want me to read it.
S|Do not expect me to carefully read it if I am on the CC list.
S|I may not get it if you BCC me.
S|That is, e-mails sent to "undisclosed recipients" often end up classified as spam.
S|- Put my name in the e-mail, e.g., start with "Hello Dr.
S|- Put your name in the e-mail, e.g., end with "-your name".
S|- Do not use text-messaging abbreviations like "u" or "ur".
S|- If you are in my class, say so, and indicate which one.
S|- If you want to meet me, be sure that you know when my office hours are.
S|If you want to meet me during office hours, do not bother with the e-mail.
S|Just come to my office.
S|- Try to keep questions easy to answer, like yes/no.
S|- If you need me to answer a question, make sure to use a question mark.
S|- Try to keep it short and to the point, but include details when necessary.
S|- Use plain text when possible.
S|- Do not include attachments, unless necessary.
S|- If you have a question about code you are writing for a class, DO attach the code to the e-mail.
S|- Do not expect an immediate answer.
S|- Do not ask me things that you could easily look up.
S|- Use your official (GSU) e-mail address.
S|- When you get a response, acknowledge the receipt.
S|Something simple like "Thanks.", "I got it." or "I got the attachment." works well.
S|- Do not ask me "what's your schedule?" My office hours are posted.
S|My classes are posted.
S|- I would rather read "Sorry, I did / did not ..." than a long-winded excuse.
S|- Do not send an attached document when you could simply paste it in the e-mail body.

S|# Managing Your Email | Absolute Beginners Guide to Computer Basics (2nd Edition) Using Outlook Express is easyand composing a new email message isn't much different from writing a memo in Microsoft Word.
S|You just have to know which buttons to push!
S|It's easy to create a new email message.
S|Just follow these steps: 1.
S|Click the Create Mail button on the Outlook Express toolbar; this launches a New Message window.
S|Use the New Message window to compose a new email message.
S|Enter the email address of the recipient(s) in the To field, then enter the address of anyone you want to receive a carbon copy in the Cc box.
S|You can enter multiple addresses, as long as you separate multiple addresses with a semicolon (;).
S|You can test your email account by sending a message to yourself; just enter your own email address in the To field.
S|Move your cursor to the main message area and type your message.
S|When your message is complete, send it to the Outbox by clicking the Send button.
S|Now you need to send the message from your Outbox over the Internet to the intended recipient (you!).
S|You do this by clicking the Send/Recv button on the Outlook Express toolbar.
S|Assuming your computer is connected to the Internet, your message will now be sent.
S|When you receive new email messages, they're stored in the Outlook Express Inbox.
S|To display all new messages, select the Inbox icon from the Folders list.
S|All waiting messages now appear in the Message pane.
S|You also can double-click a message header to display the message in a separate window.
S|To read a specific message, select its header in the Message pane.
S|The contents of that message are displayed in the Preview pane.
S|Replying to a Message.
S|To reply to an email message, follow these steps: 1.
S|Select the message header in the Message pane.
S|Click the Reply button on the Outlook Express toolbar; this opens a Re: window, which is just like a New Message window except with the text from the original message "quoted" in the text area and the email address of the recipient pre-entered in the To field.
S|Enter your reply text in the message window.
S|Click the Send button to send your reply back to the original sender.
