## Short Answer

A compass works because its magnetic needle aligns with Earth's magnetic field. The needle is a small magnet that points toward magnetic north, allowing navigation by showing direction relative to Earth's magnetic poles.

## Detailed Explanation

### Background

Compasses have been used for navigation for centuries, helping travelers find their way by pointing toward magnetic north. Compasses demonstrate how magnetic fields affect magnets, showing how Earth itself acts like a giant magnet. The compass needle responds to Earth's magnetic field, aligning itself with field lines.

Compasses have been essential tools for navigation for over a thousand years, enabling exploration and trade across oceans and continents. Even today, with GPS technology, compasses remain important backup navigation tools and help us understand Earth's magnetic field, which protects our planet and guides migratory animals.

### Scientific Principles

Compasses work through several key principles:

1. **Magnetic needle**: A compass contains a small magnet (the needle) that's free to rotate. The needle has north and south poles like any magnet.
2. **Earth's magnetic field**: Earth acts like a giant magnet with magnetic north and south poles. The magnetic field extends from the poles, creating field lines that compass needles align with.
3. **Alignment**: The compass needle aligns with Earth's magnetic field lines, with its north pole pointing toward magnetic north (which is near but not exactly at geographic north).
4. **Low friction**: The needle is mounted with minimal friction, allowing it to rotate freely and align with the magnetic field.
5. **Magnetic declination**: There's a difference between magnetic north (where compasses point) and true north (geographic north), called magnetic declination, which varies by location.
6. **Magnetic field variations**: Earth's magnetic field changes over time and varies by location. The magnetic poles slowly drift, and local magnetic anomalies can affect compass readings, requiring adjustments for accurate navigation.

### Real Examples

- A compass needle always points north (toward magnetic north) when used away from other magnetic influences, demonstrating alignment with Earth's field.
- Compasses can be affected by nearby magnets or metal objects, which distort the local magnetic field and cause incorrect readings.

### Practical Applications

- **Navigation**: Compasses provide direction for navigation, essential for hiking, sailing, flying, and other activities requiring direction finding.
- **Smartphone compasses**: Many smartphones have electronic compasses that work similarly to traditional compasses, using magnetic sensors to detect direction.
- **Emergency navigation**: Compasses provide reliable navigation when GPS or other systems fail, making them important emergency tools.
