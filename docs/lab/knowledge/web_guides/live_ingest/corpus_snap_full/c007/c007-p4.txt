TITLE: ENERGY
# Understanding Forms and Sources of Energy

Forms of energy are terms scientists use to classify the main types of energy—kinetic and potential. Radiant (light), thermal (heat), and mechanical (motion) energy are examples of forms of energy.

Sources of energy are resources that supply the energy needed to heat our homes, run our appliances, and move our automobiles. Petroleum, coal, and wind are examples of natural energy sources.

## Light Bulb Energy Transformation Demonstration

Tell students that energy's ability to change form is what makes it useful and that when energy changes from one form to another, it is called an energy transformation. An incandescent light bulb demonstrates this: inside the light bulb, there is a thin metal wire called a filament that glows when electricity flows through it. The electricity flowing through the filament creates a great deal of friction, and eventually the friction causes the filament to become so hot that it glows "white" hot, producing visible light.

The electrical energy going into the bulb is changed into radiant (visible light) and thermal (heat) energy.

Each time an energy transformation takes place, some of the original form of energy is changed into waste heat. Waste heat occurs during any energy transformation because of the First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics.

## Battery-Operated and Hand-Generator Flashlight

A battery-operated flashlight converts stored chemical energy into electrical energy, then thermal energy, then radiant energy. A hand-generator flashlight produces radiant energy from thermal energy, produced from electrical energy, produced from mechanical energy (pumping the handle), produced from chemical energy (food). Tracing back to the original source: radiant energy (light powers the photosynthetic process in green plants), produced by nuclear energy (fusion in the Sun).
