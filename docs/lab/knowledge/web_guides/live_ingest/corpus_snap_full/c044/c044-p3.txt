## HOW TIMES HAVE CHANGED!


In order to plant and cultivate crops for themselves


and their animals, early settlers spent many backbreaking hours removing rocks from their fields.


Today, many people, especially those living in urban areas, spend a lot of time, money, and energy building rock gardens, stone patios, walkways, retaining 
walls, and even Zen gardens. Some people simply like having a few big rocks or boulders on their property as decoration .


##### Plan


###### It out!


Imagine you have just moved into a home in a new subdivision . There are no trees, plants or grass on your property. You have decided you would like a “low 
maintenance”, “water efficient” landscape.


1 . Decide on a garden style. Since you want to conserve water and have a low maintenance garden, you might choose one of the following designs:


##### Zen Garden (often referred to as a Japanese Karesansui Garden) .


This is a “dry” garden type, using stones, gravel and sand .


### Rock Garden - A few “waterwise” plants or


shrubs set among colourful rocks is simple to build and easy to care for. (Some people like to have a few large rocks or boulders or patches of river rock or 
pebbles throughout their “rockery” .

## Other Words To Know


| bedrock  The rock that lies just below the soil in the Earth’s crust. |  | 

| --- | --- | 

| compacted  Squeezed together. Rock fragments are compacted by the |  |

|  | larger mass. | 

| consolidation  The process that turns sediment into rock. |  | 

| core  The inner layer of the Earth . |  | 

| escarpment  A long high cliff of sedimentary rock. |  |

| fault  A break in layers of rock resulting from pressure and causing a |  | 

|  | shift in a section of the Earth’s crust. | 

| intrusive rock  Rock formed from magma that cooled and hardened beneath |  |

|  | atmosphere. |
