## The Three Main Types of Rocks

Almost every rock belongs to one of these groups:

1. **Igneous**

2. **Sedimentary**

3. **Metamorphic**

The difference is based on **how the rock formed**.

That is the big idea.

Geologists do not just ask, “What does this rock look like?”

They also ask: **How was this rock made?**

That question helps us understand what type of rock it is.

---

## 1. Igneous Rocks: Rocks Made from Melted Rock

**Igneous rocks form when melted rock cools and hardens.**

Deep underground, melted rock is called **magma**.

When melted rock reaches the surface, like from a volcano, it is called **lava**.

When magma or lava cools, it becomes solid rock.

That rock is called **igneous rock**.

A simple way to remember it:

**Igneous = made from melted rock.**

---

## Examples of Igneous Rocks

Common igneous rocks include:

- **Granite**

- **Basalt**

- **Obsidian**

- **Pumice**

**Granite** usually forms underground when magma cools slowly. Because it cools slowly, crystals have time to grow. That is why granite often has visible specks
 and grains.

**Basalt** usually forms when lava cools on the surface. It is often dark gray or black.

**Obsidian** is volcanic glass. It forms when lava cools very quickly.

**Pumice** is a volcanic rock full of tiny holes. Those holes come from gas bubbles trapped in the lava.

---

## How to Spot an Igneous Rock

Igneous rocks may have:

- Visible crystals

- A glassy look

- A dark volcanic color

- Tiny holes from gas bubbles

- A speckled appearance

Not every igneous rock looks the same, but they all have one thing in common: They started as melted rock.

---

## Arizona Igneous Rocks

Arizona has many igneous rocks because it has a long volcanic history.

You can find basalt, granite, volcanic tuff, and other igneous rocks in different parts of the state.

If you see dark lava rock in Arizona, there is a good chance you are looking at an igneous rock.

---

## 2. Sedimentary Rocks: Rocks Made from Layers or Pieces

**Sedimentary rocks form from pieces of older rocks, minerals, shells, mud, sand, or other material that gets pressed together over time.**

These pieces are called **sediment**.

Sediment can be moved by water, wind, ice, or gravity. Over time, sediment piles up in layers. Those layers can get buried, squeezed, and cemented together.

Eventually, they become sedimentary rock.

A simple way to remember it:

**Sedimentary = made from layers or pieces.**

---

## Examples of Sedimentary Rocks

Common sedimentary rocks include:

- **Sandstone**

- **Limestone**

- **Shale**

- **Conglomerate**

**Sandstone** forms from sand.

**Limestone** often forms from shells, coral, and sea life in ancient oceans.

**Shale** forms from mud or clay.

**Conglomerate** looks like natural concrete. It is made from rounded pebbles and rock pieces stuck together.

---

## How to Spot a Sedimentary Rock

Sedimentary rocks may have:

- Layers

- Fossils

- Sand grains

- Mud-like texture

- Pebbles stuck together

- Shell fragments

Sedimentary rocks are important because they often tell us what an area used to be like.

A desert may have limestone that formed in an ancient ocean. A mountain may contain sandstone that was once sand on a beach or riverbank.

That is one of the coolest things about geology:

The land today may not look anything like it did long ago.

---

## Arizona Sedimentary Rocks

Arizona has many sedimentary rocks.

The Grand Canyon is one of the best examples in the world. Its colorful layers show different environments from Earth’s past.

Some layers were formed by ancient seas.  

Some came from beaches.  

Some came from rivers.  

Some came from deserts.

That means Arizona’s rocks are like pages in a giant history book.

---

## 3. Metamorphic Rocks: Rocks Changed by Heat and Pressure

**Metamorphic rocks are rocks that have been changed.**

They started as one kind of rock, but heat, pressure, or fluids changed them into something new.

The rock does not fully melt. If it melted completely, it could become igneous rock later.

Instead, it gets changed while staying solid.

A simple way to remember it:

**Metamorphic = changed by heat and pressure.**

---

## Examples of Metamorphic Rocks

Common metamorphic rocks include:

- **Marble**

- **Slate**

- **Schist**

- **Gneiss**

- **Quartzite**

**Marble** forms when limestone changes.

**Slate** forms when shale changes.

**Quartzite** forms when sandstone changes.

**Schist** often has shiny minerals and can look sparkly.

**Gneiss** often has light and dark bands or stripes.

---

## How to Spot a Metamorphic Rock

Metamorphic rocks may have:

- Stripes or bands

- Sparkly minerals

- Wavy patterns

- Hard, dense texture

- Flattened layers

- Crystals lined up in one direction

These features happen because the rock was squeezed and heated deep underground.

Metamorphic rocks are like rocks that went through Earth’s pressure cooker.

---

## Arizona Metamorphic Rocks

Arizona has metamorphic rocks too, especially in mountain areas where old rocks have been buried, squeezed, heated, and lifted back up over time.

In some places, you can find schist, gneiss, quartzite, and marble.

These rocks tell stories of deep underground pressure and long geologic change.

---

## Simple Comparison

Here is the easiest way to remember the three rock types:

**Igneous rocks** are made from melted rock.

**Sedimentary rocks** are made from layers or pieces.

**Metamorphic rocks** are changed by heat and pressure.

That is the big picture.

If you can remember that, you already understand one of the most important ideas in geology.

---

## Can One Rock Type Turn Into Another?

Yes.

Rocks can change over time.

This is called the **rock cycle**.

For example:

Lava cools and becomes igneous rock.  

That igneous rock breaks apart into sediment.  

The sediment gets buried and becomes sedimentary rock.  

The sedimentary rock gets pushed deep underground.  

Heat and pressure change it into metamorphic rock.  

If it melts, it can become magma again.  

Then it can cool into igneous rock.

The rock cycle is Earth’s recycling system.

Rocks are always being made, broken down, changed, and remade.

It usually happens very slowly, but it never really stops.

## Why the Three Rock Types Matter

Understanding the three types of rocks helps you understand Earth.

It helps explain:

- Volcanoes

- Mountains

- Caves

- Fossils

- Canyons

- Gemstones

- Copper deposits

- Ancient oceans

- Desert landscapes

- Arizona geology

It also helps when you are trying to identify rocks.

Instead of only asking, “What color is it?” you can ask a better question: **How did this rock form?**

That question gets you closer to the answer.

---

## Beginner Rock Identification Questions

When you find a rock, ask yourself:

**Does it look like it came from lava or magma?**  

It might be igneous.

**Does it have layers, grains, fossils, or pieces stuck together?**  

It might be sedimentary.

**Does it have bands, stripes, sparkle, or signs of being squeezed?**  

It might be metamorphic.

You do not have to get it right every time. Rock identification takes practice.

Even experienced rockhounds sometimes need field guides, maps, tests, and help from other people.

The goal at first is not to know everything.

The goal is to start noticing clues.

## Arizona Is a Great Outdoor Classroom

Arizona is an amazing place to learn the three rock types.

You can see volcanic rocks, ancient sedimentary layers, and old metamorphic rocks across the state.

That means Arizona is like a giant outdoor geology classroom.

You can learn while hiking, camping, rockhounding, visiting state parks, walking through washes, exploring mountains, or driving through road cuts.

The more you learn, the more the landscape starts to make sense.

A black rock might tell a volcanic story.  

A layered cliff might tell an ancient ocean story.  

A sparkly striped rock might tell a deep underground pressure story.

Every rock has a story.

The three rock types help you understand what kind of story it might be.

## Simple Summary

There are three main types of rocks:

**Igneous rocks** form when melted rock cools and hardens.

**Sedimentary rocks** form from layers, pieces, shells, sand, mud, or minerals that get pressed together.

**Metamorphic rocks** form when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure.

The easiest way to remember it: **Igneous = melted rock**

**Sedimentary = layers or pieces**  

**Metamorphic = changed rock**

Once you understand these three groups, you are ready to learn more about specific rocks found in Arizona.

The three types of rocks are one of the most important basics in geology.

They help us understand how rocks form, why they look different, and what they can tell us about Earth’s past.

Some rocks were born from fire.  

Some were built from layers.  

Some were changed deep underground.

That is why rocks are not boring.

They are clues.

They are history.

They are pieces of Earth’s story.

And learning how to read that story is what makes geology rock.
