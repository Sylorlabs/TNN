 The Geological Rock Cycle

The rock cycle is a dynamic system where rocks are constantly being created, altered, destroyed, and reformed. It connects Earth’s deep interior with surface 
processes, shaping landscapes and influencing natural resources.

Igneous Rocks

Formation: Created when molten magma or lava cools and solidifies.

-Types:

  - Intrusive (Plutonic): Formed beneath Earth’s surface (e.g., granite).

  - Extrusive (Volcanic): Formed from lava at the surface (e.g., basalt).

- Key Features: Crystalline texture, often hard and durable.

- Cycle Path: Igneous rocks can be broken down into sediments or transformed into metamorphic rocks under heat and pressure.

 2. Sedimentary Rocks

- Formation: Produced by the accumulation, compaction, and cementation of sediments (sand, silt, clay, organic matter).

- Examples: Sandstone, limestone, shale.

- Processes Involved:

  - Weathering & Erosion: Break down existing rocks into particles.

  - Transportation & Deposition: Sediments carried by wind, water, or ice.

  - Lithification: Sediments compact and cement into rock.

- Cycle Path: Sedimentary rocks can be buried and metamorphosed, or melted back into magma.

 3. Metamorphic Rocks

- Formation: Created when existing igneous or sedimentary rocks are subjected to intense heat and pressure without melting.

- Examples: Marble (from limestone), schist, gneiss.

- Processes Involved:

  - Recrystallization: Minerals reorganize into new structures.

  -Foliation: Layered textures due to directional pressure.

- Cycle Path: Metamorphic rocks can melt into magma or weather into sediments.

How They Interconnect

- Igneous → Sedimentary: Weathering and erosion break igneous rocks into sediments.

- Sedimentary → Metamorphic: Burial and pressure transform sedimentary rocks.

- Metamorphic → Igneous: Melting forms magma, which cools into igneous rocks again.
