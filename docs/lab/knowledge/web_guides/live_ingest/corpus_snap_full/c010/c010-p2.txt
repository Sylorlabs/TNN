# Flexi answers - Explain how the Earth's tilt is the cause of the seasons, NOT the distance from the Sun. | CK-12 Foundation


Flexi Says:


## The Earth's tilt is the primary reason for the seasons. The Earth's axis is tilted at an angle of about 23.5 degrees relative to its orbit around the Sun.
This tilt means that as the Earth orbits the Sun, different parts of the Earth receive different amounts of sunlight at different times of the year. During
summer, the Earth's tilt causes the Sun's rays to hit that part of the Earth more directly, resulting in more concentrated sunlight and warmer temperatures. In
contrast, during winter, the tilt causes the Sun's rays to hit at a more oblique angle, spreading the sunlight out over a larger area and resulting in cooler
temperatures. It's important to note that the Earth's distance from the Sun varies slightly during its orbit due to its elliptical path, but this variation has
a much smaller effect on the Earth's climate than the tilt of its axis.
