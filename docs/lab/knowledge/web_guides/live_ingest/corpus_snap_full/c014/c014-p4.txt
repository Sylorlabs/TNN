# Percentage and Decimal Review Explained: Definition, Examples, Practice & Video Lessons

Topic summary

**Percentage and Decimal Review** centers on converting between **percentages** and **decimals** and using that skill to find **percentage change**. A
percentage can be rewritten as a decimal by moving the decimal point two places to the left, and a decimal can be rewritten as a percentage by moving the
decimal point two places to the right. If a decimal point is not shown, it is implied at the end of the number.

A key idea is that **percent change** measures how much a value has changed relative to its **original value**. The core formula is \( \text{percent
change}=\frac{\text{new value}-\text{original value}}{\text{original value}} \). This calculation first gives a decimal, which is then converted to a
percentage by moving the decimal point two places to the right.

### Converting Percentages and Decimals Video Summary

Understanding the relationship between percentages and decimals is essential for various mathematical applications. To convert a percentage to a decimal, you
need to identify the position of the decimal point, which is often implied at the end of whole numbers. The conversion process involves moving the decimal point
two places to the left. For example, converting 50% to a decimal results in 0.50, which can also be expressed as 0.5. Similarly, 1.25% becomes 0.0125 after
moving the decimal two places left, filling in with a zero as necessary.

When converting larger percentages, such as 120%, the decimal point is moved two places left, yielding 1.2. Conversely, for smaller percentages like 5%, the
conversion to decimal results in 0.05. This highlights the importance of recognizing the difference between 5% and 50%, as their decimal representations are
significantly different: 0.05 and 0.50, respectively.

For decimals to percentages, the process is reversed. You move the decimal point two places to the right. For instance, 0.34 becomes 34% and 0.61 converts to
61%. When dealing with smaller decimals, such as 0.06, the conversion results in 6%. A decimal like 0.4512 translates to 45.12%. It's also important to note
that a decimal of 1 implies 100% when the decimal point is moved two places to the right.

In summary, mastering the conversion between percentages and decimals is crucial for effective mathematical problem-solving. This skill allows for better
comprehension of numerical data and enhances overall mathematical fluency.

### Calculating Percentage Change Video Summary

To calculate percentage change, you need to determine how much a value has changed in relation to its original value. The formula for percentage change is:

**Percentage Change = \(\frac{\text{Change in} x}{\text{Original Value of} x}\)**

Here, the "Change in x" can be calculated as the new value minus the original value. For example, if last year's sales revenue was \$550,000 and this year's
sales revenue increased to \$800,000, you can find the percentage change as follows:

1. Calculate the change in sales revenue:

**Change in Sales Revenue = New Value - Original Value**

Change in Sales Revenue = \$800,000 - \$550,000 = \$250,000

2. Now, apply the percentage change formula:

**Percentage Change = \(\frac{250,000}{550,000}\)**

Percentage Change = 0.4545 (as a decimal)

3. To convert this decimal into a percentage, move the decimal point two places to the right:

Percentage Change = 45.45%

This means that the sales revenue increased by 45.45% from last year to this year. Understanding how to calculate percentage change is essential for analyzing
growth or decline in various contexts, such as sales, population, or any measurable quantity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do you convert a percentage to a decimal?**

A: To convert a percentage to a decimal, you move the decimal point two places to the left. For example, if you have 50%, the decimal point is implied at the
end, so moving it two places left gives you 0.50 or simply 0.5. If the percentage is less than 1%, like 1.25%, you still move the decimal two places left, which
results in 0.0125. This method works for any percentage, including those greater than 100%, such as 120%, which becomes 1.2 as a decimal.

**Q: How do you convert a decimal to a percentage?**

A: To convert a decimal to a percentage, move the decimal point two places to the right. For example, 0.34 becomes 34%, and 0.061 becomes 6.1%. If the decimal
is very small, like 0.0004, moving the decimal two places right gives 0.04%, which is a very small percentage. For whole numbers like 1, the decimal is implied
at the end, so moving it two places right results in 100%. This conversion helps in understanding values in percentage terms.

**Q: What is the formula to calculate percentage change?**

A: The formula to calculate percentage change is new&#x2212;originaloriginal. This means you subtract the original value from the new value, then divide the
result by the original value. The result is a decimal representing the change. To express it as a percentage, move the decimal point two places to the right.
For example, if sales increased from 550,000 to 800,000, the change is (800,000 - 550,000) / 550,000 = 0.4545, which is 45.45% increase.

**Q: Why is it important to understand the position of the decimal point when converting between percentages and decimals?**

A: Understanding the decimal point's position is crucial because it determines the value's scale. For example, 5% is 0.05 as a decimal, while 50% is 0.5. If you
misplace the decimal, you could confuse these values, leading to incorrect calculations. Also, when converting percentages greater than 100%, like 120%, the
decimal becomes 1.2, which is greater than 1. Recognizing the implied decimal point at the end of whole numbers helps avoid errors during conversion.

**Q: How do you interpret a percentage change result?**

A: A percentage change result tells you how much a value has increased or decreased relative to its original amount. A positive percentage means an increase,
while a negative percentage indicates a decrease. For example, a 45.45% increase means the new value is 45.45% higher than the original. This helps in
understanding growth or decline in economic data, sales, or other measurements. Always remember to convert the decimal result from the formula into a percentage
by moving the decimal two places to the right.
