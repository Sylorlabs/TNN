TITLE: What Are Reflection and Refraction? Understanding the Differences and How Light Changes Direction
Reflection and refraction are two of the most important ways light changes direction. These fundamental physics concepts explain how light waves behave when they encounter different surfaces and materials, making them essential for understanding everyday optical effects.

Knowing the difference between reflection and refraction helps explain why mirrors produce clear images, why objects appear bent in water, and how cameras and eyeglasses focus light. Once these ideas are understood, many familiar observations become much easier to explain.

## What Is Reflection in Physics?

Reflection happens when light hits a surface and bounces back into the same medium instead of passing through it. Mirrors are one of the most common examples of reflection in everyday life.

The law of reflection states that the angle of the incoming light equals the angle of the reflected light. Both angles are measured from an imaginary line called the normal, allowing mirrors to produce clear and accurate images.

Smooth surfaces create sharp reflections, while rough surfaces scatter light waves in different directions. Since the light stays in the same medium, this is a key difference between reflection and refraction.

## What Is Refraction and Why Does Light Bend?

Refraction occurs when light moves from one medium to another, such as from air into water or glass. Because light travels at different speeds in different materials, it bends as it crosses the boundary.

This bending follows Snell's law, which helps predict how light waves change direction. Different materials bend light by different amounts, allowing lenses to focus images and prisms to separate colors.

Unlike reflection, refraction lets light continue through a new medium at a different angle. This explains why a straw appears bent in water and why eyeglasses, cameras, microscopes, and fiber-optic cables work.

## What Is the Difference Between Reflection and Refraction?

The easiest way to understand the difference is to remember that reflection bounces light back, while refraction bends light into a new material. Reflection keeps the ray in its original medium, whereas refraction changes both its direction and medium.
