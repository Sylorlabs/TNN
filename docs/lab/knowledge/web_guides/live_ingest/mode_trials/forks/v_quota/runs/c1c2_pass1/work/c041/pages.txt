S|# What's an eclipse?
S|1 / 12 What is an eclipse?
S|An eclipse happens when one object in space blocks another from view.
S|That happens because the Moon and Earth are constantly moving in orbit around the Sun.
S|As the Earth and Moon move around, sometimes they are lined up with the Sun.
S|Although the Sun is a lot bigger than the Moon, it is also a lot farther away from Earth, so the Sun and Moon can look almost the same size from Earth.
S|On Earth we see solar eclipses--when the Moon blocks the Sun--or Lunar eclipses—when Earth blocks the Moon.
S|In a Lunar eclipse, the Moon, Earth, and Sun are lined up with Earth between the Moon and the Sun.
S|In that case, Earth blocks the Sun from shining on the Moon, so Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon.
S|A solar eclipse can be partial or total.
S|In both kinds, when the Moon comes between Earth and the Sun, it casts a shadow on Earth.
S|In a partial eclipse, only part of the Sun is blocked from view.
S|In a total eclipse, the Sun is completely blocked for people viewing it from Earth.
S|The Moon casts a dark shadow on Earth, and the sky goes completely dark.
S|It seems like it is nighttime.
S|The total eclipse only lasts for a few minutes, as the Moon moves past the Sun.
S|Only the people within the small area covered by the center of the Moon’s shadow will see a total eclipse.
S|People just outside of that area will see a partial eclipse.
S|Solar eclipses can be dangerous to look at directly.
S|If you’re lucky enough to view one in-person you should only look at the solar eclipse through special viewing glasses.
S|Otherwise, your eyes could be permanently damaged.
S|Total solar eclipses do not happen very often.
S|Even though Earth and the Moon are always moving in a regular pattern through the sky, their orbits are not perfectly circular.
S|They don’t always line up in the same way in relation to the Sun.

S|## Frequently Asked Questions **Q: 1.
S|What are solar and lunar eclipses in simple terms for kids?** A: Think of the Sun, Earth, and Moon as friends playing hide-and-seek in space.
S|A solar eclipse happens when the Moon moves between the Sun and Earth, hiding the Sun from our view for a short time.
S|A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth moves between the Sun and Moon, casting its shadow on the Moon and making it look dark or reddish.
S|What is the main difference between a solar and a lunar eclipse?** A: The main difference is the order of the Sun, Earth, and Moon.
S|Here's a simple breakdown:Solar Eclipse: The Moon is in the middle (Sun-Moon-Earth alignment).
S|It blocks the Sun's light from reaching Earth.Lunar Eclipse: The Earth is in the middle (Sun-Earth-Moon alignment).
S|Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
S|How does a solar eclipse make the day turn dark?** A: During a total solar eclipse, the Moon lines up perfectly to block the Sun's entire face.
S|Even though the Moon is much smaller than the Sun, it's also much closer to Earth, so it appears to be the same size in the sky.
S|When it covers the Sun completely, its shadow falls on a small part of Earth, and for people in that shadow, the sky gets dark like it's night-time for a few minutes.
S|How does a lunar eclipse happen, and why does the Moon sometimes look red?** A: A lunar eclipse happens when the full Moon passes directly into Earth's shadow.
S|This shadow has two parts: a lighter outer shadow (penumbra) and a darker inner shadow (umbra).
S|When the Moon is fully inside the umbra, it can look reddish.
S|This is because some sunlight still reaches the Moon by bending through Earth's atmosphere.
S|Our atmosphere filters out most of the blue light, letting the red light pass through, which then illuminates the Moon.
S|This is often called a 'Blood Moon'.
S|If the Moon orbits Earth every month, why don't we have eclipses all the time?** A: This is a great question!
S|The reason we don't have eclipses every month is that the Moon's orbit around the Earth is slightly tilted.
S|Imagine Earth's path around the Sun is a flat plate.
S|The Moon's path is like another plate tilted at an angle.
S|Because of this tilt, the Moon's shadow usually passes just above or below Earth, and Earth's shadow usually misses the Moon.
S|Eclipses only happen on those rare occasions when all three align perfectly on the same level.
S|Why must you never look directly at a solar eclipse without protection?** A: It is extremely dangerous to look at a solar eclipse directly because the Sun's rays are incredibly powerful.
S|Even when most of the Sun is covered, the remaining sliver can emit enough ultraviolet (UV) radiation to cause serious and permanent damage to your eyes, a condition called solar retinopathy.
S|You should only ever view a solar eclipse through certified eclipse glasses or a special-purpose solar filter.
S|What are the different types of solar and lunar eclipses?** A: Eclipses can look different depending on how perfectly the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up.
S|The main types are:Types of Solar Eclipses: A Total eclipse is when the Moon completely blocks the Sun.
S|A Partial eclipse is when it only blocks a part of the Sun.
S|An Annular eclipse happens when the Moon is a bit farther from Earth and appears smaller, so it creates a bright 'ring of fire' around it.Types of Lunar Eclipses: A Total eclipse is when the entire Moon enters Earth's darkest shadow.
S|A Partial eclipse occurs when only a part of the Moon goes through the shadow.
S|A Penumbral eclipse is very faint, as the Moon only passes through Earth's lighter outer shadow.
S|Why were people in ancient times scared of eclipses?** A: In ancient times, people did not have the scientific knowledge to understand what eclipses were.
S|To them, the Sun was a source of life and light.
S|Seeing it suddenly disappear during the day (a solar eclipse) or watching the Moon turn a dark, bloody red (a lunar eclipse) was a terrifying and mysterious event.
S|Many cultures believed it was a bad omen or a sign that the gods were angry.
S|Today, we know they are just a beautiful and natural alignment of shadows in space.
