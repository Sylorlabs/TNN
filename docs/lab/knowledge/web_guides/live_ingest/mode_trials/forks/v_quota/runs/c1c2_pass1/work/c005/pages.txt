S|## What Is Reflection in Physics?
S|Reflection happens when light hits a surface and bounces back into the same medium instead of passing through it.
S|Mirrors are one of the most common examples of reflection in everyday life.
S|The law of reflection states that the angle of the incoming light equals the angle of the reflected light.
S|Both angles are measured from an imaginary line called the normal, allowing mirrors to produce clear and accurate images.
S|Smooth surfaces create sharp reflections, while rough surfaces scatter light waves in different directions.
S|Since the light stays in the same medium, this is a key difference between reflection and refraction.
S|## What Is Refraction and Why Does Light Bend?
S|Refraction occurs when light moves from one medium to another, such as from air into water or glass.
S|Because light travels at different speeds in different materials, it bends as it crosses the boundary.
S|This bending follows Snell's law, which helps predict how light waves change direction.
S|Different materials bend light by different amounts, allowing lenses to focus images and prisms to separate colors.
S|Unlike reflection, refraction lets light continue through a new medium at a different angle.
S|This explains why a straw appears bent in water and why eyeglasses, cameras, microscopes, and fiber-optic cables work.
S|## What Is the Difference Between Reflection and Refraction?
S|The easiest way to understand the difference is to remember that reflection bounces light back, while refraction bends light into a new material.
S|Reflection keeps the ray in its original medium, whereas refraction changes both its direction and medium.
