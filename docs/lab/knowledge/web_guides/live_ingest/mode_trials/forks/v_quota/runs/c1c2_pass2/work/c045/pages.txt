S|# What Is a Volcano?
S|| NASA Space Place – NASA Science for Kids **The Short Answer:** A **volcano** is an opening on the surface of a planet or moon that allows material warmer than its surroundings to escape from its interior.
S|When this material escapes, it causes an eruption.
S|[Image 0: Lava fountain at Kilauea Volcano, Hawai`i.] Lava fountain at Kīlauea Volcano, Hawai`i.
S|Credit: J.D Griggs, USGS A **volcano** is an opening on the surface of a planet or moon that allows material warmer than its surroundings to escape from its interior.
S|When this material escapes, it causes an eruption.
S|An eruption can be explosive, sending material high into the sky.
S|Or it can be calmer, with gentle flows of material.
S|[Image 1: This photograph shows an eruption of Mount St.
S|Helens in Washington in July 1980.] This photograph shows an eruption of Mount St.
S|Helens in Washington in July 1980.
S|This eruption sent ash 6 to 11 miles (10-18 kilometers) into the air, and was visible in Seattle, Washington, 100 miles (160 kilometers) to the north.
S|Credit: Mike Doukas, USGS These volcanic areas usually form mountains built from the many layers of rock, ash or other material that collect around them.
S|Volcanoes can be active, dormant, or extinct.
S|Active volcanoes are volcanoes that have had recent eruptions or are expected to have eruptions in the near future.
S|Dormant volcanoes no longer produce eruptions, but might again sometime in the future.
S|Extinct volcanoes will likely never erupt again.
S|[Image 2: Lava bubbles up from Kīlauea Volcano in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park.] Lava bubbles up from Kīlauea Volcano in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park.
S|Credit: Scott Horvath, USGS.
S|Volcanoes occur when material significantly warmer than its surroundings is erupted onto the surface of a planet or moon from its interior.
S|On Earth, the erupted material can be liquid rock ("lava" when it's on the surface, "magma" when it's underground), ash, cinders, and/or gas.
S|There are three reasons why magma might rise and cause eruptions onto Earth’s surface.
S|[Image 3: schematic of the three ways volcanoes can form] Volcanoes on Earth form from rising magma.
S|Magma rises in three different ways.
S|Magma can rise when pieces of Earth's crust called tectonic plates slowly move away from each other.
S|The magma rises up to fill in the space.
S|When this happens underwater volcanoes can form.
S|Magma also rises when these tectonic plates move toward each other.
S|When this happens, part of Earth's crust can be forced deep into its interior.
S|The high heat and pressure cause the crust to melt and rise as magma.
S|A final way that magma rises is over hot spots.
S|Hot spots are exactly what they sound like--hot areas inside of Earth.
S|These areas heat up magma.
S|The magma becomes less dense.
S|When it is less dense it rises.
S|Each of the reasons for rising magma are a bit different, but each can form volcanoes.
S|## Are There Volcanoes Elsewhere In Our Solar System?
S|There have been a lot of volcanoes on other planets in the past.
S|Some places in our solar system have active volcanoes erupting right now!
S|Venus and Mars are covered with extinct volcanoes.
S|Some of the moons of Jupiter, Saturn, and Neptune actually have eruptions happening right now!
S|We have taken pictures of these with many different NASA spacecraft!

S|# Facts On Volcano Eruptions For Kids By Doug Donald Updated Mar 24, 2022 [Image 0: ...] An erupting volcano is one of nature's most spectacular and breathtaking events.
S|Few things demonstrate the power of Earth's natural forces more vividly than a volcano's flying rocks, flowing lava and ash clouds rising miles into the sky.
S|Nobody really knows how many active volcanoes there are in the world, because many have not erupted in many years and others lie unknown deep under the oceans.
S|## Why Volcanoes Erupt [Image 1: ...] ## Why Volcanoes Erupt The top surface of the Earth is called the crust.
S|Less than 20 miles thick, it sits on top of a layer of molten rock and gas called magma.
S|The crust is made of huge pieces called tectonic plates that fit together like a puzzle, but heat and pressure from the Earth's core make them move slowly against each other, forming cracks in the crust.
S|A volcano is a mountain located on a crack in the crust, opening into the pool of magma beneath it.
S|When heat from deep inside the earth creates enough pressure, magma and gases push up through the opening and erupt from the volcano, spewing ash, steam, rocks, and molten lava into the air.
S|## Magma and Lava [Image 2: ...] ## Magma and Lava The molten or liquid rock inside a volcano is called magma.
S|Magma is made mostly of rock and gases, but sometimes contains suspended crystals.
S|Magma flowing out of a volcano during an eruption is called lava.
S|Lava is very hot, sometimes more than 2,000 degrees Fahrenheit, and it glows red or white hot as it flows.
S|Lava turns into volcanic rock when it cools.
S|Some lava from Hawaii's Kilaeua volcano flows to the ocean where it cools, hardens into rock and makes the island bigger every year.
S|## Lahars [Image 3: ...] ## Lahars Like other mountains, many volcanoes have snow, ice and sometimes glaciers on their slopes.
S|Heat from an erupting volcano can melt the snow and ice.
S|When the melted snow mixes with rocks and ash from the volcano it creates a huge, dangerous mudflow called a Lahar.
S|Lahars move far too fast for anyone in their path to outrun them.
S|They usually flow down valleys and river beds and are destructive and deadly if they flow into a populated area or town.
S|In 1985 lahars from the Nevad del Ruiz volcano in Columbia buried the entire town of Armero, killing over 20,000 people.
S|## Pyroclastic Flows [Image 4: ...] ## Pyroclastic Flows Some erupting volcanoes produce a mixture of extremely hot gases and rock called a pyroclastic flow.
S|Pyroclastic flows look like giant dirty clouds that sweep down the sides of the volcano and destroy everything in their path.
S|They can reach temperatures of over 1,000 degrees Fahrenheit and move faster than 400 miles per hour.
S|Pyroclastic flows travel many miles away from the volcano and can even travel over water.
S|The heat from a pyroclastic flow can melt snow and ice and create a lahar.
S|## Other Volcano Eruption Facts [Image 5: ...] ## Other Volcano Eruption Facts The eruption of Mount St.
S|Helens in 1980 literally blew the top off of the mountain.
S|Helens is now 1,300 feet shorter than it was before the eruption.
S|Not all eruptions are violent and scary, but they can still be dangerous.
S|Sometimes an eruption is just steam and ash billowing from volcano.
S|But volcanic ash is made from crushed rock and can make people sick.
S|Volcano eruptions can change the weather.
S|The ash in the air can make travel around the world, blocking sunlight and making temperatures cooler for months.
