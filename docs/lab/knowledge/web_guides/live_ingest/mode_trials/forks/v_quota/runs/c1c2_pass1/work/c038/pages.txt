S|# DOE Explains...Batteries | Department of Energy **Batteries** and similar devices accept, store, and release electricity on demand.
S|Batteries use chemistry, in the form of chemical potential, to store energy, just like many other everyday energy sources.
S|For example, logs and oxygen both store energy in their chemical bonds until burning converts some of that chemical energy to heat.
S|Gasoline and oxygen mixtures have stored chemical potential energy until it is converted to mechanical energy in a car engine.
S|Similarly, for batteries to work, electricity must be converted into a chemical potential form before it can be readily stored.
S|Batteries consist of two electrical terminals called the cathode and the anode, separated by a chemical material called an electrolyte.
S|To accept and release energy, a battery is coupled to an external circuit.
S|Electrons move through the circuit, while simultaneously ions (atoms or molecules with an electric charge) move through the electrolyte.
S|In a rechargeable battery, electrons and ions can move either direction through the circuit and electrolyte.
S|When the electrons move from the cathode to the anode, they increase the chemical potential energy, thus charging the battery; when they move the other direction, they convert this chemical potential energy to electricity in the circuit and discharge the battery.
S|During charging or discharging, the oppositely charged ions move inside the battery through the electrolyte to balance the charge of the electrons moving through the external circuit and produce a sustainable, rechargeable system.
S|Once charged, the battery can be disconnected from the circuit to store the chemical potential energy for later use as electricity.
S|Batteries were invented in 1800, but their complex chemical processes are still being studied.
S|Scientists are using new tools to better understand the electrical and chemical processes in batteries to produce a new generation of highly efficient, electrical energy storage.
S|For example, they are developing improved materials for the anodes, cathodes, and electrolytes in batteries.
S|Scientists study processes in rechargeable batteries because they do not completely reverse as the battery is charged and discharged.
S|Over time, the lack of a complete reversal can change the chemistry and structure of battery materials, which can reduce battery performance and safety.
S|## DOE Office of Science Contributions to Electrical Energy Storage Research Research supported by the DOE Office of Science, Office of Basic Energy Sciences (BES) has yielded significant improvements in electrical energy storage.
S|But we are still far from comprehensive solutions for next-generation energy storage using brand-new materials that can dramatically improve how much energy a battery can store.
S|This storage is critical to integrating renewable energy sources into our electricity supply.
S|Because improving battery technology is essential to the widespread use of plug-in electric vehicles, storage is also key to reducing our dependency on petroleum for transportation.
S|BES supports research by individual scientists and at multi-disciplinary centers.
S|The largest center is the Joint Center for Energy Storage Research (JCESR), a DOE Energy Innovation Hub.
S|This center studies electrochemical materials and phenomena at the atomic and molecular scale and uses computers to help design new materials.
S|This new knowledge will enable scientists to design energy storage that is safer, lasts longer, charges faster, and has greater capacity.
S|As scientists supported by the BES program achieve new advances in battery science, these advances are used by applied researchers and industry to advance applications in transportation, the electricity grid, communication, and security.
S|## Electrical Energy Storage Facts - The 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded jointly to John B.
S|Stanley Whittingham, and Akira Yoshino "for the development of lithium-ion batteries." - The Electrolyte Genome at JCESR has produced a computational database with more than 26,000 molecules that can be used to calculate key electrolyte properties for new, advanced batteries.

S|# Battery Reactions and Chemistry | HowStuffWorks A lot happens inside a battery when you pop it into your flashlight, remote control or other wire-free device.
S|While the processes by which they produce electricity differ slightly from battery to battery, the basic idea remains the same.
S|When a load completes the circuit between the two terminals, the battery produces electricity through a series of electrochemical reactions between the anode, cathode and electrolyte.
S|The anode experiences an **oxidation reaction** in which two or more **ions** (electrically charged atoms or molecules) from the electrolyte combine with the anode, producing a compound and releasing one or more electrons.
S|At the same time, the cathode goes through a **reduction reaction** in which the cathode substance, ions and free electrons also combine to form compounds.
S|While this action may sound complicated, it's actually very simple: The reaction in the anode creates electrons, and the reaction in the cathode absorbs them.
S|The net product is electricity.
S|The battery will continue to produce electricity until one or both of the electrodes run out of the substance necessary for the reactions to occur.
S|Modern batteries use a variety of chemicals to power their reactions.
S|Common battery chemistries include: - **Zinc-carbon battery**: The zinc-carbon chemistry is common in many inexpensive AAA, AA, C and D dry cell batteries.
S|The anode is zinc, the cathode is manganese dioxide, and the electrolyte is ammonium chloride or zinc chloride.
S|- **Alkaline battery**: This chemistry is also common in AA, C and D dry cell batteries.
S|The cathode is composed of a manganese dioxide mixture, while the anode is a zinc powder.
S|It gets its name from the potassium hydroxide electrolyte, which is an alkaline substance.
S|- **Lithium-ion battery (rechargeable)**: Lithium chemistry is often used in high-performance devices, such as cell phones, digital cameras and even electric cars.
S|A variety of substances are used in lithium batteries, but a common combination is a lithium cobalt oxide cathode and a carbon anode.
S|- **Lead-acid battery (rechargeable)**: This is the chemistry used in a typical car battery.
S|The electrodes are usually made of lead dioxide and metallic lead, while the electrolyte is a sulfuric acid solution.

S|# How Batteries Work for Kids | What is a Battery?
S|| Science for Kids - ClassX This lesson introduces the basics of batteries, explaining their function as portable energy sources that power everyday devices through chemical reactions that create electricity.
S|It covers the flow of electrons, the importance of matching battery terminals, and various types of batteries suited for different applications.
S|Additionally, it emphasizes the importance of battery safety and recycling to protect both users and the environment.
S|### Understanding Batteries: The Basics #### What is a Battery?
S|Batteries are cool gadgets that store energy and give us electricity without needing to be plugged into a wall.
S|They help power lots of things we use every day, like remote control toys, flashlights, and even cars!
S|Batteries make our lives easier by letting us use these things wherever we go.
S|#### How Do Batteries Work?
S|Inside a battery, special chemical reactions happen that create electricity.
S|A battery has two ends: a positive side (with a plus sign) and a negative side (with a minus sign).
S|When you put a battery in a device, you have to match the ends correctly—negative to negative and positive to positive.
S|This lets tiny particles called electrons move from the negative side, through the device, and back to the positive side, creating electricity.
S|#### The Flow of Electrons The movement of electrons is what we call an electric current.
S|When you turn on a device, it completes a path, or circuit, allowing electricity to flow.
S|This flow is what powers everything from small toys to big machines.
S|#### Types of Batteries Batteries come in all sorts of shapes and sizes, each made for different jobs.
S|Here are some common types: - **AA Batteries:** These are regular-sized batteries used in many household items.
S|- **AAA Batteries:** Smaller than AA batteries, they are often used in things like remote controls.
S|- **Button Cell Batteries:** These tiny batteries are found in watches and small gadgets.
S|- **9V Batteries:** Often used in smoke detectors and other devices that need more power.
S|- **Car Batteries:** Big batteries that help start cars.
S|Each type of battery is special and works best in certain devices.
S|#### Battery Safety and Recycling Batteries are super useful, but they have chemicals inside that can be dangerous if not handled properly.
S|Never open a battery, as it can be harmful.
S|Also, don’t throw batteries in the trash.
S|Instead, take them to recycling centers, which you can find at many grocery stores and other places.
S|Recycling helps keep our planet clean and safe!
S|#### Conclusion Batteries are amazing devices that give us portable power for many things we use every day.
S|Knowing how they work and the different types can help us appreciate them even more.
S|Always remember to handle batteries safely and recycle them to protect our environment.
S|If you’re curious about batteries or other science topics, feel free to ask more questions!
