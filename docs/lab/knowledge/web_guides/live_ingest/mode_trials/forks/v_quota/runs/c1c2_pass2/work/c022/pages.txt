S|# Your Heart & Circulatory System (for Kids) - Children's of Alabama We see and hear about hearts everywhere.
S|A long time ago, people even thought that their emotions came from their hearts, maybe because the heart beats faster when a person is scared or excited.
S|Now we know that emotions come from the, and in this case, the brain tells the heart to speed up.
S|So what's the heart up to, then?
S|How does it keep busy?
S|What does it look like?
S|Let's find out.
S|### What Does the Heart Do?
S|Your heart is really a muscle.
S|It's located a little to the left of the middle of your chest, and it's about the size of your fist.
S|Your heart is sort of like a pump, or two pumps in one.
S|It pumps blood around your body.
S|provides your body with the oxygen and nutrients it needs.
S|It also carries away waste.
S|- The **right side** of your heart **receives blood from the body** and **pumps it to the lungs**.
S|- The **left side** of the heart r**eceives blood from the lungs** and **pumps it out to the body**.
S|### How Does the Heart Beat?
S|Before each beat, your heart fills with blood.
S|Then it **contracts** to pump the blood along.
S|When the heart contracts, it squeezes — try squeezing your hand into a fist.
S|That's sort of like what your heart does so it can pump the blood.
S|Your heart does this all day and all night, all the time.
S|The heart is one hard worker!
S|### What Are the Parts of the Heart?
S|The heart is made up of four different blood-filled areas, and each of these areas is called a chamber.
S|There are two chambers on each side of the heart.
S|One chamber is on the top and one chamber is on the bottom.
S|- The two chambers on top are called the **atria** (say: AY-tree-uh).
S|The heart has a left atrium and a right atrium.
S|- The two chambers on the bottom are called the **ventricles** (say: VEN-trih-kulz).
S|The heart has a left ventricle and a right ventricle.
S|### What Are the Atria?
S|And What's An Atrium?
S|The atria are the chambers that fill with the blood returning to the heart from the body and lungs.
S|If you're talking only about one, call it an **atrium**.
S|### What Are the Ventricles?
S|The ventricles are the chambers that pump out the blood to the body and lungs.
S|### What's the Septum?
S|Running down the middle of the heart is a thick wall of muscle called the **septum** (say: SEP-tum).
S|The septum's job is to separate the left side and the right side of the heart.
S|The atria and ventricles work as a team — the atria fill with blood, then dump it into the ventricles.
S|The ventricles then squeeze, pumping blood out of the heart.
S|While the ventricles are squeezing, the atria refill and get ready for the next contraction.
S|So when the blood gets pumped, how does it know which way to go?
S|### What Do the Heart Valves Do?
S|Your blood relies on four special valves inside the heart.
S|A valve lets something in and keeps it there by closing — think of walking through a door.
S|The door shuts behind you and keeps you from going backward.
S|- Two of the heart valves are the **mitral** (say: MY-trul) **valve** and the **tricuspid** (say: try-KUS-pid) **valve**.
S|They let blood flow from the atria to the ventricles.
S|- The other two are called the **aortic** (say: ay-OR-tik) **valve** and **pulmonary** (say: PUL-muh-ner-ee) **valve**, and they're in charge of controlling the flow as the blood leaves the heart.
S|These valves all work to keep the blood flowing forward.
S|They open up to let the blood move ahead, then they close quickly to keep the blood from flowing backward.
S|And How Does Blood Circulate?
S|Movement of blood through the heart and around the body is called **circulation** (say: sur-kyoo-LAY-shun), and your heart is really good at it — it takes less than 60 seconds to pump blood to every cell in your body.
S|Your body needs this steady supply of blood to keep it working right.
S|Blood delivers oxygen to all the body's cells.
S|To stay alive, a person needs healthy, living cells.
S|Without oxygen, these cells would die.
S|If that oxygen-rich blood doesn't circulate as it should, a person could die.
S|- The **left side** of your heart sends that oxygen-rich blood out **to the body**.
S|The body takes the oxygen out of the blood and uses it in your body's cells.
S|When the cells use the oxygen, they make carbon dioxide and other stuff that gets carried away by the blood.
S|It's like the blood delivers lunch to the cells and then has to pick up the trash!
S|- The **returning blood** enters the **right side** of the heart.
S|The right ventricle pumps the blood to the lungs for a little freshening up.
S|In the lungs, carbon dioxide is removed from the blood and sent out of the body when we exhale.
S|An inhale, of course, and a fresh breath of oxygen that can enter the blood to start the process again.
S|And remember, it all happens in about a minute!
S|### What Do Arteries Do?
S|And What Do Veins Do?
S|You probably guessed that the blood just doesn't slosh around your body once it leaves the heart.
S|It moves through many tubes called, which together are called **blood vessels**.
S|These blood vessels are attached to the heart.
S|- The blood vessels that carry blood **away from** the heart are called **arteries**.
S|- The blood vessels that carry blood **back to** the heart are called **veins** ### Listen to the Lub-Dub When you go for a checkup, your doctor uses a stethoscope to listen carefully to your heart.
S|A healthy heart makes a lub-dub sound with each beat.
S|This sound comes from the valves shutting on the blood inside the heart.
S|The first sound (the lub) happens when the mitral and tricuspid valves close.
S|The next sound (the dub) happens when the aortic and pulmonary valves close after the blood has been squeezed out of the heart.
S|Next time you go to the doctor, ask if you can listen to the lub-dub, too.
S|### How Can I Find My Pulse?
S|Even though your heart is inside you, there is a cool way to know it's working from the outside.
S|It's your pulse.
S|You can find your pulse by lightly pressing on the skin anywhere there's a large artery running just beneath your skin.
S|Two good places to find it are on the side of your neck and the inside of your wrist, just below the thumb.
S|You'll know that you've found your pulse when you can feel a small beat under your skin.
S|Each beat is caused by the contraction (squeezing) of your heart.
S|If you want to find out what your heart rate is, use a watch with a second hand and count how many beats you feel in 1 minute.
S|When you are resting, you will probably feel between 70 and 100 beats per minute.
S|When you run around a lot, your body needs a lot more oxygen-filled blood.
S|Your heart pumps faster to supply the oxygen-filled blood that your body needs.
S|You may even feel your heart pounding in your chest.
S|Try running in place or jumping rope for a few minutes and taking your pulse again — now how many beats do you count in 1 minute?
S|### How Can I Keep My Heart Healthy?
S|Most kids are born with a healthy heart and it's important to keep yours in good shape.
S|Here are some things that you can do to help keep your heart happy: - Remember that your heart is a muscle.
S|If you want it to be strong, you need to it.
S|How do you do it?
S|By being active in a way that gets you huffing and puffing, like jumping rope, dancing, or playing basketball.
S|Try to be active every day for at least 30 minutes!
S|An hour would be even better for your heart!
S|- Eat a variety of healthy foods and avoid foods high in unhealthy fats, such as saturated fats and trans fats (reading can help you figure out if your favorite snacks contain these unhealthy ingredients).
S|- Try to eat at least five servings of fruits and vegetables each day.
S|- Avoid sugary soft drinks and fruit drinks.
S|It can damage the heart and blood vessels.
S|Your heart deserves to be loved for all the work it does.
S|It started pumping blood before you were born and will continue pumping throughout your whole life.

S|## Heart ### What Is the Heart and What Does It Do?
S|The heart is an organ that’s a pump, usually beating about 60 to 100 times per minute.
S|With each heartbeat, the heart sends blood throughout your body, carrying oxygen to every cell.
S|After delivering the oxygen, the blood returns to the heart.
S|The heart then sends the blood to the lungs to pick up more oxygen.
S|This cycle repeats over and over again.

S|## What Are the Parts of the Heart?
S|### Heart Chambers The heart has four chambers — two on the top and two on the bottom: - The two top chambers are the right atrium and the left atrium.
S|They receive the blood entering the heart.
S|A wall called the interatrial septum is between the atria.
S|- The two bottom chambers are the **right ventricle** and the **left ventricle**.
S|These pump blood out of the heart.
S|A wall called the **interventricular septum** is between the two ventricles.
S|### Heart Valves Parts of the heart called valves keep blood moving through the heart in the right direction.
S|The atria are separated from the ventricles by the **atrioventricular valves**: - The **tricuspid valve** separates the right atrium from the right ventricle.
S|- The **mitral valve** separates the left atrium from the left ventricle.
S|Two valves also separate the ventricles from the large blood vessels that carry blood leaving the heart: - The **pulmonic valve** is between the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery, which carries blood to the lungs.
S|- The **aortic valve** is between the left ventricle and the aorta, which carries blood to the body.
S|[Image 1: Two diagrams show the position of the heart in the body and a close-up, cross section of a healthy heart.
S|Blood flows from the left atrium into the left ventricle and through a valve into the aorta.] ## How Does the Heart Beat?
S|The heart gets messages from the body that tell it when to pump more or less blood depending on a person's needs.
S|For example, when you're sleeping, it pumps just enough to provide for the lower amounts of oxygen your body needs when resting.
S|But when you're exercising, the heart pumps faster so that your muscles get more oxygen and can work harder.
S|The way the heart beats is controlled by a system of electrical signals in the heart.
S|### Sinus & Atrioventricular Nodes The **sinus node**, or sinoatrial node, is a small area of tissue in the wall of the right atrium.
S|It sends out an electrical signal to start the contracting (pumping) of the heart muscle.
S|This node is called the pacemaker of the heart because it sets the rate of the heartbeat and causes the rest of the heart to contract in its rhythm.
S|These electrical impulses make the atria contract first.
S|Then the impulses travel down to the **atrioventricular node**, or AV node, which acts as a kind of relay station.
S|From here, the electrical signal travels through the right and left ventricles, making them contract.
S|### Systole & Diastole One complete heartbeat is made up of two phases: #### Systole The first phase is called **systole** (SISS-tuh-lee).
S|This is when the ventricles contract and pump blood into the aorta and pulmonary artery.
S|During systole, the atrioventricular valves close, creating the first sound — the lub — of a heartbeat.
S|When the atrioventricular valves close, this keeps the blood from going back up into the atria.
S|During this time, the aortic and pulmonary valves are open to let blood into the aorta and pulmonary artery.
S|When the ventricles finish contracting, the aortic and pulmonary valves close to prevent blood from flowing back into the ventricles.
S|These valves closing is what creates the second sound — the dub — of a heartbeat.
S|#### Diastole The second phase is called **diastole** (die-AS-tuh-lee).
S|This is when the atrioventricular valves open and the ventricles relax.
S|This allows the ventricles to fill with blood from the atria and get ready for the next heartbeat.

S|## Circulatory System ### What Is the Circulatory System and What Does It Do?
S|The circulatory system is made up of your heart and three main kinds of blood vessels: arteries, veins, and capillaries.
S|The system moves blood in these blood vessels throughout the body.
S|The circulatory system carries oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to cells, and removes waste products, like carbon dioxide.
S|These roadways travel in one direction only, to keep things going where they should.
S|**Arteries** carry blood away from the heart, and **veins** carry blood back to the heart.
S|**Capillaries** are tiny blood vessels that connect very small arteries to very small veins.
S|### What Are the Parts of the Circulatory System?
S|Two pathways come from the heart: pulmonary circulation and systemic circulation.
S|#### Pulmonary Circulation The pulmonary circulation is a short loop from the heart to the lungs and back again.
S|The pulmonary artery is a big artery that comes from the heart.
S|It splits into two main branches and brings blood from the heart to the lungs.
S|At the lungs, the blood picks up oxygen and drops off carbon dioxide.
S|The blood then returns to the heart through the pulmonary veins.
S|#### Systemic Circulation The systemic circulation carries blood from the heart to all the other parts of the body and back again.
S|Blood that returns to the heart after pulmonary circulation has picked up lots of oxygen from the lungs.
S|So it can now go out to the body.
S|The aorta is the largest artery, and it leaves the heart carrying the oxygen-rich blood.
S|Branches from the aorta send blood to the muscles of the heart itself, as well as all other parts of the body.
S|Like a tree, the branches get smaller as they get farther from the aorta.
S|#### Blood Vessel Networks At each body part, a network of tiny blood vessels called capillaries connects the very small artery branches to very small veins.
S|The capillaries have thin walls, and through them, nutrients and oxygen are delivered to the cells.
S|At the same time, waste products from the cells go into the capillaries.
S|Once blood has picked up the waste, it then flows from the capillaries to small veins.
S|These veins lead to larger and larger veins as the blood approaches the heart.
S|Valves in the veins keep blood flowing in the right direction.
S|Two large veins that lead into the heart are the superior vena cava and inferior vena cava.
S|(The terms superior and inferior don't mean that one vein is better than the other, but that they're located above and below the heart.) Once the blood is back in the heart, it needs to reenter the pulmonary circulation and go back to the lungs to drop off the carbon dioxide and pick up more oxygen.
S|### How to Keep Your Heart Healthy To keep your heart healthy, it’s important to follow healthy habits, such as: - getting plenty of intense physical activity (like running or swimming) - eating a balanced, nutritious diet - staying at a healthy weight - quitting smoking, if you smoke - seeing your doctor regularly for checkups and talking about any family history of heart problems Let your doctor know if you have any chest pain, trouble breathing, dizzy or fainting spells, or if you feel like your heart sometimes goes really fast or skips a beat.

S|### Your Beating Heart ##### Objectives: Students will: • Feel their pulse, at rest and after exercise • Count and compare heart rates • Understand how exercising is one way to keep the heart healthy ###### Materials: • Stopwatch or clock • "Healthy Heart" handout • Jump ropes (optional) ##### Class Time: 20 minutes ##### Activity: Every time your heart beats, it pushes oxygencarrying blood through your body.
S|You can feel your heart beat - it’s called finding your pulse.
S|Hold two fingers gently against your wrist or the side of your neck, where you can feel (and maybe even see) a blood vessel.
S|(You have to be very still and quiet.
S|If you have trouble, ask your teacher for help.) Then, as your teacher times 30 seconds, count how many times your heart beats.
S|Double that number (or add it to itself).
S|This is how many times your heart beats in 1 minute at rest.
S|When you move around a lot, blood needs to move oxygen around your body even faster so your heart pumps faster.
S|Sometimes you can even feel your heart pounding in your chest.
S|For 1 minute, run in place or do jumping jacks (or if there’s space, jump rope).
S|After a minute, stop, find your pulse, and count how many times your heart beats.
S|How does it compare to when you were at rest?
S|Remember, your heart is a muscle, so it’s important to keep it strong!
S|Exercise and being active help keep your heart healthy.
S|Look at the pictures on the "Healthy Heart" handout.
S|Circle all the activities that make your heart beat faster.
S|Draw a box around the activities that usually don’t make your heart beat any faster than normal.

S|### K to Grade 2 • Human Body Series # Cardiovascular System ### Circulation Circle ##### Objective: Students will: • Understand how the heart circulates blood in the body and the role of arteries and veins ###### Materials: • Beanbags or small balls for tossing • Nametags/signs (VEIN, HEART, LUNGS, ARTERY, BODY) ##### Class Time: 20 minutes ##### Activity: Every time your heart beats, it pushes blood through your body.
S|The right side of your heart receives blood from your body (through veins) and sends it to the lungs to get oxygen.
S|The left side of your heart then gets the blood from your lungs and pumps it out to the rest of your body (through arteries).
S|Your blood is like a train, carrying oxygen and nutrients to different parts of your body and then taking waste away.
S|Like train tunnels, your veins and arteries are the paths through which your blood moves.
S|Your heart, lungs, and body are like the train stations – where your blood is coming and going.
S|This whole process of moving blood through your body is called circulation.
S|Form a group with four other classmates (there should be five of you in all).
S|Each of you has a special job: VEIN, HEART, LUNGS, ARTERY, and BODY.
S|Pretend the beanbag or ball is the blood.
S|The VEIN will send blood to the HEART.
S|The HEART will send blood to the LUNGS for oxygen.
S|The LUNGS will send blood back to the HEART.
S|Then the HEART will send the blood through the ARTERY.
S|The ARTERY will send the blood to the rest of the BODY.
S|The BODY will return the blood through the VEIN.
S|Practice tossing the beanbag in this circulation.
S|When you think you’re ready, ask your teacher to time you for 1 minute.
S|How many times can your group circulate the beanbag in the correct order in 1 minute?
S|##### Extension: Your heart works really hard.
S|Can your group do what your heart does every day?
S|Try passing around three or four or even five beanbags or balls in the correct order.
S|How many times can you circulate the beanbags in 1 minute?
S|Your heart does this job thousands of times every day!
