S|# 12.1: Introduction - Biology LibreTexts 1.
S|- Page ID 24192 Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is located in the **nucleus** of eukaryotic cells (animals, plants, fungi, and protists).
S|DNA contains information to direct the cell in the manufacture of proteins.
S|**Proteins** control development, organ function, metabolism, enzymatic reactions, photosynthesis, muscle action, brain activity, and many other cellular processes.
S|DNA is often referred to as the “blueprint for life”.
S|DNA is a polymer composed of the **nucleotide bases** guanine (G), adenine (A), thymine (T), and cytosine (C), and two sugar/phosphate backbones.
S|Two DNA strands are twisted to form a **double helix**.
S|The number of nucleotide bases (G, A, T, C) in each human cell is about 3 billion.
S|The 3 billion base pairs in the **human genome** are located on 46 strands of DNA called **chromosomes**.
S|The Human Genome Project has determined the order of the nucleotides on each chromosome.
S|A **gene** is a sequence of nucleotide bases (DNA) that codes for a specific protein.
S|Human DNA contains about 20,000 genes while the cells of the rice plant contain over 40,000 genes.
S|In the DNA isolation procedure, plant cell walls and cell membranes are broken down by blending or mashing and heating the cells.
S|Detergent in the extraction solution dissolves lipids in the cell membrane causing the cells to **lyse**.
S|When cells undergo lysis, the cellular components, including the DNA, are released.
S|The technique of **filtration** uses a medium, in this case cheesecloth, to separate solids from liquids.
S|The resultant material is referred to as **filtrate**.
S|When cold ethanol is added to the filtrate, DNA **precipitates** at the water/ethanol **interface**.
S|Although an individual DNA molecule is not visible with the naked eye, DNA isolated from large quantities of cells can be observed.

S|### The Structure of DNA In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick discerned the **double helix structure** of DNA.
S|The double helix consists of two DNA strands wound or twisted around each other in a sort of arrangement that looks like a spiraling staircase.
S|Each strand is made up of a long chain of monomer nucleotides (nucleotides are the building blocks of nucleic acids, DNA and RNA).
S|[Image 1: DNA strand] The nucleotide of DNA is made up of a deoxyribose sugar molecule, or a five-carbon sugar molecule, and attached to this sugar molecule is a phosphate group along with one of four nitrogenous bases.
S|The bases consist of two purines (adenine and guanine) and two pyrimidines (cytosine and thymine).
S|Nucleotides are bound together by covalent bonds between the phosphate of one nucleotide and the sugar of the next nucleotide.
S|This bond forms a phosphate-sugar foundation from where the nitrogenous bases project.
S|The strands are held together by a hydrogen bond between the bases.
S|Sequencing of this bonding is very particular: The purine adenine bonds only with the pyrimidine thymine, and the pyrimidine cytosine only bonds with the purine guanine.
S|Because of this highly stable configuration of the DNA molecule, it allows the DNA molecule to act as a template for the replication of a new DNA molecule, but also for the transcription of the related RNA molecule.
S|A segment of DNA that codes for the cell’s synthesis of a particular protein is called a.
S|## DNA Replication So the way the DNA replicates is by separating back into two single strands.
S|[Image 2: Unit Circle] These two, now separated, strands are what act as the template for that replication to take place.
S|The new strands are copied by the same principle of hydrogen-bond pairing between bases that make up the double helix.
S|Once the two strands break apart, and replicate, by bonding to another strand, each new DNA molecule contains one strand of the original.
S|This is often referred to as semiconservative replication, and it is key to the stable inheritance of genetic traits.
S|Within a cell, DNA ordered into dense protein-DNA complexes are called chromosomes.
S|Inside of eukaryotes, you can locate the chromosomes inside the nucleus.
S|However, DNA can also be found in mitochondria and chloroplasts.
S|Nearly every cell in a person’s body has the same DNA.
S|In prokaryotes, all the DNA is a single round-shaped chromosome in the cytoplasm.
S|There are a few prokaryotes, bacteria for instance, and a few eukaryotes that have extrachromosomal DNA called plasmids.
S|Plasmids are self-replicating genetic material.
