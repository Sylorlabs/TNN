S|## Egypt’s incredible nature [Image 4: Facts about Ancient Egypt] Egypt is home to a wide variety of animals and plants, including cheetahs, hyenas, crocodiles and cobras.
S|The best places to see Egypt’s wildlife are in its 21 protected regions, which include oases, deserts, mountains, coastal areas, river islands and wetlands.
S|Egyptians have always been close to the natural world.
S|The ancient Egyptians left paintings and carvings (which can still be seen today!) of large animals like elephants, hippos, leopards and cheetahs.
S|These animals were once common in Egypt, but they are now rare or extinct because of hunting and habitat loss.
S|## The history of Egypt [Image 5: Facts about Ancient Egypt] The first people to live on the banks of the Nile were hunters and fishermen, who settled there over 8,000 years ago.
S|They learned to grow crops and raise animals, and they began to build villages and towns.
S|They traded with their neighbours and learned to sail boats.
S|By 3000 B.C., a civilisation was established.
S|Around 3100 B.C., the kingdoms of Upper and Lower Egypt were unified under a powerful king, later called a pharaoh.
S|These kings built huge pyramids, temples and other impressive monuments.
S|They also conquered other lands.
S|By 1000 B.C., Egypt had split into smaller parts and the kingdom was in decline.
S|Strong neighbours attacked and took over Egyptian territory – in 31 B.C., Egypt fell under Roman control and centuries later, in A.D.
S|640, Muslim warriors took over Egypt.
S|These Arab forces ruled Egypt for several centuries and founded the modern capital, Cairo.
S|In 1882, the British invaded and occupied Egypt.
S|The British wanted control of the **Suez Canal**, which linked the Mediterranean Sea with the Red Sea and greatly shortened the sailing trip from Asia to Europe.
S|The British stayed until Egypt declared independence in 1952.
S|## Egyptian people and culture [Image 6: Facts about Ancient Egypt] About 90 percent of Egyptians are Muslim, which means they are followers of the **Islam religion**.
S|About ten percent of Egyptians are **Copts**, one of the oldest branches of the Christian religion.
S|Egypt’s population is growing rapidly.
S|This puts strain on Egypt’s resources, since most people live in a narrow strip of land along the Nile River.
S|Having so many people in such a small area causes overcrowding everywhere, from schools and hospitals to apartment buildings and public transport.
S|Children are highly valued in Egypt, especially in rural areas where they help on family farms.
S|Children are also expected to look after their parents in their old age.
S|## Egyptian government and economy [Image 7: Facts about Ancient Egypt] Egypt’s geography, population, history and military strength have made it highly influential in the region.
S|Egypt is a democratic republic, although some critics claim that it is not truly democratic.
S|Until 2005, there was never more than one presidential candidate to vote for.
S|Egypt’s most valuable resources are oil and gas, which are exported to other countries.
S|Other exports include metals, textiles, livestock and chemical products.
S|Tourism is also an important part of Egypt’s economy, with visitors flocking to see the famous ancient monuments and to enjoy the country’s beautiful beaches.

S|## ancient Egypt About 5,000 years ago, the civilization of ancient Egypt began in the Nile River valley of northeastern Africa.
S|Ancient Egypt was one of the world’s first civilizations.
S|It is also one of the most famous civilizations in history.
S|The ancient Egyptians built huge pyramids, temples, palaces, and tombs.
S|Their paintings and carvings are among the most splendid ever created.
S|They also produced art with simple scenes of everyday life, work, and play that shows many details of their lives.
S|Egypt’s dry climate has helped to preserve many of the things that the ancient Egyptians made.
S|Archaeologists first started to uncover the remains of ancient Egypt in the late 1700s.
S|Through those remains historians have been able to learn a great deal about the world of the ancient Egyptians.
S|## Life in Ancient Egypt The remains from ancient Egypt, including clothing, leather, wood, and food, provide an excellent idea of how the ancient Egyptians lived.
S|Much information has also come from tombs, including the paintings of scenes of everyday life on the walls of the tombs.
S|Because the Egyptians thought that life continued after death, everything they might need in the next world was placed in their tombs.
S|One queen, Herneith, was buried with her dog.
S|In a nobleman’s tomb at Saqqarah researchers found a whole meal, consisting of barley porridge, quail, kidneys, pigeon stew, fish, bread, and small circular cakes.
S|In the early tombs there were models of anything the dead might require, such as houses, granaries, boats, cattle, and servants making bread and beer.
S|Later these scenes were painted on the tomb walls to serve the same purpose.
S|The tombs of the wealthy contained jewelery and other precious objects.
S|The Egyptians preserved the dead as mummies by removing the internal organs and preserving the body with salt or other chemicals.
S|In addition to those of people, thousands of mummies of sacred animals such as cats, ibises, and crocodiles have been found.
S|Mummies of important people were placed in elaborate coffins called sarcophagi, often bearing a portrait of the deceased.
S|The coffin of Tutankhamun was made of solid gold.
S|## Medicine In order to preserve the dead, the Egyptians needed to know a great deal about the human body.
S|Their study of the body produced skillful doctors.
S|Some of the earliest medical and surgical records have been found in Egypt.
S|One of the earliest is the Ebers Papyrus, written about 1550 bce, which lists 700 cures for common sicknesses and problems.
S|## Writing The creation of a written language was one of the Egyptians’ finest achievements.
S|The first Egyptian writing was in a picture form called hieroglyphics.
S|The pictures, which stood for sounds of words, were cut or painted on the walls of tombs and temples, and were delicately colored in red, yellow, green, black, and blue.
S|Hieroglyphics were in use by 3100 bce.
S|They remained in use even after a form of cursive writing developed in about 2900 bce.
S|It was written in ink with a pen made of reeds.
S|This script was mainly written on papyrus, a form of paper made from papyrus plants.
S|Another script began to appear in about 700 bce.
S|The new form, called demotic, was used for ordinary writing.
S|The earlier form, called hieratic, was then used mostly for sacred writings.
S|The key to understanding Egyptian writing was the Rosetta Stone, a stone tablet dating from about 200 bce that was found in Egypt in 1799.
S|The stone shows words in Greek with translations in hieroglyphics and demotic script.
S|By comparing the Egyptian text with the Greek text, a scholar who knew Greek was able to uncover the meaning of the hieroglyphics and the demotic words.
S|## Everyday Life Villages and towns were situated near the Nile River because it was the chief highway as well as the only source of water.
S|The houses were made of mud brick.
S|Windows were small, high openings covered with loosely woven matting to keep out the heat and glare of the sun.
S|The walls were decorated with bright frescoes.
S|Straw matting and rugs covered the floors.
S|Wall paintings are an especially rich source of information about how the common people lived with their children, hunted and farmed, raised animals, and performed daily tasks.
S|The clothing, jewelery, and hair styles of all classes of people can be clearly seen.
S|Egyptians were fond of perfumes and cosmetics.
S|Both men and women wore a kind of green eyeshadow and outlined their eyes with a black substance called kohl.
S|Women also applied color to their lips and nails.
S|Vessels to hold cosmetics and the tools needed to grind them were included in tombs for use in the afterlife.
S|Clothing was simple because of the hot climate of Egypt.
S|Small children, servants, and laborers often wore little clothing.
S|White linen was the most commonly used fabric, with wool for cloaks when warmth was needed.
S|In early garments the cloth was simply draped and tied around the body or held in place with pins and belts.
S|Men typically wore a skirt of linen and sometimes a vest, while women wore a straight, simple ankle-length dress with shoulder straps.
S|Necklaces and wide collars decorated with gems were often worn.
S|Clothing became more elaborate over time.
S|Many people wore wigs.
S|Men often wore beards, and a false beard made of metal was worn by the ruler—even a female ruler.
S|## Religion The ancient Egyptians worshiped many gods.
S|The main god of the sun was Re (or Ra).
S|Each city had its own special gods.
S|When the city of Thebes grew in importance, its special god Amun did also.
S|Eventually, the Egyptians combined Amun with Re.
S|Amun-Re was considered the king of the gods.
S|Because the ancient Egyptians believed in life after death, Osiris, one of the gods of the dead, was also important.
S|His wife Isis and his son Horus were worshiped alongside Osiris.
S|Some Figures of Egyptian Mythology name description Amun one of the chief gods Bastet goddess of music and pregnant women; cat headed Hathor goddess of women and love; cow-headed Horus god whose eyes were the Sun and the Moon; appeared as a falcon Isis important goddess; wife of Osiris and mother of Horus Mont god of war; falcon-headed Nut goddess of the sky Osiris important god of the underworld Re chief Sun god Seth god of disorder; had some features of a dog Thoth god of the Moon, wisdom, and writing; ibis-headed ## Inventions The ancient Egyptians were a very practical people.
S|They learned the principles of engineering that enabled them to build vast structures such as the pyramids.
S|They did not have pulleys to aid in lifting heavy weights, but they were able to move large stone blocks by means of sledges, rollers, and ramps.
S|The Egyptians observed the stars and worked out calendars based on the phases of the Moon and on the solar year (see astronomy).
S|They also developed a complicated system of irrigation, which was based on the flooding times of the Nile.
S|## History ## Earliest Egypt People have lived along the banks of the Nile River for at least 10,000 years.
S|The narrow valley of the Nile was once swampy and overgrown with trees, rushes, and papyrus (a tall, reedlike plant).
S|Over the centuries, the landscape changed as the river brought down fertile silt to form a broad delta at the river’s mouth.
S|The people gradually cleared the swamps and built villages.
S|They learned to plant and grow crops.
S|Eventually, they organized two kingdoms.
S|Upper Egypt was in the narrow river valley in the south.
S|Lower Egypt was in the north, in the broad delta (triangular piece of land) where the river flows into the sea.
S|In about 2900 bce a king named Menes united the two parts of Egypt.
S|Menes built the capital city of Memphis.
S|The site of Memphis is near Cairo, the capital of modern Egypt.
S|## Old, Middle, and New Kingdoms After Menes, many kings, called pharaohs, ruled ancient Egypt.
S|During the long history there were more than 30 dynasties, or ruling families, of pharaohs.
S|Historians have grouped several of these dynasties into three important periods: the Old Kingdom, the Middle Kingdom, and the New Kingdom.
S|The Old Kingdom lasted from 2575 to 2130 bce.
S|It was during this period that the Egyptians built their great pyramids.
S|The best-known remains in Egypt are the three pyramids of Giza, built by Khufu, Khafra, and Menkaure beginning in about 2550 bce, during the 4th dynasty.
S|It is still not completely understood how the Egyptians were able to build such huge stone structures with the simple means of their time.
S|Near the second pyramid, partly cut in the rock and partly built of masonry, is the Great Sphinx.
S|The Sphinx has the facial features of a man but the body of a lion; it is approximately 240 feet (73 meters) long and 66 feet (20 meters) high.
S|The pyramids were part of an architectural complex, or collection of buildings that included temples and other monuments.
S|After 2130 bce Egypt suffered civil wars.
S|The country was split up until about 1938 bce.
S|Then the rulers of Thebes, a city far up the Nile in Upper Egypt, defeated their enemies and reunited Egypt.
S|This began the Middle Kingdom, which lasted until 1630 bce.
S|During the Middle Kingdom, Egyptian rule was extended into the neighboring country of Nubia (modern Sudan) and north to the Euphrates River in Syria.
S|The capital was moved south to Thebes, far up the Nile in Upper Egypt.
S|This was a period of great art and architecture, but few buildings from this period survive today.
S|A second period of weakness followed the Middle Kingdom.
S|An Asian people known as the Hyksos invaded Egypt.
S|The Egyptians finally forced out the Hyksos by 1539 bce.
S|This began the New Kingdom, which lasted until 1075 bce.
S|A mighty pharaoh of the New Kingdom was Thutmose III, who ruled from 1479 to 1426 bce.
S|Thutmose’s reign was the time of ancient Egypt’s greatest power and wealth.
S|He also ordered the building of many tombs and temples.
S|These include the great tombs hollowed out in the rock of the Valley of the Kings, west of Thebes.
S|During the 19th dynasty strong kings protected Egypt.
S|Ramses II, who ruled from 1279 to 1213 bce, carried out a vast building program.
S|The kingdom fell apart because the kings who followed Ramses had to use the army to defend Egypt against invaders.
S|The country split in two parts, riots and strikes were common, and disorder spread throughout the land.
S|## Alexander and the Ptolemies Peoples from Asia, first Assyrians and then Persians, later took over Egypt.
S|Then in 332 bce Alexander the Great of Macedonia (a country near Greece) conquered Egypt.
S|He founded the city of Alexandria at the mouth of the Nile.
S|After Alexander’s death, Ptolemy, one of his generals, seized the throne.
S|In 323 bce Ptolemy founded a new dynasty of Egyptian kings, called the Ptolemies.
S|The Ptolemies were Macedonians who spoke Greek, and many Egyptians disliked having foreign rulers.
S|Nevertheless, under the Ptolemies Egypt grew rich and powerful.
S|They built the Pharos, a huge lighthouse, at Alexandria.
S|Like the great pyramids, the Pharos was one of the seven wonders of the ancient world.
S|The Ptolemies also built a famous library at Alexandria.
S|Scholars came from all over the Greek-speaking world to study there.
S|## Roman and Muslim Conquests The last of the Ptolemies was Queen Cleopatra.
S|Roman forces defeated her navy at the Battle of Actium in 31 bce.
S|In 30 bce Egypt lost its independence to Rome.
S|After the Roman Empire split in half in the 300s ce, Egypt became a part of the eastern half, called the Byzantine Empire.
S|Finally, in the 600s ce Muslim Arabs took over Egypt.
S|Egypt is still a Muslim country today.
