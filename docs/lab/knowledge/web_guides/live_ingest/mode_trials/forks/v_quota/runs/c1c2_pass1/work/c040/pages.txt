S|Common Student Mistakes (Trap Alerts) - View more Phases of the Moon Explained for Kids The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite.
S|It does not produce its own light.
S|Instead, it reflects sunlight.
S|As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different portions of its sunlit side.
S|This creates the different **phases of the Moon**.
S|Understanding Moon phases helps us know why the Moon looks different on different nights.
S|Why Does the Moon Change Its Shape?
S|The Moon does not actually change its shape.
S|It always remains a sphere (ball-shaped).
S|What changes is how much of the Moon's sunlit side we can see from Earth.
S|- **The Moon orbits Earth:** It takes about **29.5 days** (roughly one month) to complete one orbit around Earth.
S|- **The Sun lights up half the Moon:** Just like Earth, half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun.
S|- **Our view changes:** As the Moon moves around Earth, the lit portion that we see from Earth changes daily.
S|- **Moon phases:** These different shapes we see are called the **phases of the Moon** or **lunar phases**.
S|The Eight Main Phases of the Moon The Moon goes through **eight major phases** during its monthly cycle.
S|Each phase has a specific name and appearance.
S|### 2.1 New Moon - The Moon is between the Earth and the Sun.
S|- The sunlit side faces away from Earth.
S|- We **cannot see the Moon** in the sky during this phase.
S|- The Moon rises and sets with the Sun.
S|- This is the **starting phase** of the lunar cycle.
S|### 2.2 Waxing Crescent - **Waxing** means the lit portion is growing or increasing.
S|- A small, curved sliver of light appears on the **right side** of the Moon.
S|- This crescent shape is thin and looks like the letter "C" reversed.
S|- Visible in the evening sky after sunset.
S|### 2.3 First Quarter - Exactly **half of the Moon** appears lit from Earth.
S|- The **right half** is illuminated (in the Northern Hemisphere).
S|- Called "First Quarter" because the Moon has completed **one-quarter** of its orbit around Earth.
S|- The Moon is at a 90-degree angle from the Sun as seen from Earth.
S|- Visible from afternoon until midnight.
S|### 2.4 Waxing Gibbous - **Gibbous** means more than half but not fully lit.
S|- More than half of the Moon is visible and the lit portion is still **growing**.
S|- The Moon appears almost full but is not completely round.
S|- Visible most of the night.
S|### 2.5 Full Moon - The **entire face** of the Moon visible from Earth is lit.
S|- Earth is between the Sun and the Moon.
S|- The Moon looks like a **complete, bright circle**.
S|- Rises at sunset and sets at sunrise.
S|- The **brightest phase** of the Moon.
S|- Occurs approximately **15 days** after the New Moon.
S|### 2.6 Waning Gibbous - **Waning** means the lit portion is shrinking or decreasing.
S|- More than half of the Moon is visible, but the lit area is **getting smaller**.
S|- The left side starts to darken gradually.
S|- Visible most of the night and early morning.
S|### 2.7 Last Quarter (Third Quarter) - Exactly **half of the Moon** appears lit again.
S|- This time, the **left half** is illuminated (in the Northern Hemisphere).
S|- Called "Last Quarter" because the Moon has completed **three-quarters** of its orbit.
S|- Visible from midnight until morning.
S|### 2.8 Waning Crescent - A small, curved sliver of light appears on the **left side** of the Moon.
S|- The lit portion continues to **decrease**.
S|- This crescent shape looks like the letter "C".
S|- Visible in the early morning before sunrise.
S|- After this phase, the cycle returns to **New Moon**.
S|Understanding Waxing and Waning These two terms help describe whether the Moon is growing brighter or darker from our view on Earth.
S|- **Waxing:** The illuminated portion of the Moon is **increasing**.
S|Occurs from New Moon to Full Moon.
S|We see more of the lit side each night.
S|- **Waning:** The illuminated portion of the Moon is **decreasing**.
S|Occurs from Full Moon to New Moon.
S|We see less of the lit side each night.
S|### 3.1 Easy Way to Remember - **Right side lit = Waxing (Growing):** If the right side of the Moon is lit, the Moon is waxing.
S|- **Left side lit = Waning (Shrinking):** If the left side of the Moon is lit, the Moon is waning.
S|- Think of the letter **"D"** for the shape when waxing (right side curved).
S|- Think of the letter **"C"** for the shape when waning (left side curved).
S|- **Lunar Cycle:** The complete period of all Moon phases from one New Moon to the next.
S|Takes approximately **29.5 days**.
S|- **Crescent:** When less than half of the Moon is visible.
S|Appears as a thin, curved shape.
S|- **Quarter:** When exactly half of the Moon is visible from Earth.
S|- **Gibbous:** When more than half but not all of the Moon is visible.
S|Appears oval or egg-shaped.
S|- **Orbit:** The path the Moon follows as it moves around Earth.
S|- **Satellite:** An object that revolves around a planet.
S|The Moon is Earth's natural satellite.
S|Common Student Mistakes (Trap Alerts) - **Mistake 1:** Thinking the Moon produces its own light.
S|*Correction:* The Moon only **reflects sunlight**.
S|It does not generate light.
S|- **Mistake 2:** Believing the Moon changes its actual shape.
S|*Correction:* The Moon is always spherical.
S|Only our **view of the sunlit portion** changes.
S|- **Mistake 3:** Confusing "Quarter" with appearance.
S|*Correction:* First Quarter and Last Quarter are named for the Moon's **position in its orbit** (1/4 and 3/4), not because we see one-quarter of the Moon.
S|We see **half** the Moon during Quarter phases.
S|- **Mistake 4:** Thinking Moon phases are caused by Earth's shadow.
S|*Correction:* Moon phases are caused by the **changing angle** between the Sun, Moon, and Earth.
S|Earth's shadow causes **lunar eclipses**, not phases.
S|Why Do We Always See the Same Side of the Moon?
S|- The Moon takes the same time to **rotate once on its axis** as it takes to **orbit Earth once**.
S|- Both take approximately **27.3 days**.
S|- This is called **synchronous rotation** or **tidal locking**.
S|- As a result, we always see the **same face** of the Moon from Earth.
S|- The side we cannot see is called the **"far side"** of the Moon (not "dark side" because it receives sunlight too).

S|As the Moon travels around Earth, our viewing angle shifts.
S|Because of that shift, we see different portions of the bright half over time.
S|That’s all that’s happening.
S|The phases are not caused by Earth’s shadow, except in the rare case of a lunar eclipse.
S|The Moon follows a repeating cycle of phases.
S|It begins with the new Moon, when the lit side faces away from us.
S|Then comes the waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, and full Moon.
S|After that, the pattern reverses through the waning gibbous, last quarter, and waning crescent.
S|The word “waxing” simply means the visible light is increasing.
S|“Waning” means it’s decreasing.

S|# Understanding Moon Phases for 5-Year-Olds: A Fun Guide / Learning Corner ## What Are Moon Phases?
S|Moon phases are how we see the moon change shape in the sky over time.
S|Sometimes it looks like a full circle, and other times, it looks like a slice of a pie!
S|There are four main shapes we’ll talk about: **New Moon**, **First Quarter**, **Full Moon**, and **Last Quarter**.
S|New Moon When the moon is a **new moon**, it’s really hard to see because it’s all dark!
S|It’s like turning off a light.
S|It’s the start of a new month for the moon!
S|First Quarter After a few days, the moon starts to grow and becomes a **first quarter moon**.
S|This is when we can see half of the moon shining brightly in the sky.
S|Imagine cutting a cookie in half and eating one part!
S|Full Moon The full moon is when we can see the whole moon shining brightly in the sky.
S|It’s like the moon is showing off all of its light!
S|It happens about two weeks after the new moon.
S|Last Quarter Finally, the moon changes again and becomes a **last quarter moon**.
S|Now we can see the other half of the moon!
S|It’s like eating the last piece of a chocolate bar, leaving half for later!
S|## Why Do Moon Phases Happen?
S|The moon goes around the Earth, and as it moves, the sunlight shines on different parts of it.
S|That’s why we see the moon change shape!
S|It takes about 29 days for the moon to go through all these phases.
S|Let’s play a game!
S|Look at the moon each night and see if you can recognize which phase it’s in.
S|Draw a picture of the moon you see and its different shapes!
