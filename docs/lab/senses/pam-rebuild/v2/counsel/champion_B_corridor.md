# Corridor Champion: Historical Audit / Refusal (Corner B)

Champion: subagent b6580999. Corner: F1 (TCAT), G4 (context-diverse quarantine), G6 (joint-error bins as refusal only), F5 (counter-corroboration trap).

## 1. The case for the corner

**The deployment blocker is a corridor anomaly, not a candidate anomaly.** The six timbredisc wrongs (seq 10983–11192, judg=RICH truth=BRIGHT, conf 701–718, mrgF 353–382, six agreeing) are observationally truth-like at the single-trial level — that is precisely what "front-end sustained, corroborated error" means. Trial 1145 proves no pointwise comparison of a candidate against an incumbent can ever be safe (WRONG, conf 874, mrgF 10410, strong=1, agree=1, dominated the correct incumbent on every logged axis). Any mechanism whose predicate is evaluated *on the candidate* is therefore living inside the one region of the problem already proven uninhabitable.

Corridor auditing moves the predicate off the candidate entirely. F1 asks about the last 128 decisions, not this percept: are K+1 consecutive installs percept-similar, and is the corridor's false-positive rate above base? G4 asks about the label's history: have M diverse contexts produced it, or is this one spoof on repeat? G6 asks about the bin's history: has corroboration here gone jointly wrong before? F5 asks about the bank: have we installed-then-reversed this before? None of these predicates compare the candidate's evidence to anything. They refuse on history.

**Ceiling compliance is structural, not argued.** (1) Zero-bit judgment-side: none of the four run a predicate over a judgment plus a deterministic transform of that judgment. TCAT hash-chains decisions; quarantine counts context-diverse hits; bins log raw-byte hashes; the exemplar bank matches percepts to percepts. The second looks happen on raw bytes under deliberate re-sense, exactly as the grok G7 scope challenge permits. (2) Trial-1145 pointwise impossibility: corridor refusal never lets a trial install by dominating axes — and symmetrically, no corridor mechanism *installs* by any trial's score at all. The worst they do is HOLD or BLOCK, and the block-to-install path runs through three deliberate re-inspections (F5) or varied re-senses (F1). (3) The 74.8% gate ceiling is arithmetic about the current PASS predicate; the corner does not move the gate, it adds refusal gates above it — orthogonal by construction. It concedes recall: it aims at the 20.9% false-accept rate, not the 278 un-reached corrects. (4) Everything is deterministic functions of committed state — hash chains, bin counters, exemplar banks — zero RNG, byte-identical reruns.

**Program law fit.** PAMs are organs of one unified brain that must be conscious about rechecking and revision. A brain's memory of its own mistakes, consulted before every install, *is* the deliberate-revision organ. The alternative corners either re-sense the same corrupted view (G2/G1 without history) or gamble on new machinery (G5). This corner weaponizes what the brain already knows.

**The corner is a stack, not a menu.** Read together, the four hypotheses repair each other's truth-signal gap: F1's "running FPR" and G6's "joint-wrong rate" both need post-install ground truth, which no oracle supplies. F5's negative exemplar bank — percepts installed then deliberately reversed — *is* that ground truth. Composition: the exemplar bank feeds F1's corridor FPR and G6's bin rates; G4 raises the cost of minting the repeated hits F1 watches for; F1's clique detection is the tripwire that discovers new exemplars for F5. Grok ranked G6 a mere "safety interlock" and G4 a cheap battery; fable ranked F5 first. The stack reading says they are four layers of one defense: cost-raising (G4) → interlock (G6) → tripwire (F1) → learned block (F5).

## 2. Steelman: the best attack on this corner

**Novelty starves history.** 53% on 12 sealed novel families. A quiet bin is not evidence — grok concedes this for G6, and it is worse for F1: the attacker's *first* corridor has no FPR. Honest answer: the corner never draws positive conclusions from history. G6's rule is explicit — clean bins mean "not refused," never "safe." F5 and F1 only fire on observed reversals and observed cliques. The corner cannot prevent the first sustained wrong, and it concedes it. It guarantees the *second*, *third*, and *n-th* identical wrongs cost more and get caught faster. Judge it on that: it is a refusal mechanism, not a recall mechanism. It does not touch the 278 correct high-conf that never reach PASS, and it should not claim to.

**The M-diverse-context attacker.** Grok admits G4 "dies cleanly if an attacker can buy M diverse contexts." Correct — and F1 is not immune either, if the attacker produces perceptually-dissimilar routes to the same wrong label. But note the attacker's bind: to keep the spoof effective across contexts, the wrongness usually rides on shared signal, which pushes percept-similarity back above F1's 80% threshold. The honest kill bar stands: if the battery (100 labels × M contexts at ≥D separation, each independently producing wrong PASS at conf≥701) installs above 5%, G4 is dead and F1 must face the same battery.

**Over-blocking and delay costs.** TCAT HOLD gates a whole family to three varied re-senses — expensive if the family is productive. F5 delays corrects that fall near exemplars. The asymmetry defense is real but not free: blocking is recoverable, installing wrong is not, but a brain that refuses everything learns nothing. The cost must be measured, not asserted — that is what the kill bars are for (F5's 25%-delayed->50-trials bar; F1's >5% false-hold rate on uncorrelated-wrong trials).

**Staleness.** Rates computed over all time fossilize. A bin that earned >2% joint-wrong two regimes ago can be clean now, and an attacker can rotate through fresh bins. The repair is mandatory, not optional: all historical rates must be rolling-window (e.g. last 128 decisions, last N trials per bin), and deliberate re-sense is the re-open key — a bin or exemplar that survives triple varied re-inspection is *removed* (F5 already specifies this; G6 needs the same clause). Without decay, the corner degrades into a grudge list.

**The FPR oracle problem (my own catch).** F1 as written needs "running FPR" of installed sub-sequences — false positives require knowing truth post-install. As a standalone mechanism this is a real gap. The stack reading closes it: compute corridor FPR via the reversal bank (installed-then-reversed percepts), not via an oracle. F1 should be amended to define FPR this way before any build.

## 3. Ranking by expected value

1. **F5 Counter-Corroboration Trap.** Weaponizes already-discovered errors into active gates; blocking is the safe-direction default (delay recoverable, wrong-install corruption is not); lowest implementation complexity (distance lookup + block + triple-confirm); graceful failure mode — its kill condition (over-blocking) is a delay cost, not a corruption event. It is also the truth-signal source the other three need.
2. **F1 TCAT (amended).** Detects the exact signature of the deployment blocker — sustained correlated installs at above-base FPR. Purely additive to existing gates; never compares a trial to an incumbent. Demoted from #1 only because it needs the F5 bank to define its FPR, and because its first-corridor blindness is real. With the reversal-bank amendment, it is the tripwire of the stack.
3. **G6 Historical joint-error bins (refusal only).** Cheap pre-reversal interlock: disables corroboration where joint wrongs were observed, pending deliberate re-sense. Ranks below F1 because raw-byte bin granularity is a free parameter an attacker can dodge (too coarse poisons clean families, too fine is trivially evaded), and because "quiet bin" novelty is unhandled. Keep as the layer that covers not-yet-reversed errors.
4. **G4 Context-diverse quarantine.** Raises the attacker's cost to M diverse contexts plus a canonical-window re-sense, and correctly nullifies the six-near-duplicate failure mode (six identical PASSes count as one). Ranks last because it dies cleanly to a resourced attacker — cost-raising is not a barrier — and because requiring M diverse contexts delays legitimate recurring-correct installs on the RK-3 stream.

## 4. Cheapest decisive first experiment: F5 backtest on the logged 43 accepts

No new binary. No new runs. The evidence needed already exists in the committed logs: the 43 ACCEPT_INSTALL trials from the V2-D red team (9 false, 34 true), and the six timbredisc wrongs as the seeded exemplar bank (labeled "installed then reversed").

**Battery.** For each of the 43 logged trials, compute the F5 block predicate: candidate within defined distance of any exemplar in the bank (same family, confidence within 150, margin within 2000 — fable's stated thresholds, frozen before the run). Deterministic function of logged values; byte-identical reproducible.

**Kill bars (both must pass):**
- **Block recall:** ≥8 of the 9 false accepts must be blocked pre-confirmation. (The six timbredisc wrongs are in the bank by construction; the test is whether the other three falses fall within exemplar distance — if the bank only catches its own members, F5 is a replay list, not a trap.)
- **Over-block cost:** ≤4 of the 34 true accepts may fall within exemplar distance (would-be-delayed). Exceeding this means the distance rule over-generalizes and the delay cost is already eating correct installs.

**Why decisive.** If F5 fails block recall, the exemplar-distance rule cannot generalize from six known wrongs to three siblings — the trap concept is dead at its cheapest possible instantiation, and there is no reason to build the live gate. If it fails over-block, the safe-direction claim (delay is cheap) is false at realistic density and F5 must be redesigned, not tuned. If it passes both, F5 has earned the live-gate build, and the bank it builds becomes the truth signal for the F1 amendment and G6's bin rates. Total cost: one offline script against committed logs.
