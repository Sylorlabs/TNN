# Champion C: Attack the Ceiling at the Sense

## The ceiling is arithmetic, not opinion

The 74.8% figure is a count, not a theory: 824 of 1,102 correct judgments ever reach the PASS predicate; 278 correct, high-confidence percepts never do. No gate rule — no adjudicator, no corroboration rule, no margin re-weighting, no veto battery — can install a percept that does not PASS at the sense level, because every gate in the evidence base operates downstream of the sense's judgment and its confidence. V2-A's RK-3 (65.88%) died at the adjudicator; V2-D's confidence-separation (88.48%) is alive but its independent-evidence acceptance was killed precisely because it tried to install without sense verification (43 ACCEPT_INSTALL on 288 UNRESOLVED, 9 false, 20.9% vs a 0.6% calibration claim — REDTEAM_V2 §7).

The arithmetic is tighter than it looks. An 85% bar on 1,102 trials demands at least 937 correct installs. The sense supplies 824. The gate must therefore be *fed* at least 113 more by the sense — and the only reservoir is the 278. Gate-side innovation has exactly zero room left: it is capped at 824 + whatever the sense hands it. Every counsel position that lives entirely on the gate side — corridor audits (G6, F1), quarantine rules (G4), counter-corroboration (F5) — is defending or rearranging installs inside the ceiling. They can reduce false installs, and that is worth doing, but none of them can move 74.8% to 85%. That gap is owned by the sense, full stop.

This is also why the "heavier sense" objection has the arrow of proof reversed. The objection says: a second sense may just mint more confident wrongs, like the six corroborated-wrong RICH trials (seq 10983–11192, judg=RICH, truth=BRIGHT, conf 701–718, mrgF 353–382). But those six were mint errors of the *current* sense at the *current* bar — and they already installed as PASS. The six are evidence that the current sense is the wrong tool for sustained verification, not that a second sense is futile. G5 is deliberately scoped to the 278: it fires *only* when the current sense is high-confidence but not PASS, emits PASS only under the existing numerical bar, and never raises an already-PASS item. It cannot touch the six-wrong failure mode, and it does not claim to. The six are the deployment blocker; they belong to the temporal side of the corner (F3). One failure mode per hypothesis — that is the discipline that kept the ceilings honest.

## Why sustained errors need re-verification, not stronger gates

The deployment blocker is observational: a front-end sustained, corroborated error is observationally truth-like. The six agreeing wrongs are the proof — six independent-looking PASSes, all wrong, all confident. No gate rule can adjudicate them away at decision time, because at decision time they look exactly like truth. Trial 1145 proved the stronger form: a WRONG at conf 874, mrgF 10410, dominating a correct incumbent on *every logged axis*. A gate is a function of logged axes. It cannot beat an adversary that dominates all of them.

The only property a sustained error cannot fake indefinitely is *time*. A spoof that is sustained at one temporal window need not be sustained at three; a family that was stable need not stay stable. That is the entire argument for F3: per-family max install-age T, after which the install expires and must be re-earned through strict 3-crop re-verification (beginning 20%, middle 60%, end 20%). It is the program-law move — the unified brain does not let a percept be settled forever; it *consciously re-checks* what it installed. V2-D's failure is the cautionary tale for the opposite instinct: when the system could not get verification, it stopped requiring it (install on agreement without the sense's own PASS), and bought 9 false installs. F3 never lowers the verification requirement; it *re-applies* it. Stronger gates decide once with more machinery. Re-verification decides again with fresh evidence. Against an observationally-truth-like sustained error, only the second has a mechanism.

F4 is the same temporal instinct pointed at the other edge: the 278 that never PASS. The sealed-family result — 53% accuracy, only 44/288 reaching PASS, zero PASSes in eight of twelve families — says the sense does not recognize the novel. If many of the 278 are novel-family percepts (plausible: novel families rarely PASS, and the 278 are exactly the high-conf-not-PASS population), then no in-distribution gate tuning will ever catch them. F4's provisional install with an 80%-of-next-10 confirm bar and purge-plus-blacklist on failure is the honest shape of that claim: it admits the first install may be wrong and builds the correction into the mechanism rather than pretending the gate can see what the sense cannot. It attacks the ceiling from the cold-start side; G5 attacks it from the second-pass side.

## The scope license (G7) — why a second raw read is legal

The three proofs constrain judgment-side work, not raw-byte work, and the corner's whole legal basis is reading them at their true width. The zero-bit proof (KB4: judgment-side channels carry 0.0000 bits) covers predicates of a judgment and a deterministic transform of that judgment. A second raw read is neither. The trial-1145 dominance proof covers the axes actually logged (conf, mrgF, strong, agree); it does not say no future raw measurement can differ. The 74.8% ceiling covers the *current* PASS predicate. G5 and F4 both live in the space the proofs do not close: new raw evidence, new predicates over raw bytes. G7 is not a build; it is the fence that keeps the builders from being prosecuted under proofs that do not apply to them. Ranked below the builds, but it is load-bearing: without it, any gate-camp opponent can wave "zero-bit" at a second sense and sound rigorous while being out of scope.

## Program law, embodied

The PAMs program law is conscious perception: the brain chooses what to inspect, re-senses under uncertainty, verifies before install — never autopilot. The sense-side corner is that law applied to the ceiling. G5: on uncertainty (high-conf, not PASS), deliberately look again, with heavier machinery, and abstain rather than guess. F3: on age, deliberately re-earn what you installed; nothing is settled forever. F4: on novelty, admit the first judgment is a hypothesis, hold it provisional, and confirm or purge by subsequent evidence. All three refuse the V2-D temptation to install without verification, and all three refuse the V2-A temptation to adjudicate with more judgment-side cleverness. They push the verification requirement outward — in time (F3), in population (F4), in machinery (G5) — exactly where the proofs still leave room.

## Steelman: the best attack on this corner

The strongest evidence-grounded objection is the mint: **a heavier second sense may simply mint more confident wrongs, and the current sense's own wrongs already look like that.** The six timbredisc wrongs were PASS at conf 701–718 with the *first* sense; a heavier second sense given the same raw bytes and a mandate to "find PASS" has every incentive to learn the same spoof features. G5's kill battery only caps *new* false-PASS at ≤2 points above today's rate on the 12 sealed families — but the sealed families are 53%-accuracy territory where the sense rarely PASSes at all (44/288), so the cap is measured where false-PASSes are rare, not where the minting happens. An adversary (or just the training distribution) that teaches the second sense the spoof features would show up as *converted corrects* on the 278 while quietly adding wrongs the battery under-measures. The honest version of this attack: G5's kill bar is asymmetric — it counts conversions precisely and wrongs approximately.

Second: **53% novelty accuracy makes F4's provisional path a wrong-install pipeline.** At 53% correct on novel families, roughly half of provisional installs are wrong from the first trial. The 80%-of-next-10 confirm bar will purge many — but purge happens after 10 trials of a wrong installed percept being live in the knowledge base, and the family blacklist only fires after the purge. The evidence base has no measurement of how much damage a wrong provisional does during its 10-trial life, and the six-wrong cluster shows that wrongs *corroborate each other* — a wrong provisional could be "confirmed" by wrong subsequent trials from the same spoofed family, exactly the correlated-corroborator pattern F4 is supposed to route around. The confirm bar assumes subsequent trials are independent evidence; the deployment blocker is evidence that they are not.

Third: **F3's staleness re-verification risks destabilizing correct installs.** The autopsy record shows R2-4's safety rested on an already-RICH incumbent absorbing the six wrong challengers — stability of correct installs is load-bearing. Forcing every family through 3-crop re-verification on expiry re-opens every installed percept to a 3-crop disagreement that may be noise rather than spoof. F3's own kill bar concedes the point (post-TAM accuracy <85% = falsified), but 85% on 5,000 trials is a coarse net: a mechanism that purges 2% of correct installs and catches the six wrongs would still read as a pass while quietly degrading the installed base. The destabilization objection is real, and the answer is not "the bar catches it" but that the bar needs a per-install-churn ledger, not just a final accuracy.

The corner survives these attacks but does not dismiss them. Each attack becomes a tightening of its kill batteries: G5 needs the false-PASS cap measured on *spoof-rich* families, not just the sealed novel set; F4 needs the correlated-confirmation failure mode as an explicit kill battery (wrong provisional confirmed by wrong same-family trials); F3 needs churn tracking, not just terminal accuracy.

## Ranking by expected value

1. **G5 — new sense aimed at the 278.** The only hypothesis that *must* exist: the 85% bar is arithmetically unreachable without converting ≥113 of the 278, and no gate hypothesis can do it. It holds the gate fixed, so it cannot break V2-D's 88.48%; it abstains rather than guesses, so it cannot mint unmeasured wrongs if the kill battery is honest; and it is the only direct mechanism for the gap the arithmetic identifies. Everything else is defense. G5 is the offense, and offense is what the bar requires.
2. **F3 — confidence-decay staleness.** The only hypothesis that attacks the deployment blocker at its root rather than at decision time. Trial 1145 and the six-wrong cluster prove decision-time adjudication is the wrong layer; sustained error dies only when "installed" stops meaning "settled." Higher variance than G5 (destabilization risk), but it is the temporal half of the corner's thesis, and without it the six-wrong failure mode has no answer at all.
3. **F4 — novelty-first provisional path.** Attacks the ceiling from the cold-start side, where the 53% sealed-family result says the action is. Ranked below F3 because the 53% accuracy is a live, measured risk to the provisional channel itself — the confirm bar is only as good as the independence of subsequent trials, and the evidence base says correlated wrongs exist. High ceiling, high variance; run it as the second offensive prong, not the first.
4. **G7 — scope challenge.** Not a build, so it has no standalone expected value — but it is the license without which G5 and F4 get killed by misapplied proofs. It belongs in the ranking as the constraint that must be *stated* alongside any sense-side build, and as the kill-bar enforcer (re-run the mini Zag proof on any second look smuggled through the first judgment).

## Cheapest decisive first experiment: G5's conversion battery

**Battery.** Freeze RK-3, the current sense, and all gate numbers exactly as measured (no threshold retuning — that is the point). Extract the 278 correct high-confidence non-PASS trials from the frozen evidence stream. Build (or commission) one heavy second-pass sense candidate — wider window, different byte grouping, or prototype match against already-verified installs; the mechanism is the builder's choice, the bar is fixed. Run it *only* on the 278. It emits PASS under the existing numerical bar or abstains.

**Three-prong measurement, all from frozen artifacts:**
- *Conversions:* count of the 278 that become true PASS (verified against frozen truth labels).
- *Mint check:* run the second sense on the 12 sealed novel families (288 trials); measure its false-PASS rate vs today's rate.
- *Regression:* full RK-3 replay with G5 in the loop; RK-3 must not drop below 88.48%.

**Numerical kill bars (G5's own, tightened by the steelman):**
- **Kill if <100 of the 278 convert** (falsified as the ceiling attack; note the true 85%-bar requirement is ≥113, so 100 is the falsification floor, not the victory line — 100–112 is "real effect, insufficient," ≥113 is "the bar falls").
- **Kill if the second sense's false-PASS rate on the 12 sealed families exceeds today's rate by more than 2 points.**
- **Kill if RK-3 drops below 88.48%.**
- **Steel addition:** run the six timbredisc wrong trials (seq 10983–11192) through the second sense; if it re-emits PASS on ≥2 of them, it has learned the spoof features and the conversion count is suspect — kill regardless of the conversion number.

Why this is the cheapest decisive test: no new fixtures to seal, no adversary battery to construct, no gate to retune — every input is already frozen, every label is already known, and the single number that matters (conversions of the 278) is directly the number the arithmetic demands. One binary, three frozen datasets, four kill bars. If it fails, the corner's top hypothesis dies in one run; if it converts ≥113 with the mint check clean, the 85% bar is within reach for the first time in the program's history.
