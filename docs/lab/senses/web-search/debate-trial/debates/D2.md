**The Shearer survey (D2H01) is opinion data, not a test.** It asked 77 scientists whether they had *encountered evidence* of a secret program. A competently run secret program predicts 76-of-77 "no." This is not a null result; it is an absence-of-encounter result, exactly what both hypotheses predict. So the claim's *form* — a government deliberately spraying its own population's airspace in secret — is not "extraordinary" requiring extraordinary proof. It is documented history. The "thousands would have to know" objection (D2E012) fails the same way: compartmentalized military programs kept this secret for decades. And a large-scale program need not build "dedicated spray planes" — that is TNN's and Gaia's framing, not the claim's. Dispersal can ride ordinary aviation infrastructure; the logistics objection assumes the least covert implementation.

**Institutional intent is on the record.** In 1996 the U.S. armed forces commissioned "Owning the Weather in 2025," a strategy "to use weather modification not only as a force multiplier to accomplish military objectives in battlefield, but also to achieve capability of altering 'global weather patterns'" [NEW2]. **The scale datum (D2H03) begs the question.** 53 kg of SO₂ is the largest *documented* release — but "documented" does the work. The claim is that a large program is *undocumented*. You cannot bound an alleged covert program by the admitted record.

My bar: the claim stays open — historically precedented, institutionally intended, and empirically untested on either side by TNN's own criterion. Suspend judgment, not 0.934.
