# H3 ear brief — sol-H3: spatially frozen long-scene world beds

## What you are hearing

Two A/B/X trials. Each clip is a 60-second excerpt (minutes 1:00–2:00) from a
5-minute B-γ world scene (all captured field recordings, no synthesis).

- A and B are two different **world designs**: the *familiar* world (children
  playing, the B-γ kids grammar) and the *novel* world (dream/air textures, a
  different foreground grammar). Both have the same world-bed construction
  (ocean wash/wind bed + `bridge_world` instants).
- X is a repeat of one of them (A or B). The A/B/X assignment is in
  `KEY_H3.sealed.txt` — do not open it before judging.
- `h3_t1_A.wav` / `h3_t1_B.wav` / `h3_t1_X.wav`
- `h3_t2_A.wav` / `h3_t2_B.wav` / `h3_t2_X.wav`

## The questions (answer per trial)

1. **Which world feels ALIVE vs STAGED?** Alive: the world keeps happening —
   air moves, distant events surprise you, the bed feels like a place. Staged:
   it feels like a loop, a frozen backdrop, scenery that never changes.
2. **Is X A or B?** (sanity check that the two worlds are discriminable at all)

If neither feels alive/staged, or you cannot tell X apart, say so — "no
difference" is valid.

## Context (machine side, for after you judge)

The machine test (12 standard/control scene pairs, paired Wilcoxon + Holm)
found the world beds are measurably static: 60 s self-similarity ≈ 0.92–0.94,
and reshuffling the bed grains (control) changes nothing significant —
spectral novelty, 10 s novelty, repetition similarity, and event-conditioned
bed response all move n.s. in inconsistent directions. Bridge-midpoint floors
run 0.35–1.57× the B-γ kids median (kill required ≥3×). Caveat: the ocean
corpus holds only 19 wash + 21 wind grains, so the control bed reuses 95–100%
of the standard bed's source grains in a different order — the machine
comparison tests arrangement, not material. Your ears decide whether the
world feels alive or frozen; if both worlds feel equally staged, the frozen-
bed defect is confirmed by ear and a world-first recomposition follows.
