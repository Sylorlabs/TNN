# Fork B-α — MONSTER.md

**The monster objection:** Humans imagined movie monsters and crazy effects they'd never
experienced. That was imagination too. A study-then-invent position must answer
imagination-beyond-experience, or admit its boundary.

---

## What B-α claims

B-α claims that a learned vocabulary of real event grains, arranged by a deliberated
scene structure, can produce sounds that do not sound like synthesis — and that the
arrangement itself (which events, in which order, at which rates, with which overlaps)
is the locus of invention.

B-α does **not** claim to invent new physics. It claims that recombination of understood
events, guided by a scene's causal logic (a chase has pursuit, a trip has a thud then
laughter; a tide has sets and pauses), is sufficient to render scenes the system never
directly studied — *provided* the scene's elements have studied analogues.

## The collage question (answered honestly)

**Is event-level transformation just sophisticated collage?**

Partially, yes — and B-α does not hide this. Every sample in a B-α render was recorded
by someone else's microphone. What B-α contributes:

1. **Segmentation into events** (study) — deciding what counts as a unit.
2. **Description** (study) — the descriptor vocabulary.
3. **Grammar** (study) — bigrams and gap quantiles: how events follow each other.
4. **Scene deliberation** (invention) — the composed beat structure, which has no studied
   counterpart. No recording contains "Mara chases Jo, Jo trips at 16 s, all three collapse
   laughing." The *sequence* is invented; the *grains* are remembered.

The honest position: B-α is **collage with a deliberated score**. If "imagination"
requires inventing the grains themselves, B-α is not imagination — it's arrangement.
The fork's claim is that arrangement, when the grains are real and the score is
scene-motivated, escapes the synth smell. Micah's ears adjudicate.

## Imagination beyond experience: the alien test

Test 3 is the monster fork's question posed to B-α: render "an alien ocean's surf at dawn,
under a sky that hums" with **no direct study referent** — no ocean recordings, no waves,
no throat singing, no whales. Only Mars wind, dust devils, storms, and ice.

This is B-α stretching: using wind as surf (both are sustained airy swells with attack
and decay), wind slowed as sky-hum (both are continuous atmospheric tones), storm as
deep alien creatures (both are low, powerful, and strange). The mapping is analogical —
"what in my vocabulary behaves like what the scene needs?" — which is arguably the
beginning of imagination: using the known to reach the unknown.

**If it fails:** the claim renames to "study per scene" — B-α renders scenes it studied,
and the monster objection stands unanswered by this fork. That outcome is documented,
not hidden.

**What B-α cannot do (admitted):**
- Invent a sound-making mechanism it never observed (that's Fork C's territory).
- Render a scene whose elements have no analogue in its vocabulary (e.g., a truly
  unprecedented physics).
- Guarantee that analogical stretch reads as intended — only the ear oracle can confirm.

## The synth smell, revisited

The debate diagnosed regularity + stationarity + symmetry as the synth smell. B-α's
defense is structural, not parametric: real event grains carry the irregularity,
non-stationarity, and asymmetry of the physical world for free. No parameter tuning was
used to "add naturalness" — naturalness is inherited, not synthesized. If B-α ever sounds
like a synth, it will be because the *arrangement* imposed regularity (e.g., metronomic
timing, symmetric phrasing) — a failure of the score, not the grains. The grammar's gap
quantiles (p10/p50/p90, not fixed grids) are the structural defense against this.
