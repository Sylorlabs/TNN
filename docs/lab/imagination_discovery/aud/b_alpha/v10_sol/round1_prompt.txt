CONTEXT: A research system renders 30s mono 44.1kHz audio of little kids (ages ~3-6) playing in a playground - one consistent imagined scene. Current method is concatenative: gating pre-cut recorded atoms (laughs, shouts, footsteps) on/off over a bed. It sounds choppy - audible chunk boundaries. A previous pure-synthesis control used sine LUTs + inharmonic partial stacks + chirps and sounded synthetic.

TASK: Design a GENERATIVE synthesis architecture that renders this scene from an internal model of the sound itself - no recorded atoms, no chunk splicing, no gated on/off events. The render must be choppy-free BY CONSTRUCTION (continuous state, smooth parameter trajectories).

HARD CONSTRAINTS:
1. Implementation language is a minimal C-like systems language: no DSP libs, no FFT, no ML. You build oscillators, filters, noise from scratch. Deterministic hash-based pseudo-noise streams allowed; true RNG banned.
2. Byte-identical reruns required.
3. Output must pass statistical gates calibrated on real playground recordings: spectral flux, periodicity, stationarity (no slow amplitude wandering), crest factor, no clipping, no near-silent dips.
4. Must plausibly sound like little kids in a playground to human ears: high-pitched voices (F0 ~250-500 Hz), laughter, running footsteps, distant ambient play, playground air.

DELIVERABLE: a concrete architecture - (a) the sound-production model: what physical/acoustic phenomena you model and with what equations/blocks; (b) the control structure: how a deterministic "scene script" drives continuously-varying parameters over 30s; (c) the primitive DSP blocks needed (oscillators, filters, noise, envelopes) with enough detail to implement; (d) why choppiness is impossible by construction in your design; (e) the 3 biggest risks to it sounding real. Be implementable, not aspirational. Max ~600 words.
