# Fable round 1 — PAR tournament: servo/choppiness + contender map (2026-09-24)

You are an outside expert reviewer for the TNN byte-generation program. Micah
(the principal) asked you to "dive deeper" on two questions. Be thorough and

## Context: the tournament

Four contenders challenge NATIVE (stateful-sequential generation) for TNN byte
generation, tested per path (audio/image/video/dialogue) under a frozen
preregistered battery (§2: determinism, 9 quality bars + CHOP-1/2/3, coherence,
cost, RT-LONG honest-cents, RT-CASCADE, RT-EDGE, failure modes; §5 red team;
§6 adoption bars: overthrow needs all 9 bars + coherence ≥ native + strict win
on ≥1 axis + no regression elsewhere + byte-identical reruns + surviving red
team; ties keep native).

- A: pure-PAR end to end (every sample a pure function of plan and time, zero
  carried state). Partial win: RT-LONG 440.00 Hz +0.00¢ vs native 453.75 Hz
  +53.27¢; cascade-immune; coherence 1.0. Loses on COST: −46% single-threaded
  (integer-division phase recompute vs carried add). NO OVERTHROW (cost
  regression).
- B: PAR render, plan-seeded region state (carried state allowed, seeded ONLY
  from the plan at region boundaries, never from prior output). OVERTHROW on
  audio: RT-LONG octave latch 880→440 at 0¢, 9/9 gates, coherence 1.0, 0
  post-cut cascade propagation, 10/10 region-leakage probes pass, marginally
  cheaper than PAR. Known bug: inverted release envelope (deterministic,
  verdict-unaffecting, being fixed).
- C: bounded-feedback AR (region-scoped servo with plan-derived targets,
  boundary state reset, continuous feedback intra-region only). Overthrow on
  the letter: RT-LONG 440.02 Hz (+0.1¢) vs PAR 880.04 Hz (+1200.1¢); 9/9 gates;
  coherence ~1.0; cascade 0. Caveat: CHOP-3 shows 41 vs 29 flux spikes (all
  within the complete-event-list explanation window — 0 "unexplained" — but 6
  of the C-only spikes sit within 12 ms of servo-adapt block boundaries and
  the rest are servo-amplified vibrato extrema; the servo is not sonically
  transparent at flux level).
- D1: bidirectional two-pass render (forward plan pass + backward smoothing,
  no output→content feedback). TIE with PAR (bit-identical on the battery).
  D2: plan-reference (re-render-verifies the cue window, latches the plan's
  declared f0 — 0¢ honest error by construction, no sensor in the loop);
  overthrow-with-caveat (no native binary existed for the RT-LONG comparison).

Current per-path map: audio→B, image→PAR, generative video→PAR, predictive
video→state demanded, dialogue→state demanded (pure PAR loses on genuine
circularity: plan must precede rendering; dialogue history is produced by
rendering).

Micah's criterion for the servo, verbatim: "I don't want choppiness when
unnecessary."

The servo's proven wins: (1) RT-LONG octave correction (+0.1¢ vs +1200.1¢);
(2) dense N=16 chord self-limiting (servo holds RMS 0.54 FS while PAR's
plan-exact sum clips at int16 from N=2 up — PAR renders the plan faithfully
even when the plan asks for the impossible); (3) sustained 1292-block
corruption: 1295/1295 vetoed, gain trajectory bit-identical to clean.
The servo's cost: 12 extra supra-threshold flux spikes vs PAR on CHOP-3.
C already has a 10% model-error deadband (block RMS errors ≤10% vs the
analytic target are never corrected — the rail-pin defense by design).

Our design response (in flight): a GATED servo that engages ONLY when a
preregistered necessity criterion fires (e.g. measured block RMS deviates
from plan-derived target by >X%, or pitch inference disagrees with nominal
by >Y cents sustained over Z blocks); otherwise the pure plan-target path
with the servo fully idle. A dedicated crew is sweeping the servo's
operating envelope (plan-correct → octave lie → near-miss → sub-octave →
2-octave lie → vibrato/glide → N=16 chord → sustained corruption →
rail-pin adversarial) measuring servo activity vs benefit per scenario.

## Your questions (one batched round — answer all)

Q1. Necessity gating: is an engage-only-when-necessary gate the right shape
for this servo, or does it destroy something the continuous servo provides
(e.g. the N=16 self-limiting is continuous regulation, not a discrete
correction — does a gate preserve it)? Propose the necessity criterion YOU
would preregister (exact signals, thresholds, hysteresis if any) and justify
each choice against both the proven wins and the choppiness cost.

Q2. Choppiness standard: Micah judges "choppiness when unnecessary" by ear,
but our crews must test without ears (analyzer-first standing rule). Propose
an operational, measurable choppiness definition that you believe tracks what
he will hear — and say what it would MISS. If the 12 extra flux spikes are
all within PAR's own transient magnitude envelope, is there still a
regression? Give your judgment procedure for the 41-vs-29 question.

Q3. Red-team the gated servo: design the two or three adversarial plans most
likely to break engagement gating (faults sized to hide under the threshold;
plans engineered to flap the gate; slow drifts that never trip a block-level
criterion). For each, say whether you expect stock-C, gated-C, or servo-off
to survive, and why.

Q4. Contender map: attack the current per-path map (audio→B, image→PAR,
generative video→PAR, predictive video→state, dialogue→state). Which cell is
most likely wrong, and what single experiment would most efficiently falsify
it? Is there a path where you expect C's gated servo to beat B?

Q5. Blind spots: what is the tournament NOT testing that could overturn a
verdict? Name the two most important missing tests across all contenders and
paths, with concrete battery designs (frozen-battery style: exact metric,
bar, and what would count as a kill).

Be specific and quantitative where the evidence allows; flag every claim that
rests on judgment rather than measurement. If a question is unanswerable from
the evidence given, say so instead of inventing an answer.
