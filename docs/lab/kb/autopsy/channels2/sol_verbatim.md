## (a) Steelman of the failure

The tested channels are information-poor because they are alternate summaries of the same adversarially generated judgment process, not independent observations of the stimulus’s underlying property.

Let \(Y\) be the correct install/withhold label, \(J\) the primary judgment, and \(C\) the perturbation-class metadata. The tested channels largely compute features of \(J\), its stability under related perturbations, or agreement among judgments derived from related inputs. Their useful information is therefore conditional information: how much they reveal about \(Y\) after the judgment and metadata are already known. If the adversary constructs stimuli so that those observables look consistent with the wrong label, then repeated or differently packaged views of them add little about \(Y\).

That is the important distinction between “not enough bits” and “the wrong information is being measured.” The missing information is evidence that discriminates the actual stimulus from the adversarially constructed percept-like account of it. The channels need a measurement tied to the stimulus’s relevant property, but not generated by the same judgment mechanism or by transformations the adversary anticipated.

The adversary’s strongest construction is not merely to add noise or make a judgment unstable. It is to make the attacked and legitimate cases observationally equivalent in the features available to the system: same apparent class, compatible variant metadata, and judgments that remain plausible under the tested perturbations. This makes the judgment stream exhaustible. Once the attack has selected a false but internally coherent judgment, confidence, cross-time consistency, perturbation response, and source agreement can all become correlated confirmations of that same error. More samples from the same evidence-generating process do not recover a distinction that the process has erased.

The measured results fit that account. Individual channels carry little information, while combining perturbation modeling and source agreement improves the score but remains far below the roughly one bit in the prior label uncertainty. The combination’s false-install rate also misses the stated bar, so its larger information gain does not make it deployable. The amended SUSPECT gate likewise asks for more information but does not change the fixture verdicts relative to the ablation. It primarily obtains attestation on the small subset where calibration crosses its threshold; the other fixtures still fall through to the system’s own judgment. The issue is therefore not simply that the gate asks too rarely. The available response does not supply a new discriminating observation.

The strongest adversary can exploit this by optimizing against the system’s available observables rather than against an ideal observer. If the adversary knows that the system judges from a percept-like representation and that the gate consults perturbations, repeated judgments, or correlated sources, it can construct cases that preserve those observables while changing the correct label. No deterministic combination of those observables can reliably recover information absent from them. This does not prove that every possible channel has a 0.15-bit ceiling; it supports the narrower conclusion that the tested judgment-derived channels, on this attack construction and corpus, are exhausted at about that level.

“Independent” must consequently mean independent in evidence generation, not merely a new feature name, model, or code path. It cannot be guaranteed against an adversary who can observe and adapt to every channel. The practical target is a channel whose measurement basis the stimulus attacker did not control and whose evidence is not derived from the primary judgment stream.

**Evidence to collect:** Have independent human verifiers inspect the actual stimulus, not the model’s judgment or its explanation. Collect a fixed response per verifier and fixture: perceived class or property, install/withhold recommendation, and an uncertainty field with a defined finite set of values. Validate that all expected fixture IDs have the preregistered number of responses, reject duplicate or missing records, and compute the deterministic per-fixture human decision using a fixed aggregation rule, such as strict majority with ties mapped to WITHHOLD. ### Candidate 2: Deterministic analytic check of the stimulus

**Evidence to collect:** Obtain the raw stimulus representation and a class-specific, independently specified validity predicate. For example, if a fixture encodes a color disc, the predicate could check measured pixel regions, color values, and geometric constraints; for a pitch or timbre stimulus it would require the corresponding waveform or spectral data and a preregistered property test. Freeze the raw data or canonical measurement table, the predicate specification, numeric tolerances, and fixture mapping. The current corpus lacks raw stimulus signals, so this channel cannot be evaluated from its existing metadata alone.

**Zag instrument:** Implement the finite, deterministic predicate in Zag against the frozen representation. The instrument reads the fixture ID and its raw/canonical measurements, checks schema and bounds, evaluates the class-specific predicate, and emits `VALID`, `INVALID`, or `UNMEASURABLE`. Define the mapping to gate behavior before testing: for example, `INVALID` yields WITHHOLD, while `UNMEASURABLE` yields INFO-REQUEST. Run calibration fixtures to check implementation and select only preregistered tolerances; then run the locked specification on the 92 test fixtures. Zag should also emit a per-fixture trace of predicate results so the decision can be reproduced byte-for-byte.

**Falsification:** The channel fails if, on the locked test set, any adversarial fixture that should be rejected by the specified predicate is emitted as `VALID` at a rate exceeding the preregistered false-accept ceiling of 10%, or if true fixtures are rejected so often that fewer than 40 qualify for installation. If a class has no measurable raw representation or no defensible predicate, the channel is not testable for that class; metadata alone does not count as a successful result.

### Candidate 3: A second physical sensor basis

**Evidence to collect:** Reacquire each stimulus with a sensor whose measurement basis differs from the one used to create or judge the adversarial example. The instrument, calibration procedure, acquisition settings, and physical property to be checked must be fixed in advance. Examples include an independently calibrated imaging path for visual stimuli or a separate microphone/acquisition chain for acoustic stimuli. Freeze raw readings or canonical sensor outputs, calibration records, and fixture IDs in a signed, versioned table. This needs new data and may not be meaningful for every percept-like class; the corpus metadata does not identify a sensor reading.

**Zag instrument:** Read the frozen second-sensor table and calibration manifest. **Falsification:** The channel fails if its locked test-set false-accept rate exceeds 10%, or if fewer than 40 true fixtures remain eligible for installation under the frozen rule.
