**PRO-WRAPS**

**Strongest case.** PACKAGE 3 found no useful judgment-side signal under the frozen threat model: C2 produced 0.0000 bits on sense A and false-installed at 43.48%; C3 produced 0.0049 bits pooled (0.0046 A, 0.0043 B), with 45.0% false-install; confidence produced 0.0797 bits. C1’s 0.9968 bits show the adversarial examples are detectable by a different mechanism, not that judgment-side channels work. The appeal battery now tests the main plausible gaps in PACKAGE 3: amplitude, repeated observations, alternative transforms, and structured noise. The defensible claim is operational and scoped: if none clears the preregistered bar, retire this family for this threat model, while G1 requires new evidence before extending that decision to new threat models.

**Most damaging objection.** A finite battery of candidate regimes cannot establish that the whole judgment-side family is exhausted. In particular, a NULL result may reflect the tested transforms, noise regimes, or measurement power rather than the absence of every useful judgment-only channel. G1 limits the scope of the claim; it does not make the tested regimes exhaustive.

**Sharpest pre-result prediction endorsed.** No R1 level, R2 configuration, valid R3 cell, or R4 variant will clear the frozen recovery bar. In particular, R3 will not produce an informative deterministic channel, and the noise-based tests will remain below 0.15 bits or at false-install of 0.15 or higher.

**What forces PRO to concede.** Any one member clears both conjuncts on pooled TEST adversarials: **bits > 0.15 and false-install < 0.15**, with two byte-identical reruns and zero scorer cross-check errors. Under the frozen rule, that means NOT WRAPS for that member class, followed by the required fresh-seed confirmation battery.

**CON**

**Strongest case.** PACKAGE 3 tested specific instances, not the entire space of judgment-side methods: one transform family, one noise amplitude and distribution, and one noisy re-observation. C2’s 0.0000-bit result supports a strong conclusion about deterministic re-readings where the tested identity held; it does not establish that new transforms will satisfy that identity. Likewise, C3’s 0.0049 bits at ±300 pcm / ±8 img does not determine what happens across amplitudes, repeated draws, or structured noise. The appeal battery probes those concrete gaps. Until it reports, “permanent exhaustion” is not established by PACKAGE 3 alone.

**Most damaging objection.** The CON paper’s proposed mechanisms are possibilities, not preregistered recovery predictions. A 4–5 point agreement gap need not yield deployable information after majority voting; a new transform may preserve the judgment identity or fail calibration; and different noise may add no signal. CON therefore risks treating untested possibilities as evidence against retirement. The frozen recovery bar supplies a common operational standard rather than accepting suggestive mechanisms as recovery.

**Sharpest pre-result prediction endorsed.** The strongest falsifiable prediction consistent with CON’s case is that **at least one** appeal regime will clear the frozen bar. The paper does not specify which regime or give a quantitative prediction; its most direct candidate is R1, where some amplitude is predicted to separate fooled from correct judgments. That is a testable prediction, not a result implied by the underdetermination argument.

**What forces CON to concede.** No regime fires the frozen recovery bar: across all evaluated R1 levels, R2 configurations, valid R3 cells, and R4 variants, none has both **bits > 0.15 and false-install < 0.15**, with the required two byte-identical reruns and zero cross-check errors. That would satisfy CON’s stated NULL criterion and earn WRAPS for the frozen threat model.
