## What it changes

D1's memorization check looks for shared two-letter sequences between test
inputs and training inputs. Action sequences have no letters, so the
mechanism is restated for D2: (1) every test scenario's storm schedule,
layout, and crystal set is verified absent from training by a committed
exact-comparison check (not asserted), and (2) the independent red team must
*fail* to explain any passed episode by replaying a training trace or by
mapping the scenario to the nearest training schedule and replaying that
trace. The teeth are identical: if the red team succeeds → battery VOID,
generator fixed.

## Why

The memorization surface for action sequences is the scenario (storm
schedule + layout), not the trace: a memorized scenario would let the agent
replay a canned trace; a memorized trace alone is useless on a novel
scenario. N1–N3 commit the novelty verification to exact comparisons over
scenario records (not trust), and the red-team attacks test the two
memorization routes that bigrams stood in for in D1 — copying a training
trace, or mapping to the nearest training schedule.
