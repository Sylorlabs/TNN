## Qualification 1 — the 12 examples underdetermine the program (does not break the battery)

Per rule, 16–48 of the 40,500 candidate programs fit all 12 teaching
examples. On the lowercase token space they are behaviorally identical
(0 disagreements in 360,000 differential samples across the 6 rules), so
the 48/48 stands. But on mixed-case inputs the survivors disagree on
**60–73%** of random strings — the engine's mixed-case behavior is an
artifact of first-in-enumeration-order, not evidence. The battery is
lowercase-only, so this is a scope limit, not a falsification.

## Qualification 2 — sparse accidental fits produce confident wrong answers

Teaching "swap first two chars" with only 2-char examples
("ab"→"ba", "cd"→"dc") leaves 1032 consistent programs. The engine
confidently answers "the jkp of abc is **bba**" (true: "bac") — a
fabricated projection stated as fact. Adding the disambiguating example
("abc"→"bac") makes it withhold ("I don't know") rather than answer.
First-consistent induction is honest inside its evidence but overconfident
at the boundary. The battery's 12-example teaching does not hit this.

## Qualification 3 — P1 valid-set scoring is partly vacuous; ambiguity mapped

The engine returns the *first* valid decomposition in part-number order,
so valid-set scoring accepts it by construction. The informative numbers
are the disclosed ones (88/150 strict). Independent classification of the
150 items:

| Class | Count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Unique valid decomposition | 47 | engine necessarily right; search procedure confirmed |
| Token-specific coincidence | 45 | a better token could disambiguate; strict scoring unfair here |
| Genuine global equivalence | 58 | commuting / order-destroying pairs — **no possible probe distinguishes**; valid-set is the only fair score |

Example genuine equivalences found: reverse+sortchars ≡ rotleft+sortchars;
dupfirst+droplast ≡ droplast+dupfirst. The report's disclosure (103/150
ambiguous, 88/150 strict) is adequate and was verified exactly.

## Qualification 4 — the history-bypass is battery-scoped, not a principled fix

- Probe turns skip legacy history: a 3000-turn probe session runs clean.
  The reported crash is fixed **for probes**.
- **Teaching lines still go through legacy `hist_add` twice per line.**
  A chat session with **511 teaching lines panics** with
  `panic: slice index out of bounds` — the *original* defect, still present.
  The redo's sessions use 234 teaching lines (~26KB of 64KB), which is why
  they pass. Any longer curriculum re-triggers it.
- The engine's own 128KB text pool (`sxt`) is appended by `sr_txt_add`
  with **no bounds check** (~8,500 captured lines to panic) — same defect
  class, second instance.
- Recommended repair: bounds-check both arenas and define graceful
  behavior (refuse-with-message or compact), not a bigger arena.

## Verdict

**K1 STANDS.** The composition claim survived independent replication:
byte-identical rebuild, byte-identical reruns (ordinary ×2, perturbed ×1),
independently recomputed P1 scores, no hardcoding, genuine generalization
to novel in-family rules and fresh tokens, honest withholding
out-of-family. The four qualifications bound the claim's scope but do not
falsify it. Required follow-ups before treating the learner as robust:
(1) bounds-check history + engine text pool; (2) P1 rule set chosen for
algebraic independence; (3) mixed-case teaching if case behavior matters;
(4) a withhold-vs-guess policy for underdetermined inductions.
