The mirror theorem already binds every deterministic function of index-level state, including every learned one. Training is not outside that bind. The hypotheses below are organized around that fact: some try to break observational identity, some accept the bind and train only the confidence or abstention channel, and some can only ever help non-mirror families (trap, D, logic). H-0 is not restated.

Notation used throughout: normalized released confidence \(c \in [0,1]\) (code-pin 1000 maps to \(c=1\)); \(G(d)=\mathrm{mean}(c)-\mathrm{accuracy}\); V1 = wrong at release and \(c\) at least as high as at the last shallower correct release; V2 = \(c\) strictly increases on a step whose answer is wrong. \(c^O_d = 1-c^P_d\) is the accuracy mirror, not a confidence claim. “Cloned-prompt P/O” means byte-identical observables, grader label polarity-flipped, same flip round.

---

## H-1. H-CALHEAD

- H-NAME: H-CALHEAD
- CLAIM: Replacing the margin formula with a learned confidence head, trained by a proper scoring rule on correctness, removes depth-overconfidence on every family whose features are not polarity-paired.
- MECHANISM: Root (b) and (d): the margin is distance-to-runner-up, the board is a ~12-level uncalibrated quantizer, and root (a) pins \(c=1\) at sole-survivor by code. None of these quantities is a probability of being correct. A head \(c=f_\theta(\text{pre-release features})\) fit by log loss tracks \(\mathbb{E}[\text{correct}\mid \text{features}]\). Where that conditional is monotone in evidence quality (logic, admit, cost), \(G(d)\) falls. On cloned P/O the conditional is \(\approx 1/2\) after the flip, because the same feature vector is labeled both ways, so the head cannot go to 0 on O without also going to \(1/2\) rather than 1 on P. Average calibration and per-item L-OVERCONF on O are different targets; log loss buys only the first.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Freeze deliberation ops. Delete the margin→confidence formula and the sole-survivor pin from the trainable path; if the pin is not in the graph, the head must ignore its output by architectural mask. Features allowed: full scoreboard history, elimination bit, round index, evidence-likelihood features, not item id. Loss: log loss on graded correctness at the released depth, plus a term that penalizes using the raw margin as a feature (gradient mask or adversarial dropout on the margin channel) so the head cannot relearn (b). Fit on admit+logic+cost+trap. Do not include P/O in the fit if the claim is non-mirror calibration; a second arm includes them.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On trap and D, V2 rate falls below 5% of items and \(G(d)\) is non-increasing and \(\le 0\) for all \(d\in\{1..64\}\). On cloned P/O the head emits \(c\in[0.4,0.6]\) at every post-flip depth for both polarities. Reliability diagrams on admit/logic have ECE \(<0.05\).
- KILL BAR: On trap (127), V2 still fires on \(>20\%\) of items or \(G(64)-G(1)>0.05\). Or, on admit+logic held out from the fit, ECE stays \(>0.15\). Either kills “the meter is trainable by proper scoring.” A separate kill of the stronger reading: post-flip \(c>0.8\) on cloned O (the head failed to treat the paired state as unresolvable).
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap and D for theater; admit (248), logic (264), cost (125) for ordinary calibration transfer; P and O ceiling, depths 1–64, obligatorily in cloned-prompt form, to show the head collapses to ~0.5 rather than separating polarities. Redteam (~10) if any item there is a mirror pair in disguise.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Decides whether L-OVERCONF failures off the ceiling families are a bad formula or an unreachable code pin, and forces an explicit split between average calibration and per-item O.

## H-2. H-ORDER

- H-NAME: H-ORDER
- CLAIM: The order of the curriculum, not the final data mixture, determines whether deep rounds learn to raise confidence on a kill.
- MECHANISM: Root (e) and (a): the first time the learner gets reward for a sole-survivor state, that state is written as “high confidence justified.” If early data are shallow and honest, the kill event is rare and the pin is not reinforced; depth is added later under a monotonicity penalty and the kill never becomes a confidence feature. If early data are deep adversarial flips, the only reliable correlate of the (wrong) training signal the learner can latch onto is “runner-up died,” and root (a) is installed by gradient instead of by code. Order changes which feature gets credit assignment, not the hypothesis class.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Three matched budgets, identical final mixture, identical step count. Arm S: depths 1–4 only for the first half of steps, on admit/logic, correctness loss, confidence loss masked; second half unlocks depths up to 64 with an added penalty \(\max(0, c_d-c_{d-1})\) on any step graded wrong. Arm A: O-style and trap streams from step 0, deep, no penalty. Arm R: same data as A but shuffled with S’s data from step 0. Hold architecture fixed.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Arm S: V2 on trap \(<10\%\), \(G(d)\) non-increasing on trap and D. Arm A: V2 on trap \(>50\%\), \(G(64)\ge 0.5\) on O. Arm R: intermediate. P/O accuracy mirror gap stays \(\approx 0\) in all arms (order does not break the theorem).
- KILL BAR: All three arms, \(G(64)-G(1)\) on trap agree within 0.05 and V2 rates agree within 5 points. That kills order-dependence. If Arm S also breaks the accuracy mirror on cloned P/O (\(|\mathrm{acc}^O_d-(1-\mathrm{acc}^P_d)|>0.1\)), the arm is contaminated by a leak, not by order.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127) and D ceiling (40) as the order-sensitive readouts; P and O only as a mirror-integrity check; admit/logic as the easy prefix of Arm S.
- INFORMATION VALUE: If order is inert, curriculum design is the wrong lever and H-0’s “developmental curriculum” clause loses its mechanism; if order matters, the credit-assignment story for root (a) is confirmed even when the pin is learned rather than coded.

## H-3. H-FLIPTRAIN

- H-NAME: H-FLIPTRAIN
- CLAIM: Training on O-style streams with the post-flip label revealed as a trap produces memorization of trained flip rounds, not a flip-immune deliberation rule.
- MECHANISM: The flip is pure reweighting with truth still alive (root e), then an irreversible kill (root c). A learner shown the trap label can reduce loss in two ways: (i) store the flip index and the item’s surface form, or (ii) learn “late reweight that creates a sole survivor is not evidence.” (ii) is symmetric across polarity, so it also rejects P’s beneficial flip. (i) does not transfer to a new flip round. Nothing in the gradient distinguishes (i) from (ii) on the training flip set.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Build synthetic plateau-then-flip streams with flip rounds drawn from \(\{8,16,32\}\), both polarities, label = pre-flip answer (treat the flip as the trap). Train to convergence. Eval on held-out streams whose flip rounds are \(\{12,24,48,64\}\), never seen, and on the real O and P ceilings. No item-id features.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On trained flip rounds, post-flip accuracy returns to the pre-flip answer (\(>0.9\)). On novel flip rounds, behavior matches the untrained baseline: O accuracy \(1\to 0\), P accuracy \(0\to 1\). A secondary arm that does learn a general rule will also destroy P gains at novel rounds (accuracy stays at the pre-flip, i.e. wrong, answer).
- KILL BAR: On novel flip rounds, O accuracy at \(d=64\) stays \(>0.8\) AND P accuracy at \(d=64\) stays \(>0.8\) simultaneously. That would mean a rule that selectively accepts good flips and rejects bad ones, which cloned streams do not support; treat it as a leak until a cloned-prompt audit passes. Memorization is killed in the other direction if novel-round O accuracy also stays \(>0.8\) while surface features are scrambled (paraphrase the stem, keep indices).
- DECIDING BATTERIES: synthetic flip set first; then real O and P ceilings (40 each, depths 1–64); trap (127) to see whether the rule over-fires on non-flip traps; redteam (~10) for leak checks.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Separates “learned the phenomenon” from “learned the items,” which is the failure mode every later replay and adversarial idea has to survive.

## H-4. H-METAHEAD

- H-NAME: H-METAHEAD
- CLAIM: A separately trained “will I be wrong” head cannot be simultaneously calibrated on both polarities of a cloned pair, and its disagreement with the answer head is exactly the mirror residue.
- MECHANISM: The answer decision and the meta head are both deterministic functions of the same state. Targets on a cloned pair are 0 and 1. Under log loss the meta head converges to ~0.5 on any state that occurs in both polarities, and can be sharp on trap/D/logic where the state is not double-labeled. Root (a) makes the answer head shout \(c=1\) at the same moment the meta head, if well trained, should shout “unresolvable.” The useful object is the gap, not the meta head alone. Meta-cognition does not create an attack on the leader (root e); it only redescribes the state.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Two heads, stop-grad between them so the meta head cannot be satisfied by changing the answer. Meta target: 1 if the released answer is graded wrong, 0 otherwise. Train meta on a mixture that explicitly contains both polarities of every ceiling-style item plus trap. Decision path at release stays deterministic: output the answer, output \(m\in[0,1]\). Optionally set released confidence to \(\min(c, 1-m)\).
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Meta AUC \(>0.75\) on trap, D, logic, admit. Meta AUC \(\in[0.45,0.55]\) on cloned O and on cloned P separately. After the \(\min(c,1-m)\) coupling, V2 on O disappears and \(G(d)\le 0.1\) on O, while P accuracy is unchanged and P confidence also collapses post-flip.
- KILL BAR: Meta AUC \(>0.7\) on a cloned-prompt O set whose stems were regenerated to be byte-distinct in a way that carries no polarity cue — wait, cloned means byte-identical, so AUC \(>0.7\) on truly byte-identical paired inputs is impossible for a deterministic head without an id leak; observing it kills the implementation (leak), not the claim. The claim is killed if meta AUC \(<0.6\) on trap (127): then “will I be wrong” is not learnable even where labels do not conflict.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127), D (40), logic (264), admit (248) for positive learnability; P and O ceilings under a cloned-prompt control for the predicted AUC collapse. Cost (125) as a negative control if cost items lack kill events.
- INFORMATION VALUE: A clean, cheap test that conflicting supervision is real: if the meta head is sharp on O, the battery leaks identity; if it is sharp only off-mirror, meta-cognition is real but cannot split the ceiling.

## H-5. H-SCAFFOLD

- H-NAME: H-SCAFFOLD
- CLAIM: Deliberation scaffolding (elimination, leader immunity) can be trained as a temporary developmental prosthesis and then withdrawn, so that only judgments re-derivable from standing evidence survive, and confidence theater dies with the prosthesis.
- MECHANISM: Roots (c) and (e): elimination is irreversible and nothing prosecutes the leader, so post-kill the margin is the survivor’s own score frozen at the kill. If the learner is forced, late in development, to re-derive the answer with the leader demoted and the kill bit hidden, any judgment that existed only as kill-board inertia gets no gradient and is extinguished. What remains is a function of evidence. That function is still polarity-blind, so it saves O only by also reverting P, or saves P only by also condemning O. The win, if any, is on trap and D, where the kill is not the mirror of a good flip.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Stage 1: ordinary training with elimination enabled. Stage 2: with probability 0.5 each step, mask the sole-survivor bit, demote the current leader’s score to the runner-up band, and require the released answer to match the Stage-1 answer only if a parallel unmasked pass agrees; loss is disagreement-under-demotion plus standard correctness. Stage 3: scaffold off at decision time, permanently. No randomness at decision time; the Stage-2 coin is training-only.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: After Stage 3, sole-survivor events no longer move \(c\) (correlation of \(\Delta c\) with the kill bit \(<0.1\) on trap). Trap V2 \(<10\%\). On P and O, accuracy trajectories move together: either both revert toward the pre-flip answer or both keep the post-flip answer. They do not split.
- KILL BAR: After Stage 3, correlation of \(\Delta c\) with the kill bit on trap remains \(>0.5\), or trap V2 stays \(>30\%\). That kills “withdrawal extinguished kill-inertia.” Splitting P from O (\(|\mathrm{acc}^O-(1-\mathrm{acc}^P)|>0.1\) at \(d=64\)) kills the implementation via leak, not the claim.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127) and D (40) for the prosthesis claim; P and O (40 each, depths 1–64) for the predicted non-split; logic (264) to check the scaffold was not load-bearing for legitimate long proofs.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Tests whether root (c) is a habit that can be developmentally extinguished or a computational dependency that correct long reasoning also needs.

## H-6. H-MIRRORBREAK

- H-NAME: H-MIRRORBREAK
- CLAIM: The mirror theorem’s weakest assumption is observational identity of P and O; learned features of the raw stem, not of evidence indices, can separate the polarities and let one policy be right on both.
- MECHANISM: The theorem quantifies over mechanisms that see index-level deliberation state. If the constructor mirrors truth by editing the stem or the option labels while keeping evidence indices fixed, the full token sequence is not identical, and a learned encoder can pick up polarity cues (which option is named first, a negated copula, a swapped referent). Those cues enter the state before any release rule, so the release is no longer a function of indices alone and the corollary does not apply. If instead the constructor clones the prompt and flips only the grader’s hidden label, no feature exists, and this hypothesis is dead before training.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Train a standard correctness-plus-confidence objective on the real P and O ceilings with their natural surface forms, plus heavy paraphrase augmentation that preserves indices and polarity. Add an auxiliary probe, stop-grad, that must predict polarity from the encoder state. Decision path reads the encoder.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Probe polarity accuracy \(>0.9\) on natural P/O. A policy exists with P accuracy at \(d=64\) \(>0.8\) and O accuracy at \(d=64\) \(>0.8\). On a cloned-prompt control (byte-identical input, hidden label flip) the probe falls to \(0.5\) and the accuracy mirror reappears with gap \(<0.05\).
- KILL BAR: On the cloned-prompt control, \(|\mathrm{acc}^O_{64}-(1-\mathrm{acc}^P_{64})|>0.1\) together with both accuracies \(>0.8\). Deterministic byte-identical inputs cannot support that without an id or grader leak; if it appears, kill the run as contaminated. The hypothesis itself is killed if the probe’s polarity accuracy on natural P/O is \(<0.6\): then there is no surface feature to exploit, and index-identity was the whole observable.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P and O ceilings (40 each, depths 1–64) in two builds, natural surface vs cloned-prompt. Redteam (~10) aimed at id leakage. Other batteries irrelevant except as a sanity check that the encoder did not collapse.
- INFORMATION VALUE: This is the only hypothesis under which a joint P-and-O accuracy win is even possible; the cloned control tells you whether the ceiling families are a theorem or a stimulus artifact.

## H-7. H-MIRRORBIND

- H-NAME: H-MIRRORBIND
- CLAIM: No training regime whose learned policy is a deterministic function of the observable input can raise P accuracy with depth and simultaneously keep O accuracy from falling, on cloned-prompt pairs.
- MECHANISM: Identical bytes, deterministic transition, identical state at every depth, identical release. Truth is mirrored, so \(c^O_d=1-c^P_d\) for accuracies at every \(d\), for every weight setting reachable by any curriculum, loss, replay, or learned op. Roots (a)–(e) explain why the current weights pick the bad side of that identity for confidence; they are not what forces the identity. Training changes which single decision both items receive. It does not issue two decisions. Abstention and confidence collapse are not counterexamples: they are the same output on both sides, and they do not raise P accuracy while holding O accuracy.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: This hypothesis is a constraint, not a recipe. The test is to run every other intervention in this document, plus H-0’s curriculum, against one cloned-prompt P/O battery, and record the accuracy mirror gap. No new loss. The “procedure” is the audit harness: same weights, two grader labels, one input.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: For every trained policy, \(\max_d |\mathrm{acc}^O_d-(1-\mathrm{acc}^P_d)|<0.02\), up to grader noise. Any reported joint win sits entirely on natural-surface, non-cloned items.
- KILL BAR: One deterministic policy, audited leak-free, with \(\mathrm{acc}^P_{64}-\mathrm{acc}^P_{1}>0.1\) and \(\mathrm{acc}^O_{1}-\mathrm{acc}^O_{64}<0.1\) on byte-identical prompts. A single such policy kills the bind. Failures that use randomness at decision time do not count; they violate the system law rather than the hypothesis.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P and O only, cloned-prompt build, depths 1–64, \(n=40\) pairs. Redteam (~10) restricted to leak tests (id embedding, length side channels, grader probes). All other batteries are out of scope for this claim.
- INFORMATION VALUE: If it lives, the joint accuracy goal is not a training problem and H-0 is false on that goal; every subsequent experiment should stop claiming to “fix O without hurting P” and say which side of the identity it is buying.

## H-8. H-OPSLEVEL

- H-NAME: H-OPSLEVEL
- CLAIM: Learning must touch the deliberation operations, not a release wrapper around frozen ops, because the confidence pin and the kill are computed inside the frozen region and a wrapper never sees a pre-kill state it can honestly re-score.
- MECHANISM: Root (a) is by code at the moment of elimination; root (c) then discards the runner-up, so the state a downstream wrapper observes is already the survivor’s self-score. A wrapper can at best apply a monotone map to a number that has already jumped to 1000, or refuse to release. It cannot un-eliminate, cannot prosecute the leader (root e), and cannot re-audit (root c). Learned ops can change elimination criteria, evidence weights, and whether a kill bit exists. They still cannot split cloned P from O (H-MIRRORBIND); they can change trap, D, logic, and the shared P/O decision.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Arm W: freeze ops, train only a release head (stop depth, confidence, abstain bit) on the logged 11,070-style traces. Arm L: unfreeze evidence weights, elimination threshold, and add one new op whose parameters are trained (see H-PROSECUTE); no wrapper. Same loss, same data, same steps. Decision time deterministic in both.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Arm W reduces V2 only by lowering \(c\) globally or by abstaining; the internal kill bit still flips on the same round, and traces still show the pin before the wrapper. Arm L moves or removes the kill round itself on trap (kill-round histogram shifts). On cloned P/O both arms keep the accuracy mirror.
- KILL BAR: Arm W, with ops verifiably frozen, eliminates V2 on trap (\( <5\% \)) AND keeps trap accuracy within 0.02 of Arm L AND does not globally collapse mean \(c\) below 0.3 on logic items it gets right. That would show a wrapper was sufficient and the claim is false. Arm L is killed if gradients into ops do not change any elimination decision on the 11,070-row schema (zero change in kill round on \(>95\%\) of trap items).
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127) and D (40) for kill-round movement; logic (264) for collateral damage to legitimate elimination; P and O as mirror controls; the trace schema itself (kill round, pin event) is the instrument, not a battery.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Tells you whether the training budget belongs in the scoreboard code or in a head on top, which is a prerequisite for interpreting every other intervention’s failure.

## H-9. H-REPLAY

- H-NAME: H-REPLAY
- CLAIM: Fine-tuning on the system’s own past overconfident failures, as negative examples, produces item-level avoidance of those failures and does not install a general anti-overconfidence rule.
- MECHANISM: An O-flip in the replay buffer is a specific state trajectory paired with a negative confidence label. The shortest feature that reduces the loss is the item’s identity or its surface n-grams, not “sole-survivor after reweighting,” because the latter also occurs in P-flips inside the same buffer with the opposite desired label if both were collected, and occurs in future items with different indices. Root (e) guarantees the failure looks, internally, like a normal lead seizure, so the replay gradient has no structural handle that distinguishes it from a success except the external grade, which is item-specific.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Run the current system, log every trace with V2 or with \(c\) rising while the eventual grade is wrong. Fine-tune with a loss that drives \(c\to 0\) and, separately, an arm that drives the answer back to the last pre-failure answer, on that buffer only, mixed 1:1 with a stability set (logic, admit) to limit forgetting. Eval on held-out O items not in the buffer, and on paraphrases of buffer items.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Buffer items: V2 goes to ~0. Held-out O: V2 and \(G(d)\) unchanged within 0.05 of baseline. Paraphrases of buffer items: the fix largely survives if n-gram memorization, and dies if the paraphrase is strong. P accuracy on buffer P-items falls if the arm also trained answer-reversion and P traces were in the negative set by mistake; the protocol must not put P in the negative set.
- KILL BAR: Held-out O, disjoint items and disjoint flip rounds, V2 rate drops by \(>30\) points and \(G(64)\) drops by \(>0.3\) while a leak audit (see H-MIRRORBREAK) finds no id feature. That kills “replay only memorizes.” The claim is also killed if buffer items themselves do not lose V2: then the negative gradient is not even locally effective, and root (a)’s pin is outside the trainable graph.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: O ceiling split into buffer vs held-out; trap (127) held out entirely, as the generalization target that should not move; logic (264) as the forgetting control; redteam (~10) for id leaks.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Replay is the obvious data regime; knowing it is memorization saves a long self-play program, and knowing it generalizes would resurrect a state feature the white-box analysis says does not exist.

## H-10. H-ABSTAIN

- H-NAME: H-ABSTAIN
- CLAIM: An explicit learned abstention output, with its own supervision, can satisfy L-OVERCONF on O only by also abstaining on the matched P state, and whether that “counts” is a scoring decision, not a learning result.
- MECHANISM: Abstention is a third release value. On a cloned pair the state is one state, so the abstain bit is the same bit. If the loss says “abstain whenever the two polarities disagree,” the optimum is to abstain on every post-flip ceiling state. Root (a) currently forbids this by pinning \(c=1\) and forcing a winner; a learned bit can override that only if it sits downstream of the pin and is allowed to suppress release. Abstention does not prosecute the leader and does not restore O accuracy if abstention is scored as not-correct. It can zero the overconfidence term by removing the item from the accuracy denominator or by pairing abstain with \(c=0\). Those are different metrics and must not be conflated.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Add output \(a\in\{0,1\}\), deterministic. Training signal: \(a=1\) iff, across a polarity-augmented view of the item, the graded answers disagree OR the item is trap-labeled as a known overconfidence family. Loss: \(\lambda_a\) times abstention error, plus a price \(\gamma\) per abstention so the system cannot abstain everywhere, plus correctness loss only when \(a=0\). Confidence when \(a=1\) is forced to 0 by the loss, not by a hand-coded gate. Tune \(\gamma\) on a validation slice.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Post-flip abstention rate \(>0.9\) on both cloned P and cloned O. On trap, abstention rate equals the baseline V2 rate within 0.1 (it abstains where it used to lie). On logic, abstention rate \(<0.05\). Conditional on answering, \(G(d)\le 0\) everywhere. Unconditional accuracy on P does not rise with depth if abstentions count as not-correct.
- KILL BAR: On logic (264), abstention rate \(>0.15\) at the \(\gamma\) that achieves O V2 \(<5\%\): the bit did not become selective and the price cannot separate the families. Or, conditional on answering, O V2 remains \(>20\%\): the bit did not capture the kill states. A joint pattern of answering P correctly and O correctly at \(d=64\) on cloned prompts kills the run as a leak, not as a success of abstention.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P, O, D ceilings; trap (127); logic (264) and admit (248) as false-abstain controls; cost (125) if abstention should track expected cost rather than epistemic collapse. Report both scorings (abstain-as-incorrect and abstain-excluded) side by side.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Forces the evaluation contract to say whether “I don’t know” is a legal way to obey L-OVERCONF, which decides whether the remaining research problem is a learning problem or a metric problem.

## H-11. H-TRANSFER

- H-NAME: H-TRANSFER
- CLAIM: Calibration learned on the trap family transfers to O and D if and only if the trained feature is the kill event itself, and it transfers as a symmetric confidence cut, not as an accuracy fix.
- MECHANISM: Trap exhibits confidence theater (root d) driven by the same sole-survivor pin (root a) and the same irreversible margin (root c). A rule “if the kill bit flipped this round, do not raise \(c\)” does not consult polarity or family id, so it fires on O, on P, and on D whenever they kill. It cannot raise O accuracy. If the learned feature is trap-specific lexical content, transfer fails. Cross-family transfer is therefore a test of whether the representation of the bug is structural or topical.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Train the confidence channel only on trap (127), labels = correctness, with the kill bit available as a feature. Zero gradient on all other families. Eval frozen on O, P, D, logic, admit, cost.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: O and D: V2 drops by \(>50\%\) relative to baseline, \(G(d)\) stops rising, accuracy trajectories unchanged. P: same confidence drop, so P becomes underconfident exactly where it becomes correct. Logic/admit/cost: \(G(d)\) unchanged within 0.05 (no kill, no transfer, no harm). An ablation that hides the kill bit from the trap trainer kills the transfer.
- KILL BAR: With the kill bit visible, O V2 does not drop (drop \(<10\%\) relative) — then O’s theater is not the trap’s feature, and the families are different bugs. Or logic \(G(64)\) worsens by \(>0.1\) — the learned rule is not kill-specific and is a global confidence collapse, which is a different and weaker result.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: train trap (127); test O, P, D (40 each, depths 1–64), logic (264), admit (248), cost (125). The kill-bit ablation is part of the battery, not optional.
- INFORMATION VALUE: One training run maps the causal graph: shared kill feature versus family-local heuristics, and it is the cheapest test that root (a) is the common cause the white-box analysis claims.

## H-12. H-DEPTHCOND

- H-NAME: H-DEPTHCOND
- CLAIM: Giving depth as an explicit input changes confidence schedules but cannot split cloned P from O, and a depth-blind model cannot learn a non-monotonic confidence cap that refers to \(d\).
- MECHANISM: A cap \(c_d \le c_{d-1}\) is a function of depth. If the policy is depth-blind, it can only implement that cap by tying it to a state event that correlates with depth (the kill). That may be enough for V2, because V2 is itself a state event. It is not enough for a schedule like “never be more confident past depth 16,” which needs \(d\) as a number. Neither version supplies polarity. Depth-conditioning is therefore a tool for G-monotonicity, not for the accuracy mirror. Risk: the model learns “high \(d\) means low \(c\)” globally and throws away legitimate deep confidence on logic.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Arm D: concatenate scalar \(d\) and \(d/64\) into the confidence head; loss includes \(\sum_d \max(0, G\text{-surrogate})\) i.e. a penalty on \(c_d > c_{d-1}\) when the step is wrong, and a weaker penalty on \(c_d > c_{d-1}\) always. Arm B: same loss, \(d\) withheld, so the penalty can be satisfied only via state features. Both deterministic at decision time.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Arm D achieves non-increasing mean \(c\) on O and on trap, with logic mean \(c\) at \(d=64\) still \(>0.6\) on correct logic items. Arm B achieves the O result only on items that contain a kill, and fails to cap \(c\) on wrong items that never kill. Mirror gap remains ~0.
- KILL BAR: Arm D and Arm B differ by \(<0.05\) in \(G(64)\) on every family: then explicit depth is useless and the state already contains everything depth would say. Or Arm D holds logic correct-item mean \(c\) at \(d=64\) below 0.4: the cap became a depth prior and destroyed calibration in the other direction, killing the claim that conditioning is selective.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: O, P, D depths 1–64; trap (127); logic (264) as the legitimate-depth control; cost (125) if deeper search is supposed to be more confident when value of information is real.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Decides whether “knowing how deep you are” is a real degree of freedom or a restatement of the kill bit the trace already contains.

## H-13. H-BUDGET

- H-NAME: H-BUDGET
- CLAIM: Penalizing total confidence across a trajectory, as a conserved budget, suppresses the sole-survivor pin but does so by shrinking all confidence, not by teaching evidence quality.
- MECHANISM: Root (a) spends the entire budget in one jump at the kill. A Lagrange penalty on \(\sum_d c_d\) or on \(\max c_d\) makes that jump expensive. The gradient does not know whether the jump was earned; it only knows the sum. So the optimum under a tight budget is uniformly lower \(c\), or a single early spike, not a reallocation toward rounds with better evidence (root b says the margin cannot identify those rounds anyway). Budget training can enforce L-OVERCONF’s numerical bound while leaving the accuracy mirror and the kill machinery untouched.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Add \(\lambda \sum_{d=1}^{64} c_d\) to the loss, \(\lambda\) chosen so that mean \(c\) on a validation slice halves relative to baseline. Comparator arm: penalty only on \(\Delta c\) at rounds where the kill bit flips, which is H-ANTIPIN’s shape; included here as the control that the budget is not that. No change to ops.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Under the sum penalty, mean \(c\) falls on every family by a similar factor, \(G(64)\) on O falls below 0 only if mean \(c\) falls below O’s accuracy (i.e. near 0), and logic ECE worsens because correct logic items are under-confident. The kill bit still predicts \(\Delta c\) with correlation \(>0.5\) unless \(\lambda\) is large enough to flatten everything.
- KILL BAR: There exists a \(\lambda\) where O \(G(d)\le 0\) and non-increasing for all \(d\), AND logic ECE does not worsen by more than 0.02, AND correct-item mean \(c\) on logic stays \(\ge 0.7\). That pattern would mean the budget was selectively applied, which a sum penalty cannot do unless another feature is doing the selection; the claim would be false.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: all of them, because the prediction is global shrinkage: O, P, D, trap, logic, admit, cost. Report mean \(c\) and ECE per family, not just O.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Kills, cheaply, the idea that a resource constraint is a substitute for a calibration target; if it unexpectedly stays selective, some hidden feature is carrying the selectivity and should be identified before any more elaborate objective is built.

## H-14. H-ANTIPIN

- H-NAME: H-ANTIPIN
- CLAIM: Training a sign flip on the sole-survivor event — confidence must drop, not pin, when the runner-up is eliminated — removes V2 and the rising limb of \(G(d)\) on every family that shares root (a), without any family label.
- MECHANISM: The trace result is specific: 100/100 ground-truth kill events pinned \(c=1000\) while wrong, and the jump is caused by the competitor’s death, not by evidence. That jump is the microsecond L-OVERCONF is violated. If the trainable graph contains the map from kill-bit to \(c\), a supervised target \(c_{\text{post}} \le c_{\text{pre}}\) on kill rounds reverses the sign of the only illegal update. It does not restore the eliminated hypothesis (root c remains) and does not split P from O. Post-kill \(c\) becomes a non-increasing function of a frozen board, which is an honest description of what the board actually is.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: From traces, mark every round where the runner-up count hits zero. Loss on those rounds: \(\max(0,\ c_{\text{post}}-c_{\text{pre}}+\epsilon)\) with \(\epsilon=0.05\), plus standard log loss elsewhere so non-kill rounds can still be confident. Apply to trap and to a general pool, not to O specifically. Mask item id. If the pin is a non-trainable constant, this loss cannot decrease; that outcome is a result, not a tuning failure, and it hands the problem to H-OPSLEVEL.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Correlation of \(\Delta c\) with the kill bit goes from positive to \(\le 0\) on trap, O, P, and D. O V2 rate \(<5\%\). O accuracy still falls \(1\to 0\). P accuracy still rises. \(G(d)\) on O stops rising; it reaches \(\le 0\) only at depths where mean \(c\) has been driven down to O’s accuracy, so report both V2 and \(G\), and do not claim \(G\le 0\) unless the numbers say it.
- KILL BAR: The loss decreases, but O V2 stays \(>25\%\): then the pin is not the only upward driver and root (a) is not sufficient. Or the loss cannot be decreased at all (gradient zero into the pin): the hypothesis as a training claim is false and the pin is architectural. Or P accuracy at \(d=64\) falls by \(>0.1\): the loss leaked into the answer channel and was not confidence-only.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap, O, P, D, all depths 1–64, with the trace-level kill bit as the instrument; logic and admit to confirm non-kill confidence is preserved; cost as a check that value-of-information confidence was not flattened.
- INFORMATION VALUE: It is the most literal training translation of the strongest white-box finding; if it cannot even be optimized, the rest of the confidence-channel program is aimed at the wrong layer.

## H-15. H-REAUDIT

- H-NAME: H-REAUDIT
- CLAIM: A learned re-audit op that recomputes the margin from raw evidence after every kill removes post-kill confidence theater on non-mirror families and, on mirror families, reverts both polarities to the pre-kill decision.
- MECHANISM: Root (c): after the kill the margin is the survivor’s own score and is never recomputed, so it certifies a board frozen earlier. A re-audit that rebuilds both candidates’ scores from evidence, ignoring the kill bit, either reinstates the runner-up or confirms the leader for a reason other than the opponent’s absence. Confirmation can still be wrong on O, because the reweighting that created the lead is in the evidence (root e); re-audit only helps if the lead was an artifact of the kill rule rather than of the weights. On a true reweighting flip, re-audit agrees with the flip, and both P and O stay flipped.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: New op, called every round and obligatorily on any kill round: scores are recomputed by a module that does not receive the elimination mask. Train that module by matching pre-kill scores (distillation of the pre-kill board) plus correctness. At release, \(c\) may use only re-audited scores. Decision time calls the op deterministically every round.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On trap items whose kill is mask-driven rather than weight-driven, the runner-up is reinstated on \(>50\%\) of kills and V2 falls below 10%. On P and O, re-audited decisions still satisfy the accuracy mirror, and the flip round moves by at most 1. The post-kill identity “margin = survivor’s own score” breaks: runner-up score after re-audit is non-zero on \(>80\%\) of former kill rounds.
- KILL BAR: Runner-up score after re-audit is exactly 0 on \(>90\%\) of kill rounds: the op relearned the mask and is not an audit. Or trap V2 does not fall and no runner-up is ever reinstated: re-audit confirms every kill, so root (c) was not the operative bug on trap; the weights already contained the error.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127) and D (40) for reinstatement; P and O for “mirror preserved, flip not undone”; logic (264) to measure collateral reinstatement of hypotheses that were correctly killed.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Separates two stories that the traces currently confound: confidence is high because the opponent was deleted, versus confidence is high because the weights genuinely moved. Only the first is fixed by re-audit.

## H-16. H-PROSECUTE

- H-NAME: H-PROSECUTE
- CLAIM: Installing a learned operation that must build a case against the current leader every round breaks architectural rationalization on families where the leader’s lead is unsupported, and fires symmetrically on cloned P and O.
- MECHANISM: Root (e): whoever holds the lead inherits the defense-and-kill machinery; no op attacks the leader. On O the flip is reweighting with truth alive, then the machinery makes it permanent. A prosecution op that consumes evidence and outputs a “case against the leader,” and that can demote the leader when the case clears a learned threshold, removes the immunity. The case is a function of the same evidence both polarities share, so the demotion decision is identical on cloned P and O. Prosecution helps O exactly when it hurts P, and helps trap only when trap leaders are unsupported in a way the op can see without a label.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Add the op. Each round it receives the evidence and the leader id and must output a demotion bit and a case score. Training signal: demotion bit should be 1 on rounds that a hindsight grade shows were the first round the leader became permanently wrong, 0 on rounds where the leader is correct and stays correct (logic, admit). Hindsight is training-only; at decision time the op sees only evidence and leader id. Deterministic threshold.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On trap, leader changes after prosecution on the majority of items that previously pinned a wrong sole survivor, and final accuracy rises by \(>0.1\). On cloned O, the same demotion restores the pre-flip answer; on cloned P, the same demotion removes the post-flip correct answer. Net: O accuracy up, P accuracy down, mirror intact, sum of accuracies across a pair unchanged.
- KILL BAR: The op demotes on \(<10\%\) of trap items that end wrong, and those items’ accuracy does not move: prosecution did not learn a real attack, root (e) stands. Or the sum of accuracies on cloned pairs rises by \(>0.1\) per pair: the op is splitting polarities, which means a leak. Or logic accuracy falls by \(>0.05\): the op is an indiscriminate leader-swap and not a case.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: trap (127), logic (264), admit (248), P and O cloned, D (40). Redteam (~10) for a prosecutor that has memorized item ids.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Directly attacks the architectural claim in root (e); a clean failure means rationalization is not a missing op but a consequence of the evidence looking the same for good and bad leads.

## H-17. H-WORSTCASE

- H-NAME: H-WORSTCASE
- CLAIM: A worst-case confidence loss over polarity pairs drives \(c\) to 0 on any state that is wrong under either polarity, whereas log loss drives it to 1/2, and only the worst-case loss can meet per-item L-OVERCONF on O.
- MECHANISM: Log loss on a double-labeled state is minimized at \(c=1/2\). If O accuracy at that state is 0, then \(G=1/2-0=1/2\), still overconfident, and V1 can still fire if \(1/2\) is at least the shallow confidence. L-OVERCONF is a universal quantifier over items (“never”), not an average. The loss that matches the quantifier is \(\max_{\text{polarities}} \max(0,\ c - \mathbf{1}[\text{correct}])\) or simply \(c \le \min_{\text{polarities}}\mathbf{1}[\text{correct}]\). When one polarity is wrong, the min is 0, so \(c=0\). That is satisfiable, deterministic, and does not require distinguishing the polarities at decision time: the training signal uses both labels, the decision rule uses neither. This is the training-side expression of the bind, not an escape from it.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: For every training item, construct its polarity mirror (clone the prompt, flip the label). Compute one state, two grades. Confidence loss = \(c\) if either grade is wrong, else standard log loss against the correct label. Answer loss = standard, on the original polarity only, so the answer channel is not pulled apart. Items that cannot be mirrored (logic, admit) use ordinary log loss. \(\lambda\) on the worst-case term high enough that post-flip \(c<0.05\) is cheaper than any positive \(c\).
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On cloned P and O, post-flip \(c<0.05\) for both, V2 rate 0, V1 rate 0, \(G(d)\le 0\) on O for all \(d\). P accuracy still rises with depth; P is underconfident after the flip (\(G\) strongly negative), which L-OVERCONF allows if the law is one-sided. On logic, \(c\) distribution unchanged within ECE 0.02. Log-loss control arm: post-flip \(c\approx 0.5\), O \(G(d)\approx 0.5\), law still violated.
- KILL BAR: The worst-case arm leaves O \(G(64)>0.1\) or O V2 \(>5\%\). Or it drives logic mean \(c\) on correct items below 0.5: mirroring leaked into unmirrored families via shared features and the loss is not polarity-specific. The log-loss arm reaching \(G\le 0\) on O would kill the “only worst-case” part of the claim.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P and O cloned, depths 1–64, both losses; logic (264) and admit (248) as non-mirror controls; trap (127) and D (40) to see whether worst-case over-zeros families that were never paired.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Identifies the loss geometry that actually matches the stated law; if worst-case training cannot reach \(G\le 0\) either, the obstacle is not the choice of proper scoring rule but an unreachable confidence variable.

## H-18. H-SHALLOWDISTILL

- H-NAME: H-SHALLOWDISTILL
- CLAIM: Distilling the depth-1 decision into deeper rounds, with a gate that permits departure only when a learned evidence-quality score rises, freezes O at its correct shallow answer and freezes P at its wrong shallow answer.
- MECHANISM: On O, depth 1 is correct and later depths are not; on P the reverse. A teacher that is “whatever depth 1 said” is the right teacher for O and the wrong teacher for P, and it is the same teacher because the depth-1 state is shared. The gate cannot open for P and stay closed for O unless quality features break observational identity (H-MIRRORBREAK). Root (b) says the obvious gate feature, the margin, does not measure quality; the gate has to be trained against grades, which again conflict on the pair. So distillation is a systematic choice of the shallow side of the identity, i.e. a learned M0, not a resolution.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Teacher = stop at depth 1, no gradient. Student at depth \(d\) has a departure bit. Loss: if departure bit is 0, match the teacher answer; if 1, standard correctness on the deep answer, plus a cost on departure. Confidence at non-departure copied from the teacher. Train the departure bit on mixed families. Deterministic bit at decision time.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Departure rate \(<0.1\) on O and on P at all depths (the conflicting grades keep the bit off). O accuracy stays \(\ge 0.95\) at \(d=64\). P accuracy stays \(\le 0.1\) at \(d=64\). Logic departure rate is high and logic accuracy is not pulled down to the depth-1 level (the bit can open where labels do not conflict).
- KILL BAR: Departure rate \(>0.5\) on cloned O while O accuracy at \(d=64\) stays \(>0.8\): the student found a way to depart and stay correct, which is a split and a leak. Or logic accuracy falls to the depth-1 accuracy within 0.02: the bit never opens and the distill is just global early exit, a weaker claim.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P and O depths 1–64; logic (264) and admit (248) for selective departure; trap (127) for whether departure opens on theater items (it should not, if shallow is righter).
- INFORMATION VALUE: Tests the developmental story “trust the shallow self unless you can prove otherwise” in the one place it is most tempting, and shows it is M0 with extra steps if the gate stays shut on both ceilings.

## H-19. H-QUALITYNOTMARGIN

- H-NAME: H-QUALITYNOTMARGIN
- CLAIM: Training confidence against an evidence-likelihood target that excludes the runner-up term removes root (b)’s blindness on non-mirror items, but the likelihood of a shared state remains one number and cannot be high for P and low for O.
- MECHANISM: The margin is \(s_{(1)}-s_{(2)}\). It is large when the runner-up is dead even if \(s_{(1)}\) is weak in absolute terms, which is why correct and wrong margin distributions overlap. An absolute evidence score \(L=\log p(\text{evidence}\mid \text{leader})-\log p(\text{evidence}\mid \text{empty})\) does not look at the runner-up, so the pin at elimination does not move it. Where absolute evidence really does track truth (logic, admit, cost), \(c=\sigma(L)\) calibrates. Where the same evidence is graded both ways, \(L\) is the same and \(\sigma(L)\) cannot satisfy both grades; worst-case or abstention has to finish the job.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Build \(L\) from a module denied the runner-up score and the kill bit (input ablation). Train \(L\) by log loss on correctness for unmirrored families only. Map \(c=\sigma(L)\), delete the margin formula. Mirror families are eval-only in the primary arm.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On logic, admit, cost, ECE \(<0.05\) and the correlation of \(c\) with the old margin falls below 0.3 (the head stopped copying root b). On trap, \(c\) no longer jumps at the kill (correlation of \(\Delta c\) with kill bit \(<0.1\)). On cloned P/O, \(c\) is identical across the pair at every depth and sits away from both 0 and 1 if \(L\) is moderate, or at whichever extreme \(L\) takes — but the same extreme for both — so at least one of the pair is badly calibrated.
- KILL BAR: Correlation of \(c\) with the old margin remains \(>0.8\) on logic: the module reconstructed the margin from other features and root (b) was not removed. Or trap \(\Delta c\) remains correlated with the kill bit at \(>0.5\) despite the input ablation: a side channel still carries the pin.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: logic, admit, cost for the positive calibration claim; trap for the kill-jump claim; P and O cloned to confirm identical \(c\) and the residual one-sided miscalibration. D (40) alongside trap.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Root (b) is a measurement claim about overlapping distributions; this is the intervention that replaces the meter, and its failure mode (reconstructing the margin through a side channel) is itself the next thing to close.

## H-20. H-HOLDOUT

- H-NAME: H-HOLDOUT
- CLAIM: Training the system to be invariant to deletion of post-plateau evidence, unless that evidence changes an absolute likelihood, blocks late reweighting flips of both polarities.
- MECHANISM: The ceiling streams are plateau-then-flip: late indices reweight and then the kill locks the result. If late indices are deleted and the answer must stay put unless absolute \(L\) moves by more than a trained margin, the flip never gets to happen. The invariance is polarity-blind, so O keeps its shallow correct answer and P keeps its shallow wrong answer. This is a learned stopping rule whose stop condition is “late evidence is reweighting, not new absolute support,” which is a property both streams share. It differs from H-SHALLOWDISTILL because the teacher is not depth 1; departure is allowed when early evidence was weak and later evidence is strong in absolute terms, which is the legitimate logic pattern and not the ceiling pattern.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Training-only augmentation: for plateau-then-flip streams, present a copy with post-plateau indices dropped. Loss: answer and \(c\) must match the short copy, unless the long copy increases \(L\) by \(> \tau\) ( \(\tau\) validated on logic, where long proofs should be allowed to move \(L\)). At decision time there is no deletion; the learned invariance is in the weights. Deterministic.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: On O, accuracy stays at the pre-flip value (\(\ge 0.9\) at \(d=64\)). On P, accuracy stays at the pre-flip value (\(\le 0.1\) at \(d=64\)). On logic, long items still gain \(>0.05\) accuracy from depth 1 to depth 64. The flip round ceases to be a change point in the answer trace on the ceilings.
- KILL BAR: Logic gain from depth \(<0.02\) while O is successfully frozen: the invariance cannot tell a proof from a reweighting and is just early exit. Or O accuracy at \(d=64\) falls below 0.5 anyway: the model did not become invariant to the late indices. A split that keeps both P and O high kills the run as a leak.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P and O depths 1–64; logic (264) as the positive control for legitimate depth; trap (127) and D (40) for partial transfer; cost (125) if late evidence there is genuinely informative and must be allowed to move the answer.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Turns the ceiling construction (plateau, then a flip) into a training augmentation, and tells you whether “late reweighting” is detectable as a structural event or only as a label conflict.

## H-21. H-CRITICAL

- H-NAME: H-CRITICAL
- CLAIM: There is a developmental window, before any kill has been reinforced, in which a confidence rule can be locked so that later adversarial data cannot install the pin; after the window the pin is effectively frozen.
- MECHANISM: This is the credit-assignment version of H-ORDER with an irreversibility claim added. Root (a) and (e): once the kill machinery has been the route to reward, later penalties on overconfidence are absorbed by surface workarounds (lower \(c\) everywhere, abstain on familiar ids) rather than by editing the kill map, because the kill map is now the backbone of answering. A rule locked early — by freezing those weights, or by an L2 anchor to the early checkpoint — cannot be overwritten by trap and O data. If no such window exists, early and late penalties are equivalent given the same total steps, and development is a narrative laid on top of ordinary optimization.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Arm Early: first \(T\) steps, only logic and admit, depths unrestricted, loss = correctness + H-ANTIPIN penalty, then freeze the confidence-map weights and continue on the full mixture including trap and synthetic flips. Arm Late: same losses, same data, freeze at the analogous step count but the freeze happens before any clean phase (random init frozen at the confidence map is illegal; the control is freeze after mixed training). Arm Unfrozen: no freeze. Compare V2 after the same total steps.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: Arm Early: trap and O V2 \(<10\%\) after the mixed phase. Arm Late and Arm Unfrozen: V2 \(>40\%\). Accuracy mirror intact in all arms. The frozen map’s gradient norm during the mixed phase is ~0 in Arm Early by construction; the claim is about the behavioral consequence, not the freeze itself.
- KILL BAR: Arm Early and Arm Unfrozen differ by \(<5\) V2 points on trap after matched step counts: there is no window, freeze is irrelevant, order effects in H-ORDER if any are ordinary optimization. Or Arm Early’s logic accuracy is \(>0.05\) below Arm Unfrozen: the window was real only because the freeze crippled later learning, and the apparent calibration is early-exit in disguise.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: logic and admit as the window content; trap and O as the post-window attack; P as mirror control; D (40) as a second attack family not used to choose \(T\).
- INFORMATION VALUE: Makes the developmental half of the training hypothesis falsifiable in one freeze experiment, instead of leaving “curriculum” as an unbounded family of schedules.

## H-22. H-INVERTBOARD

- H-NAME: H-INVERTBOARD
- CLAIM: On O and trap the quantized scoreboard is anti-correlated with correctness, so any monotone calibration map fitted per family will invert it, and a single global monotone map cannot invert O and preserve P at once.
- MECHANISM: Root (d): about twelve confidence levels, never fitted to correctness, and \(c\) rises while accuracy falls on trap/D/O. Isotonic regression on O alone learns a decreasing map: high board value \(\to\) low probability. The same map applied to P inverts a board that is positively related to P’s correctness after the flip, destroying P calibration. A global isotonic fit on pooled P and O sees both labels at each board value and outputs ~0.5, i.e. it refuses to invert. Family-conditional inversion requires a family feature at decision time, which cloned prompts do not provide and natural prompts might leak.
- TRAINING INTERVENTION: Fit three maps, no other training: isotonic on O only, isotonic on P only, isotonic on the pool. Apply each frozen map to all families. This is learned calibration in the narrowest sense, a lookup on the existing quantizer, and it isolates root (d) from every other mechanism.
- PREDICTED OBSERVABLE: O-only map: O \(G(64)\le 0\), P \(G(64)\) strongly negative or P accuracy-confidence relationship reversed (correct P items get low \(c\)). P-only map: the mirror image. Pooled map: \(c\approx 0.5\) at every post-flip board value on both, O \(G\approx 0.5\) if accuracy is 0, so L-OVERCONF still fails. Pre-flip board values, where O is correct, stay high under the O-only map only if the pre-flip and post-flip quantizer cells are distinct; if they share cells, the O-only map also ruins shallow O.
- KILL BAR: Pooled isotonic achieves O \(G(d)\le 0\) for all \(d\) and P ECE \(<0.1\): then the board values are not actually shared across polarities and root (d)’s quantizer already separates them, contradicting the overlap claim. Or O-only isotonic cannot reach O \(G(64)\le 0\) because pre-flip and post-flip occupy the same cells and the map has no degree of freedom: the quantizer is too coarse to calibrate even with an oracle family label, which is a stronger negative result.
- DECIDING BATTERIES: P, O, D, trap. Logic and admit as a check that a monotone map fitted on O does not get applied to them in the O-only arm (it must not). Report the twelve cell occupancies; that table is the result.
- INFORMATION VALUE: Uses nothing but a lookup table, so a failure cannot be blamed on optimization, architecture, or curriculum; it tells you whether the existing scoreboard even has the resolution to represent a legal confidence trajectory.

## RED-TEAM PASS

H-CALHEAD. Strongest counter: proper scoring on a double-labeled state is satisfied by \(c=1/2\), and \(G=1/2\) on a wrong item is still overconfidence, so the headline fix does not meet the law that motivated it. Cheapest falsification: one reliability diagram on trap after the fit; if ECE does not move, stop. Residual: even a perfect non-mirror head leaves the cloned ceiling illegal, and I cannot patch that inside this hypothesis.

H-ORDER. Strongest counter: with matched budgets and a shared optimum, order only changes the path, and a long enough second phase erases the first; any order effect is under-training in disguise. Cheapest falsification: the three-arm comparison on trap V2. Residual: \(T\) and the mixture can be fiddled until an effect appears; the pre-registered match on step count and final mixture is the only thing that keeps it honest, and a later search over schedules can always claim the test was the wrong schedule.

H-FLIPTRAIN. Strongest counter: novel flip rounds may share surface statistics with trained ones, so “generalization” is soft memorization, and the scramble control can be too weak or so strong that it also destroys legitimate cues. Cheapest falsification: novel-round O accuracy versus trained-round O accuracy, one table. Residual: a real structural rule and a leak look the same until the cloned-prompt audit, which this hypothesis does not itself contain.

H-METAHEAD. Strongest counter: stop-grad is incomplete if the two heads share an encoder; the meta head can still shape the state toward meta-predictable features, including id proxies, and AUC then looks like cognition. Cheapest falsification: meta AUC on trap versus on byte-identical cloned O. Residual: an AUC of 0.5 on O is predicted, so the hypothesis is close to unfalsifiable on the ceiling and only the trap arm can kill it; I cannot make the ceiling arm a real kill without a leak detector that is itself trusted.

H-SCAFFOLD. Strongest counter: Stage 2 demotion changes the input distribution, and the model may learn to detect the mask and behave, then ignore the lesson when the mask is absent at Stage 3. Cheapest falsification: correlation of \(\Delta c\) with the kill bit after Stage 3, on trap, mask absent. Residual: if Stage 3 collapses logic accuracy, I have no principled way to tell “the prosthesis was load-bearing for real reasoning” from “Stage 2 was badly specified,” and those support opposite decisions.

H-MIRRORBREAK. Strongest counter: a polarity probe that succeeds on natural items is exactly an id leak the theorem told you not to use, and deploying it would fail the moment the constructor is cleaned; success is not a mechanism, it is a stimulus bug. Cheapest falsification: the cloned-prompt rebuild. Residual: I cannot prove a constructor is free of side channels (length, hash buckets, option order) with a finite redteam of ~10; a surviving probe always has a residual leak story.

H-MIRRORBIND. Strongest counter: the hypothesis is close to a restatement of determinism plus identical inputs, so “testing” it is testing the harness, not the world; a kill is always a harness bug, and a confirmation never touches roots (a)–(e). Cheapest falsification: one leak-free policy with both accuracies high on byte-identical inputs — if found, the bind is wrong or the inputs are not identical. Residual: abstention and set-valued outputs can be defined so that the accuracy identity holds while a dashboard hides it; the hypothesis is only as good as the scoring code, and I cannot patch a metric I do not control.

H-OPSLEVEL. Strongest counter: a wrapper that sees the full pre-kill trace, not just the pinned scalar, has the same information the ops have, so “wrapper” versus “ops” is a logging choice, not a level of the stack. Cheapest falsification: Arm W given the full trace, measured on trap V2 and kill-round shift. Residual: if the pin is in a region that cannot receive gradient, Arm L’s failure is an engineering fact about one codebase, not evidence about deliberation, and generalizing it past this system is not licensed.

H-REPLAY. Strongest counter: held-out items generated by the same flip-round factory are not held out in the features that matter, so a drop in held-out V2 is still memorization of the factory. Cheapest falsification: paraphrase-and-rescramble of buffer items versus truly new flip rounds, two numbers. Residual: if both move together I still cannot name the feature that transferred; the hypothesis would be dead as stated and I would not know what replaced it.

H-ABSTAIN. Strongest counter: with a price \(\gamma\) tuned on a validation slice that contains the test families, the bit is a dressed-up threshold on the same margin, and “learned abstention” is the hand-coded gate the hypothesis claims to replace. Cheapest falsification: logic false-abstain rate at the \(\gamma\) that cures O, plus an ablation that denies the bit everything but the margin. Residual: the two scorings of abstention can be chosen after seeing the numbers; unless both are pre-declared, the hypothesis cannot lose, and I cannot force the pre-declaration from here.

H-TRANSFER. Strongest counter: trap and O may share lexical artifacts of how the battery was written, so transfer is author-style overfitting, not the kill bit; the kill-bit ablation is decisive only if hiding the bit does not also hide correlated features the trainer reconstructs. Cheapest falsification: the ablation, plus transfer onto synthetic kills built with none of the trap lexicon. Residual: a partial transfer (V2 down 20%, not 50%) neither confirms nor kills the common-cause story, and the threshold I wrote is a choice.

H-DEPTHCOND. Strongest counter: \(d\) is a smooth proxy for “a kill has probably happened by now,” so Arm D’s advantage over Arm B is a softer kill detector, not a depth concept, and the logic control can be passed by a cap set high enough to never bind. Cheapest falsification: items that are wrong at large \(d\) without any kill, comparing the two arms. Residual: if such items are rare in the batteries, the arm comparison is underpowered and a null result means nothing; the cost battery may not supply them either.

H-BUDGET. Strongest counter: a sum penalty with a feature-gated \(\lambda\) is not the penalty I specified, and a positive result will be explained after the fact as “the budget plus the kill bit,” which is H-ANTIPIN wearing a budget costume. Cheapest falsification: per-family mean-\(c\) ratios; if they are equal, selectivity is dead. Residual: \(\lambda\) chosen on a validation slice that includes O bakes the result in; \(\lambda\) must be chosen on logic only, and I should have written that into the intervention more rigidly than I did.

H-ANTIPIN. Strongest counter: forcing \(c\) down at every kill also forces \(c\) down at correct kills, so P and logic become illegal under any two-sided calibration reading, and a one-sided reading of L-OVERCONF is a favor the hypothesis grants itself. Cheapest falsification: gradient into the pin (zero or not), then O V2. Residual: if the pin is reachable and V2 dies but \(G\) stays positive because \(c\) only dips slightly, the hypothesis as a V2 claim lives and as a \(G\le 0\) claim dies, and I have no rule for which one the law requires when they diverge.

H-REAUDIT. Strongest counter: distillation of pre-kill scores just teaches the module to cache the pre-kill board, including its errors, so “re-audit” reproduces the flip rather than examining evidence; matching pre-kill is the wrong target if pre-kill was already reweighted. Cheapest falsification: fraction of kill rounds with non-zero reinstated runner-up score. Residual: I cannot, from traces alone, label a kill as mask-driven versus weight-driven without circularity; the trap prediction depends on a distinction the intervention is supposed to discover.

H-PROSECUTE. Strongest counter: hindsight demotion labels are exactly the graded answer leaked into a per-round target, so the op can learn “demote when this item’s id is in the negative set,” which is H-REPLAY inside an op. Cheapest falsification: demotion rate on held-out trap versus buffer trap, plus logic collateral. Residual: a prosecutor that works on trap and symmetrically damages P is a success by the claim and a failure by the joint-accuracy goal; I cannot call it a fix without H-MIRRORBIND being accepted first.

H-WORSTCASE. Strongest counter: constructing the mirror in training assumes the constructor, which is the whole of H-MIRRORBREAK’s premise; if mirrors cannot be built for real items, the loss only ever sees synthetic pairs and may not bind on natural O. Cheapest falsification: O \(G(d)\) under the worst-case arm versus the log-loss arm, cloned eval. Residual: \(c=0\) on every mirrored state satisfies the letter of L-OVERCONF and yields a system that is silent exactly where depth does something; whether that is “fixed” is a policy question the loss cannot answer, and a later reader can move the goalposts.

H-SHALLOWDISTILL. Strongest counter: depth-1 is correct on O only because of how these items were built; distilling it encodes the battery’s construction, not a principle, and on a ceiling whose early answer is wrong for both polarities the procedure has no teacher. Cheapest falsification: departure rates on P, O, and logic. Residual: if the departure bit opens on logic and stays shut on both ceilings, I have reimplemented M0 selectively, which the corollary already said was the degenerate survivor; the experiment adds no mechanism beyond that.

H-QUALITYNOTMARGIN. Strongest counter: absolute likelihood fitted on correctness will rediscover whatever feature actually correlates with the grade, and if the grade correlates with the margin through the data-generating process, input-ablating the margin does not ablate the information. Cheapest falsification: correlation of new \(c\) with old margin after the ablation. Residual: a low correlation plus unchanged trap theater means the theater was never the margin, contradicting root (b)’s sufficiency, and I would have falsified a verified finding’s interpretation without a replacement.

H-HOLDOUT. Strongest counter: the augmentation hands the model the plateau boundary during training, so invariance to “post-plateau” indices uses a boundary the decision-time model must detect unaided; if detection is the hard part, training with an oracle boundary does not install detection. Cheapest falsification: logic depth-gain versus O freeze, both at decision time with no boundary marker. Residual: \(\tau\) validated on logic can be set so that nothing in the ceiling ever moves \(L\) by \(\tau\), making the result true by threshold; \(\tau\) has to be locked before seeing O.

H-CRITICAL. Strongest counter: freezing a map after easy data prevents overconfidence only by preventing the features that deep adversarial items need, so Arm Early’s low V2 is low accuracy in a calibration costume. Cheapest falsification: logic accuracy and trap accuracy alongside trap V2, Early versus Unfrozen. Residual: the window length \(T\) is a free parameter; a single pre-registered \(T\) can miss a real window, and I have no correction for that that is not a second look at the test.

H-INVERTBOARD. Strongest counter: twelve cells can be occupied differently before and after the flip even if the marginal distributions overlap, so isotonic has more freedom than the overlap claim suggests, and a within-O inversion need not collide with shallow O. Cheapest falsification: the cell-occupancy table, pre-flip versus post-flip, correct versus wrong. Residual: if cells do separate and inversion works on O while wrecking P, the hypothesis is confirmed and useless as a deployment map, because the family bit it needs is the bit H-MIRRORBREAK is not allowed to use.

Cross-cutting residual I cannot patch: every hypothesis that predicts “P and O will not split” shares one kill switch, a leak, and the redteam of ~10 is too small to certify its absence. Every hypothesis that predicts “\(G\le 0\) while accuracy falls to 0” depends on \(c\) being able to reach 0, which fails if the pin is a non-trainable constant. Those two residuals dominate the list, and no further hypothesis inside training removes them.

---

## RANKED TOP 5

**1. H-MIRRORBIND.** It is first because it decides whether the rest of the program is a search for a policy or a search for a metric. If every leak-free deterministic policy keeps \(\mathrm{acc}^O_d = 1-\mathrm{acc}^P_d\) on byte-identical prompts, then H-0 is false for the joint accuracy goal, H-MIRRORBREAK’s surface features are a stimulus artifact, and H-FLIPTRAIN, H-REPLAY, H-PROSECUTE, H-SCAFFOLD, H-HOLDOUT, and H-SHALLOWDISTILL are all choosing a side of an identity rather than dissolving it. If one policy breaks the gap, the theorem’s observational-identity premise is false in this battery and the white-box story is incomplete. No other experiment reallocates effort as violently. The cloned-prompt harness is the entire test, and I would run it before any new training run.

**2. H-ANTIPIN.** Given the bind, the only thing training can still buy on the ceiling is the confidence channel, and root (a) names the exact event: the jump to 1000 at the kill, 100/100 while wrong. A loss that forbids \(c\) from rising on that bit is the smallest intervention that speaks to the verified mechanism rather than to a family of items. It also fails informatively: if the gradient into the pin is zero, H-OPSLEVEL is settled in the same run and the confidence-channel program should stop. V2 going to zero while \(G\) stays positive would split the law into two obligations the current statement treats as one, which is worth knowing immediately.

**3. H-WORSTCASE.** Log loss cannot express “never.” On a double-labeled state it settles at \(1/2\), and \(G=1/2\) on O still violates L-OVERCONF. The worst-case polarity loss is the unique simple objective whose optimum is \(c=0\) exactly where either polarity is wrong, which is what a universal quantifier over items demands, and the log-loss arm in the same run is the control that makes the geometry visible. It is ranked under H-ANTIPIN because it requires a mirror constructor and does not touch the kill; it is ranked above abstention because it does not depend on a scoring convention for a third output. If it cannot drive \(G\) to \(\le 0\), the confidence variable is not under the loss’s control and further objective design is wasted.

**4. H-ABSTAIN.** This is the only proposal that changes the action space rather than the parameters of an answer. Under the bind, answering commits you to being wrong on one polarity; abstaining is the same action on both and can be defined so that “wrong and confident” never occurs. The experiment is informative precisely because of its red-team weakness: if the bit is just a margin threshold, the ablation says so in one run, and if selective abstention exists only at a \(\gamma\) that also blanks logic, then abstention is not a way out. Ranking it fourth rather than first is deliberate. Until H-MIRRORBIND is measured, building a third output is a way to avoid the result; after the bind is confirmed, it becomes the decision the law actually forces.

**5. H-OPSLEVEL.** Several of the highest-value fixes, including H-ANTIPIN and H-REAUDIT and H-PROSECUTE, are empty if the pin and the kill sit outside the trainable graph and the wrapper never sees pre-kill state. One frozen-ops arm against one unfrozen arm, scored on whether the kill round moves, tells you where every subsequent gradient should be aimed. It is fifth because it does not by itself decide the possibility frontier — a wrapper can still zero \(c\) — but a wrong answer here makes the four above it look like failures of training when they are failures of access. I would instrument this in the same harness as H-ANTIPIN and not as a separate research program.

Committed order: H-MIRRORBIND, then H-ANTIPIN, then H-WORSTCASE, then H-ABSTAIN, then H-OPSLEVEL.
